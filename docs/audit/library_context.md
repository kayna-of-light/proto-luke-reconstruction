# Library context: what the literary-compilation corpus holds on the matters the reconstruction touches

Read in full by the auditing agent, 2026-10-12. Organised by topic, not by document. Every entry names the document (path under `literary-compilation/data/`) and section. Entries marked **TEST** are factual claims about the text of Marcion's gospel, the manuscripts or the Fathers; they are checked against the primary witnesses in the verse apparatus (`docs/audit/apparatus/`), and the result is recorded there and in the summary table at the end of this file.

## 1. The community: Jamesian, Ebionite, the Poor

- The Jerusalem community under James called itself *Ebionim*, "the Poor", a theological title from Psalms and Prophets. Theology: Torah as "Law of Liberty" (Jas 1:25; 2:8, 12), faith shown through works (Jas 2:20–26), economic reversal (Jas 5:1–6), and a Christology of exaltation: Jesus as "the Living One" (ὁ ζῶν), exalted to the right hand of God. *Carriers of Living Water* (00_Master_Theses) §1, §3.4.
- Parallels Epistle of James / Luke: woes to the rich (Jas 5:1 / Lk 6:24); judging (Jas 5:9 / Lk 6:37); hearing and doing (Jas 1:22 / Lk 6:46); asking (Jas 1:5 / Lk 11:9); two masters (Jas 4:4 / Lk 16:13). *Carriers* §3.4 table.
- Gospel of the Hebrews: first appearance to James, breaking of bread; "Eucharistic appearance grounded in table fellowship". *Carriers* §3.4.
- Hegesippus: James Nazirite, Rechabite ally, *Oblias*; succession by Symeon son of Clopas on kinship. *Carriers* §3.2–3.3, §5.1.
- Elcesaites (Jewish-Christian baptists, Babylonia) → Mani; Book of Giants at Qumran and Turfan. *Carriers* §8.1–8.2.
- Quranic Isa read as preserving the Jamesian profile: fully human, "they both used to eat food" (Q 5:75) as proof of real humanity. *Carriers* §10.2. (Relevant to how the meal at Lk 24:41–43 is read: within the library's own Jamesian frame, eating marks real humanity, not an orthodox polemic.)

## 2. Proto-Luke as the library states it (claims to test)

- **TEST** "Editorial fatigue analysis … consistently shows canonical Luke exhibiting fatigue when diverging from the Marcionite text." *Carriers* §2.2. (See WORKING_DOCUMENT §1.4: untraced; Bilby's stylometry is the existing quantitative work; Lk 4:23 is the one fatigue-type case.)
- **TEST** Proto-Luke dated pre-70: "canonical Luke 21:20 reads 'When you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies' … Mark 13:14 and the Marcionite witness use the vague Danielic phrase 'abomination of desolation'." *Carriers* §6.1.
- **TEST** Luke 16:17 "easier for heaven and earth to pass away than for one stroke of a letter of the Law to fail" cited as a Proto-Lukan Torah saying. *Carriers* §3.4.
- **TEST** Martha–Mary (Lk 10:38–42) "absent from the Marcionite *Evangelion* as attested by Tertullian and Epiphanius." *Carriers* §4.2.
- **TEST** Luke 24: Proto-Luke "lacked 24:6a", "likely lacked" 24:39 and 24:41–43; "faith was generated solely by the revelation of the Word (opening the Scriptures) and the Eucharist (breaking of bread)". "The passages identified as interpolations on manuscript grounds alone are also absent from the Marcionite tradition." Table: 24:39–40 = WNI; 24:41–43 = "anti-Docetic expansion". *Carriers* §6.2, §6.5, §6.6, App. C.
- **TEST** 22:19b–20 absent from D and Old Latin (manuscript fact) and treated as Pauline insertion. *Carriers* §6.2.
- **TEST** 24:51b–52 absent from D and ℵ*. *Carriers* §6.2 table.
- Correspondential diagnostic: parables yield full correspondential depth; birth narratives and resurrection proofs yield "doctrinal citation" only; claimed to map onto the source strata. *Carriers* §2.4, §12.4; strain #21 in docs/EVOLVING_CONCEPTUAL_STRAINS.md.
- Limitations stated by the thesis itself: the reconstruction "remains a reconstruction"; intentionality at each point admits more and less charitable readings. *Carriers* §12.6.

## 3. Resurrection

- Strain #9 (docs/EVOLVING_CONCEPTUAL_STRAINS.md): progressive materialisation; Luke "flesh and bones, eating (later additions)"; "Western Non-Interpolations are later additions"; original experience "the Living One (ὁ ζῶν)".
- *Carriers* §6.6: Paul's σῶμα πνευματικόν; no empty tomb in Paul; Talpiot as family tomb; Proto-Lukan tomb "sign of absence".
- Rev 1:18 ὁ ζῶν; 1 Enoch 5:1 "the Living One" as designation for God. *Carriers* §7.6.

## 4. Nag Hammadi and "Gnosticism"

- Strain #22: "Gnostic impulse" retired; two phenomena distinguished: *self-sourcing* and *literalization* (correspondential texts read without their key). Marcion is listed among the bodies of literature wrongly lumped together.
- *Carriers* §9: NHL as a correspondential archive buried c. 367; six texts announce an interior sense; Thomas as an independent Jamesian witness (L. 12 James the Just); Silvanus as non-Gnostic use of correspondence.

## 5. *No Original Exists* (03_Biblical_Scholarship): the method claims and the test items

Method as stated: three vectors, (1) Streeter coherence (non-Markan material forms a continuous gospel), (2) the Marcionite control text, (3) a "Jamesian theological filter", plus (4) a correspondential depth test (§4, §6.4). The paper itself limits the filter: "used in isolation, it would be insufficiently precise" and "if the profile is wrong, passages attributed to the earliest stratum may belong to a later one" (§8.5).

Observations for the audit (logic, not opinion):
- Vector (1) and vector (2) describe *different* texts. Streeter's Proto-Luke is Luke minus its Markan blocks; Marcion's text contains the Markan blocks (e.g. the Gerasene demoniac, the feeding, the passion predictions). They cannot both define the same text. The reconstruction is built on (2); (1) cannot be cited as converging evidence for it.
- The "Great Omission" (Mark 6:45–8:26) is absent from Marcion's text *and* from canonical Luke alike, so it cannot discriminate between them (§5.4).
- The theological filter is applied as a method in §4.3 ("passages reflecting high Christology … are flagged as later redaction"). Applied before the witnesses are consulted it decides the result in advance. In this audit it is used only after the witnesses, to describe the profile of what they attest.

TEST items (factual, checked in the apparatus):
- Luke 1–2: "Epiphanius confirms the absence explicitly" (§5.2).
- Lord's Prayer 11:2: Marcion's text with minuscules 162 and 700 reads "May your Holy Spirit come upon us and cleanse us" for "Your kingdom come" (§4.3, App. B).
- 21:20: Marcion's text "likely preserved the Danielic phrase" (§5.5; App. A "Marcionite witness").
- 22:43–44 "absent from the Marcionite text" (§3.3).
- 23:2 Marcionite form of the accusation (App. B).
- 24:39–43: "absent or altered in the earliest known form"; "BeDuhn, Vinzent identify the entire 'flesh and bones' apology as a later orthodox insertion" (§5.6).
- WNI list as given in §5.3 (22:19b–20; 24:12, 36b, 40, 51b, 52a) and the claim "each is also absent from the Marcionite tradition".
- "The Marcionite evidence … consistently supports the shorter readings" (§7.3).
- 5:5 ἐπιστάτα in Marcion's text (App. B).

## 6. The genesis documents of the reconstruction

- *The Jamesian Protograph: A Forensic Reconstruction of Proto-Luke* (04_Early_Christian_History) is the Gemini-generated feasibility study from which the NotebookLM reconstruction descends. Its footnote 1 (the source behind most claims) is the deleted Drive guide; the other sources are Wikipedia, Reddit, blogs, Bible Hub, a Marcionite-church website and dokumen.pub. Its reconstruction principles: excise Luke 1–2; join 3:1 to 4:31; relocate Nazareth; keep the woes; Lord's Prayer with the Spirit petition and "do not let us be led into temptation"; **21:20 replaced by the Markan "abomination of desolation" ("composite based on Marcionite witness and Mark 13:14")**; 22:19b–20, 22:43–44 excised; **23:2 Marcionite accusation "destroying the law and the prophets"**; 24:12, 24:39b–43, 24:51b excised; 4:22 "Is this not Joseph's son?" removed "to align with the anti-biological Christology".
  - Its theological filter includes "Passages that reflect … the specific historical details of the 70 CE siege … are to be flagged as later redactions" (§2.3). That is a decision rule applied before the witnesses, and it is the origin of the 21:20 composite.
- *The Jamesian Protograph … Proto-Lukan Nexus …* (04_Early_Christian_History): the companion Gemini report; identifies Proto-Luke with "Q + L" *and* with Marcion's text (a contradiction: Marcion's text contains Markan material); asserts a "computer-aided analysis of editorial fatigue" (footnote to itself / to blogs).
- *The Forensic Stratigraphy of the Resurrection* (04_Early_Christian_History): works on the premise "where the Western text and Marcion agree against the canonical/Alexandrian text, we are looking at the original Proto-Luke". **TEST**: at 24:6 the two do not agree (Western omits the line; Epiphanius and Tertullian attest ἠγέρθη for Marcion), so the premise yields no verdict there and the document's omission of 24:6a has no Marcionite support. Its claim that Tertullian reports a Marcionite reading "as you see I have [not]" at 24:39 is checked against *Marc.* 4.43.7.

## 7. Marcion's own theology (for judging possible Marcionite-community readings)

- *Dancing with Fire* (05_The_Self): two gods; prohibition of allegory (Origen, *Comm. Matt.* 15.3); the Harrowing of Hell (Irenaeus 1.27.3); master text Lk 6:43 (two trees). Notes that the **Good Samaritan, the Prodigal Son, the Temptation, the Triumphal Entry and the Wicked Tenants are unattested** in Marcion's text (§5.2) and that "whether the Prodigal Son was removed by Marcion or was never in his text is not determinable" (§5.2, §8.6).
- Consequence for the audit: the library's strain #21 (docs/EVOLVING_CONCEPTUAL_STRAINS.md) and *Carriers* §2.4 treat the Good Samaritan and the Prodigal Son as the parade examples of Proto-Lukan correspondential composition. On the witnesses both are unattested for Marcion's text. The reconstruction (JSON ch. 8 = Lk 10:1–28 selection, ch. 13 = Lk 15:1–10) already leaves both out, consistent with the witnesses. The library's claim is therefore a point for Phase F, not a reason to add them back.

## 8. The Nag Hammadi collection

- *The Dynastic Archive* (04_Early_Christian_History): the NHL as the archive of a community that traced its authority to Jesus' family; 68% of named-authority texts invoke Jesus' circle (Jesus, James, Mary, Thomas) against 8% Paul; Pachomian production (cartonnage of Codex VII: 341, 346, 348 CE; Pbow, Tabennesi); the Sethian texts as the Egyptian cosmological form of the fall narrative; Philip's Temple/Bridal Chamber as correspondential literacy; Epiphanius places Nazoraeans and Ebionites in Egypt.
- Relevance to the reconstruction: the Gospel of Thomas transmits sayings that parallel Luke independently of the canonical text (e.g. Th 72 ≈ Lk 12:13–14; Th 63 ≈ Lk 12:16–21; Th 64 ≈ Lk 14:16–24; Th 65 ≈ Lk 20:9–16; Th 47 ≈ Lk 16:13; Th 113 and 3 ≈ Lk 17:20–21). Where Thomas has a parallel, it is a separate witness to the existence and wording of a saying and is recorded in the apparatus.
## 9. Other library documents read for data claims (claims grepped and checked in context)

- *What the Apparatus Cannot See* (03_Biblical_Scholarship) is the library's most disciplined text-critical document. It uses Roth's graded reconstruction and states: Tertullian *Marc.* IV attests roughly 90% of the 486 verses known for the gospel and is the sole witness for about two-thirds; Epiphanius *Pan.* 42 supplies the numbered scholia; Adamantius is third and least trusted (§ on sources, line ~134). It records 22:43–44 as present in ℵ* (removed by a corrector), absent from P69, P75, B, sy^s, present in D, L and the Byzantine text, and cited by Justin; and 23:34a as present in ℵ*, A, C, L, Byz and absent from P75, B, D*, W. It explicitly declines to use the expansion hypothesis "to establish any primary text-critical conclusion".
- *The Mistress Who Became a Sister* (04): **TEST** Tertullian passes from Lk 10:25–28 directly to 11:1–4; Epiphanius likewise; therefore 10:38–42 absent (library) — in the audit's classes this is "unattested; probably absent / undetermined" depending on whether the passage would have served the Fathers.
- *A Prophet Mighty in Deed and Word* (00_Framework): title from Lk 24:19 (unattested for Marcion's text; see WORKING_DOCUMENT §5); cites 16:17 in the form "my words shall not pass away" (line ~218) — **TEST** against Tertullian 4.33.9 and Epiphanius.
- *The Lukan Dissonance*, *They Killed His Brother*, *The Architectural Suppression*, *The Evidence They Will Not Touch*, *The Anatomy of Ascension*: repeat the claims in §2 above (24:39–43 as anti-Docetic addition; 22:19b–20; WNIs). *The Anatomy of Ascension* builds on 24:32 ("the burning of their hearts") — unattested for Marcion's text.
- *The Bulwark of the People*, *Carriers* §3: James, the *Ebionim*, Hegesippus, Nazirite and Rechabite profile.

## 10. How this context is used in the audit

The library supplies (a) the hypotheses the reconstruction is meant to test and (b) the interpretive frame for the notes. It does not supply readings. A reading enters the reconstructed text only if a witness attests it, or if the witnesses' continuous silence makes its absence probable (method rules, `docs/audit/METHOD.md`). Where a library claim about the text is contradicted by the witnesses, the apparatus says so plainly and the library is listed for correction in Phase F.
