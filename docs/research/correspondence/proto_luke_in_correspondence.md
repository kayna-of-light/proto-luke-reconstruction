# Proto-Luke Written Out in Correspondence

*Research reading, 2026-10-13. This is the method of the user's question (research question Q8, follow-up), applied by one reader. It changes nothing in the reconstruction.*

## Method

Correspondence is used as a method, not as a commentary. Every element of the text — person, place, object, number, action — is replaced by its spiritual function, using one fixed key throughout. The story is then read as it stands in that translation. The question at each step is whether the translated story still runs on without a break. Where a literal element, once translated, breaks the coherence, the method has marked it.

The text is the reconstruction as printed (`data/proto_luke.json`), in its order, grey verses included. Every passage the reconstruction does not print — attested absent (A−), probably absent (U−), or undetermined and left out (U?) — is inserted at its own place as a **test**. The same translation is applied, and the verdict is recorded: does the passage continue the story, or break it?

Verdicts:
- **continues**: the translated passage carries the story on from what precedes to what follows;
- **break**: the translated story stops cohering at this point; the kind of break is named;
- **neutral**: the passage neither carries nor breaks the line.

**The story the translation produces.** Every person and place is read as a state or faculty of one human mind. "Jesus" is the Lord present and acting in that mind (in the supreme sense, the Lord's own glorification; the two run in parallel and are not separated here). The text is then the story of a mind into which the Divine descends, and which is regenerated.

## Before 4:31: what the witnesses do not have (tests)

**Test: Luke 1–2 (A−, Tertullian and Epiphanius).** In translation: the Divine announced to affection (Mary), conceived in the natural without a human father (the Divine as the soul of the Human), born in the least place (Bethlehem; the manger: instruction), announced to simple goods (shepherds), presented in worship (the temple), and at twelve years (all truths) found among the teachers. **Verdict: continues** as a story of the Divine taking on a human natural. In translation it stands alone, however: it ends at twelve years, and the next scene (the date of 3:1) begins again from outside. It is a story of its own, joined to what follows by its subject, not by a line of narrative.

**Test: 3:2–20 (A−).** John in the wilderness: the literal sense of the Word, in a mind not yet cultivated, calls for turning back ("repentance"). Every valley filled: the lowly lifted; "the axe at the root of the trees": the evil life cut off. **Verdict: continues.** It prepares the descent.

**Test: 3:21–22 (A−).** Baptism; the spirit descending as a dove; "you are my son": purification, influx, and acknowledgement of the Human by the Divine. **Verdict: continues.**

**Test: 3:23–38 (A−).** Genealogy to Adam: the Human from the whole human inheritance. **Verdict: neutral.** A list, carrying no step of the story.

**Test: 4:1–13 (A−).** Forty days, three temptations: bread from stones (good made from one's own power), the kingdoms (the love of dominion), the pinnacle (presumption on the Divine). **Verdict: continues.** Combat before the work begins; in the supreme sense the first glorification.

**Note.** Without these passages the printed text opens with the date and then "he came down". In translation: the Divine enters the natural mind directly. That too is a complete opening. The opening coheres both ways; the method does not decide between them.

## Chapter 1 (3:1; 4:31–44)

**3:1.** The rulers: the natural mind governed from outside — empire (Tiberius), the power over the church (Pilate over Judea), and the rulers of the natural (Herod over Galilee, Philip, Lysanias). It is the mind's state before the descent: ordered, ruled, and empty of the Divine.

**4:31–32.** "He came down to Capernaum, a city of Galilee": the Divine enters the natural mind. He teaches "on the sabbaths": he joins good to truth. "His word was with authority": the truth he brings carries its own life, not borrowed authority. *Literal note: "he" has no antecedent in the text. In translation this is no break: the one who descends is not named, because he is not yet received.*

**4:33–35.** In the assembly — in the received doctrine itself — a man with an unclean daimon: a falsity from evil lodged inside the mind's religion. It cries out first: falsity recognises the Divine before the mind does ("I know who you are"). He rebukes it; it throws the man down and leaves without harming him. The first act is removal. **Continues.**

**4:36–37 (grey).** The assembly's astonishment; the report spreads through the region. **Continues.**

**Test: 4:38–39 (U?).** Simon's mother-in-law in a fever, healed, and serving: the affection belonging to faith, inflamed by lust, cooled and turned to use. **Continues.**

**4:16 (Marcionite order: after Capernaum).** He comes to Nazara, "where he had been brought up": what is the mind's own, its upbringing. He stands up to read: the Word is about to be opened in what is one's own.

**Test: 4:17–21 (U−).** He reads Isaiah 61: the spirit upon him; good news to the poor; release to captives; sight to the blind; freedom to the oppressed; "today this is fulfilled". In translation this is the table of contents of the whole road: every later healing and every reversal is one of these items. **Verdict: continues, and is needed.** Without it, "he stood up to read" is followed by nothing read. **Literal break in the printed text (4:16 → 4:23).**

**Test: 4:22 (U−).** "Is not this Joseph's son?": the own sees the Divine only as its natural son. **Continues**: it motivates what follows.

**4:23–24 (24 grey).** "Physician, heal yourself; do here what was done in Capernaum": what is one's own demands the works for itself; "no prophet is accepted in his home town": the proprium cannot receive the Divine as Divine.

**Test: 4:25–26 (U−).** Elijah sent to the widow of Zarephath, not to Israel's widows: the Divine reaches good that longs for truth outside the church. **Continues.** It also prepares the Gentile line (7:1–10; 17:11–19).

**4:28–30.** Rage; they drive him out to the brow of the hill to throw him down; he passes through their midst. The own tries to cast the Divine down from its height; the Divine is not touched and goes on. **Continues.**

**4:40–44.** At sunset (the end of a state) all the sick are brought and healed; daimons cry "son of God" and are silenced. At day he goes to a deserted place; the crowds hold him; "I must proclaim the kingdom to the other towns". The Divine does not stay where it is first received; it moves through the whole mind. *4:44 (grey): "proclaiming in the assemblies of Judea", during the Galilean stage. Minor literal strain; NA27's "Judea" against "Galilee" in many manuscripts. In translation the proclamation reaches the doctrine of the church while working in the natural. Neutral.*

## Chapter 2 (5:1–6:11)

**5:1–11.** By the lake (the natural memory), two boats (doctrines) empty, the fishermen washing their nets. He sits in Simon's boat and teaches from it: the Divine teaches from within doctrine. "Put out into the deep": into the depths of the memory. A night of labour with no catch: faith working by itself in the dark. At his word the nets fill until they break, and both boats nearly sink: natural truths in abundance once the Lord speaks. Simon falls down, "I am a sinful man": faith sees its own evil when it sees the Divine. "From now on you will catch people alive." They leave everything. **Continues.** Faith is called and turned from gathering knowledge to gathering life.

**5:12–16.** A man full of leprosy (profanation) asks; he touches him: "be made clean". "Go, show yourself to the priest, make the offering Moses commanded": the cleansed state must be brought back into the order of the Word. **Continues.** The Word's law is kept, not broken.

**5:17–26.** Teachers from Galilee, Judea and Jerusalem (every level of doctrine) watch. A paralysed man (unable to act from good) is let down through the roof by others' faith. "Your sins are forgiven" comes before "get up and walk": evil removed first, power to act second. **Continues.** The order of regeneration again.

**5:27–32.** Levi at the tax booth (the natural that knows itself as sinner) follows; a great banquet (conjunction); the righteous grumble; "the sick need the physician". **Continues.**

**5:33–38.** Fasting and the bridegroom: the marriage of good and truth is present. The new patch on the old garment, new wine in old skins: new truths cannot be put into the old proprium; they need new vessels. **Continues.** It states the principle of the whole road (cf. 22:20, the new covenant in the cup).

**Test: 5:39 (U−).** "No one who drinks the old desires the new; the old is good." The mind settled in old truths does not want the new. **Continues.** It completes the saying.

**6:1–5.** Plucking grain on the sabbath: appropriating good in the state of conjunction. David and the bread of the presence: holy good given to the hungry. "The son of man is lord of the sabbath." **Continues.**

**6:6–11 (8, 10, 11 grey).** The withered right hand (no power from good) restored on the sabbath; fury. "To save life or to destroy it." **Continues.**

## Chapter 3 (6:12–49)

**6:12–16.** The night on the mountain in prayer: love, in the dark state, joined to the Divine. At day he chooses twelve: all the principles of truth and good ordered around the Divine. Judas among them, "who became a traitor": the love of self is present in the mind's own order from the start. **Continues.**

**6:17–19.** He comes down to a level place: from love to the natural plane where the work is done. Crowds from Judea, Jerusalem, Tyre and Sidon: all levels, within and outside the church. Power goes out of him and heals. **Continues.**

**6:20–26.** Blessed are the poor (those who know they lack truth), the hungry (who desire good), those who weep; woe to the rich (full of their own), the full, those who laugh, those all speak well of. The two states of the mind, receiving and claiming, set side by side. **Continues.** This is the governing contrast of the road.

**6:27–38.** Love your enemies, give, lend expecting nothing, do not judge: life from the Divine love, which gives without return. "Good measure, pressed down, running over": influx received in the degree it is passed on. **Continues.**

**6:39–49.** The blind leading the blind; the speck and the beam (seeing evil in others, not in oneself); good tree, good fruit; out of the treasure of the heart; "why call me Lord and not do"; the house on the rock and the house on the ground. Hearing becomes doing; temptation (the flood) shows which foundation holds. **Continues.** It closes the instruction with the test of life.

## Chapter 4 (7:1–50)

**7:1–10 (mostly grey).** A centurion (power outside the church) whose slave (the natural that serves) is dying. He does not ask the Lord to come under his roof: "say the word". He knows authority from the order of his own life. The slave is healed at a distance. Good outside the church receives the Divine by the word alone. **Continues.** First of the Gentile line.

**7:11–17.** At Nain, at the gate, a widow's only son is carried out dead: the only truth of a good that longs for truth has died. "Do not weep"; he touches the bier, "Young man, rise"; he gives him to his mother. Truth is restored to the affection it belongs to. "A great prophet has arisen; God has visited his people." **Continues.** The first raising: spiritual death undone.

**7:18–28.** John, from prison, sends to ask: the literal sense of the Word, confined, asks whether this is the one. The answer is the works: blind see, lame walk, lepers cleansed, deaf hear, dead raised, the poor given the good news. This is Isaiah 61, the list that 4:17–21 would have announced. John is "more than a prophet", yet the least in the kingdom is greater: the letter prepares, but the least state of inner life is beyond it. **Continues.** *Literal note: John has not been introduced in the printed text (3:2–20 absent). The text presupposes him.*

**Test: 7:29–35 (U−).** The people and tax collectors "justified God", having been baptised by John; the Pharisees rejected God's purpose. Children in the market-place: neither the dirge (John's repentance) nor the flute (the bridegroom's joy) moves them. "Wisdom is justified by all her children." **Continues.** It completes the John section.

**7:36–50.** At a Pharisee's table (external worship) a woman who was a sinner (affection that knows its evil) wets his feet with tears (truths of repentance), wipes them with her hair (the ultimate truths), kisses and anoints them (love applied to the natural, the feet). The Pharisee judges. The two debtors: the one forgiven more loves more. "Her sins are forgiven, because she loved much." "Your faith has saved you; go in peace." **Continues.** Love and forgiveness shown as one movement; the meal line continues.

## Chapter 5 (8:1–25)

**8:1–3.** The twelve, and women healed of evil spirits who provide for them from their own means: affections, freed, serve the Divine and its truths from what they have. Mary Magdalene, from whom seven daimons went out: an affection wholly freed. **Continues.**

**8:4–8.** The sower: the truth of the Word falls on four states of reception: the path (trodden, thoughts of falsity take it), the rock (no depth), thorns (cares choke it), good soil (a hundredfold). "Whoever has ears, let him hear." **Continues.**

**Test: 8:9–10 (U−).** "To you it is given to know the mysteries; to others in parables, so that seeing they may not see." The inner sense is opened to those who can receive it and veiled from those who would profane it. **Continues.**

**Test: 8:11–15 (U?).** The explanation: the seed is the word of God; the path, those from whom the devil takes it; the rock, those who fall away in testing; the thorns, cares, riches and pleasures; the good soil, those who hold it fast "in an honest and good heart, and bear fruit with patience". **Continues.** It states the translation of the parable inside the text itself.

**8:16–18.** The lamp on the lampstand; nothing hidden that will not come to light; "take care how you hear: to whoever has, more will be given; from whoever has not, even what he thinks he has will be taken". **Continues.** The law of reception. It is stated again at 19:26.

**Test: 8:19 (A−).** "His mother and brothers came and could not reach him for the crowd." **Neutral.** 8:20 reports the same.

**8:20–21.** "My mother and brothers are those who hear my words and do them." The church and its truths (mother, brothers) are not by descent but by reception in life. **Continues.**

**8:22–25.** Crossing the lake, he sleeps; a storm fills the boat: temptation in the natural while the Divine seems absent. "Master, we are perishing." He rebukes wind and water: calm. "Where is your faith?" **Continues.**

## Chapter 6 (8:26–9:17)

**8:26–39.** The Gerasene country, "opposite Galilee": the natural mind's far side. A man naked (without truths), living among tombs (spiritual death), chained and breaking chains, driven into the wilderness: a mind held by a multitude of falsities ("Legion"). They beg not to be sent into the abyss and ask for the pigs (filthy loves); they enter them and drown in the lake. The man sits at his feet, clothed (truths restored), in his right mind. The people ask the Lord to leave: the rest of that region prefers its loves. The healed man is sent home to tell. **Continues.** Fully coherent element by element.

**8:40–56.** Jairus' only daughter (an affection of good, twelve years: all truths, at the point of completion) is dying. On the way, a woman with a flow of blood for twelve years (truth flowing away, falsified), having spent everything on physicians, touches the fringe of his cloak (the ultimate truth) and is healed; "power went out from me"; "your faith has saved you". The daughter is dead; "she is not dead but asleep"; he takes her hand, "Child, get up"; her spirit returns; "give her something to eat" (good to be appropriated at once). **Continues.** The two twelves interlock: one healing ends the twelve years of loss, the other the twelve years of life.

**9:1–6.** The twelve sent with power: nothing for the journey, no staff, bag, bread, money, second tunic. The truths are to go out depending on the Divine alone. Shake off the dust where not received. **Continues.**

**9:7–9.** Herod hears and is perplexed: John raised? Elijah? a prophet? "John I beheaded": the ruler of the natural (Herod) has cut off the letter of the Word (John) and now cannot place what has come. **Continues.**

**9:10–17.** In a deserted place (a state without good), toward evening (the end of a state): five loaves and two fish (a little good, a little natural truth) blessed, broken and given through the disciples; five thousand fed in groups of fifty; twelve baskets left over (all truths and goods remaining). **Continues.** The meal line: what is given from the Divine multiplies in being passed on.

## Chapter 7 (9:18–62)

**9:18–22.** Praying alone: "who do they say I am?" Peter: "the Anointed One of God". The first acknowledgement by faith. Then at once: the son of man must suffer, be rejected, killed, raised after three days. Acknowledgement is followed by the announcement of combat and completion. **Continues.**

**9:23–27.** Deny yourself, take up the cross daily: the daily combat against the proprium. Whoever saves his life loses it. **Continues.**

**9:28–36.** On the mountain (love), praying, his face changes and his clothing becomes dazzling white: the Divine shines through the Human and its truths. Moses and Elijah (the Word: law and prophets) speak with him "in glory". The disciples are heavy with sleep (the natural state). Peter wants three tents: to fix the vision in external worship. A cloud (the letter) overshadows them; a voice from the cloud (Divine truth through the letter): "This is my son; listen to him." Then Jesus alone. **Continues.** The Word is seen to speak of him, and the letter gives way to him.

**9:37–45.** Down from the mountain, a father's only son seized by a spirit, which the disciples could not cast out: "faithless generation". He heals the boy and gives him back to his father. Again: "the son of man is to be handed over". They do not understand. **Continues.**

**9:46–48.** Who is greatest? A child set beside him: innocence. "The least among you is the greatest." **Continues.**

**Test: 9:49–50 (U?).** A man casting out daimons in his name who does not follow them: "do not stop him; whoever is not against you is for you". Good outside the mind's own order is still of the Lord. **Continues.**

**9:51–56 (51, 53, 56 grey).** "When the days drew near for him to be taken up, he set his face to go to Jerusalem." The road begins: from the natural to the spiritual. A Samaritan village does not receive him "because his face was set towards Jerusalem". James and John want fire from heaven: zeal of anger. He rebukes them. **Continues.** *Literal note: "taken up" (9:51) points forward to an ascension that the printed text, ending at 24:47, does not contain. Weak break between 9:51 and the end of the text; see 24:48–53.*

**9:57–62.** Three who would follow: the son of man has nowhere to lay his head; "let the dead bury their dead"; no one who puts his hand to the plough and looks back. The road does not allow return to the former state. **Continues.**

## Chapter 8 (10:1–28)

**10:1–11.** Seventy (all) sent ahead in pairs (good and truth together); the harvest; lambs among wolves (innocence among falsity); no purse, bag or sandals; "peace to this house"; eat what is set before you; heal and say "the kingdom has come near". **Continues.**

**Test: 10:12–15 (U−).** Sodom's day more bearable than that town's; woes on Chorazin and Bethsaida; and "you, Capernaum, will you be exalted to heaven? You will be brought down to Hades." Capernaum is the place of the descent (4:31). In translation: the natural mind that received the Divine's works first and did not turn is the one that falls lowest. **Continues.** It is a severe step, but it follows from the law of reception (8:18): to whom much was given, much is required (12:48).

**10:16.** Whoever hears you hears me. **Continues.**

**10:17.** The seventy return: the daimons submit. **Continues.**

**Test: 10:18 (U−).** "I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven": the hells cast down from the mind's heights. **Continues.**

**10:19–20.** Authority over snakes and scorpions (sensual falsities); rejoice rather that your names are written in heaven: rejoice in what one is, not in what one has done. **Continues.**

**10:21–24.** He rejoices in the holy spirit: hidden from the wise, revealed to infants. No one knows the Father except the son. Blessed are the eyes that see. **Continues.**

**10:25–28.** "What must I do to inherit life?" Love God with heart, soul, strength and mind, and the neighbour as oneself. "Do this and you will live." **Continues.** The road states its whole content in two loves.

**Test: 10:29–37 (U−).** "Who is my neighbour?" A man goes down from Jerusalem to Jericho (from doctrine to life, from the spiritual to the natural), falls among robbers (falsities that strip truths and wound good), half dead. The priest and the Levite pass by (worship and doctrine without charity). A Samaritan (good outside the church) binds the wounds, pours in oil and wine (good and truth), sets him on his own beast (his own understanding), brings him to an inn (instruction), pays two denarii and promises to return. "Go and do likewise." **Continues.** It translates element by element. The man's road runs downward while the main road (9:51) runs upward. In translation that is no break: the Divine meets the mind where it has fallen.

**Test: 10:38–42 (U?).** Martha (serving) distracted; Mary (hearing) at his feet: "one thing is needed". Read as two affections, or as one woman under her name and her title (the library's thesis): either way, use and hearing, and hearing first. **Continues.**

## Chapter 9 (11:1–52)

**11:1–4.** "Teach us to pray." Father; may your holy spirit come; may your kingdom come; your bread for the coming day; forgive our sins as we forgive; do not bring us into temptation. In translation: the Divine love addressed as source; influx asked first; then the kingdom; then good from the Divine (bread); then forgiveness passed on; then protection in combat. **Continues.** On the Marcionite wording see the note at the end.

**11:5–8.** A friend at midnight (a state without light) asks three loaves (complete good) for a traveller; the door is shut, the children in bed; persistence opens it. **Continues.**

**11:9–13.** Ask, seek, knock. A father does not give a snake for a fish, a scorpion for an egg. "How much more will the Father give the holy spirit to those who ask." **Continues.** It closes the unit that 11:2 opened: the spirit asked for, the spirit given.

**11:14–23.** A mute daimon cast out: the mind can speak again. "By Beelzebul": the external mind calls the Divine work evil. A kingdom divided falls. "By the finger of God" (Divine power in the least things). The strong man fully armed is overcome by one stronger, who takes his armour: the proprium's defences taken by the Divine. "Whoever is not with me scatters." **Continues.**

**Test: 11:24–26 (U?).** The unclean spirit leaves, finds the house swept and empty, returns with seven worse. A mind cleared of evil but not filled with good is worse at the end. **Continues.** It completes 11:21–23.

**11:27–28.** "Blessed the womb that bore you." "Blessed rather those who hear the word of God and keep it." **Continues.** Descent counts for nothing; reception counts.

**11:29.** "This generation seeks a sign, and no sign will be given to it." **Continues.**

**Test: 11:30–32 (A−).** "Except the sign of Jonah"; Nineveh and the queen of the south will rise in judgement against this generation; something greater than Jonah and Solomon is here. **Break.** In the printed text the sentence ends "no sign will be given to it", and the next saying is the lamp (11:33): the only sign is light within. Translated, the Jonah saying gives a sign after all — the three days in the fish, the resurrection as sign — immediately after it has been refused. It also contradicts the principle of 16:31 (a rising from the dead does not convince) and of 24:11 (the report of the rising is not believed). This is the first inserted passage that breaks the inner story.

**11:33.** The lamp on the lampstand. **Continues.**

**Test: 11:34–36 (U?).** "Your eye is the lamp of your body": the understanding illumines the whole life; if it is evil, the body is dark; "take care that the light in you is not darkness". **Continues.** It completes 11:33.

**11:37–44.** At a Pharisee's table he does not wash first. "You clean the outside of the cup; inside you are full of greed. Did not the one who made the outside make the inside? Give as alms what is within, and all is clean." Woes: tithing herbs and passing over the calling and love of God; the best seats; unmarked graves. **Continues.** Inner against outer, stated in the letter.

**11:45–48.** Woes to the lawyers: burdens they do not lift; they build the tombs of the prophets their fathers killed. Worship of the letter of the Word that killed its meaning. **Continues.**

**Test: 11:49–51 (A−).** "The wisdom of God said: I will send prophets and apostles, some they will kill, so that the blood of all the prophets, from Abel to Zechariah, may be required of this generation." **Break (strain).** Translated, the guilt of every falsification from the first age to the last is imputed to one state now. That cuts across the logic of the story at every other point, where each state is answerable for what it has received (8:18; 12:47–48). Without it, 11:48 runs directly into 11:52: they built the tombs, and so they have taken away the key of knowledge.

**11:52.** "You have taken away the key of knowledge; you did not enter, and you hindered those who were entering." **Continues.** In translation: the key to the inner sense, withheld.

**Test: 11:53–54 (U?).** The scribes and Pharisees press him hard, lying in wait to catch him. **Neutral.**

## Chapter 10 (12:1–59)

**12:1–5.** The leaven of the Pharisees, hypocrisy (leaven in its bad sense: falsity working through the dough). Nothing hidden will not be revealed. Do not fear those who kill the body; fear him who can cast into Gehenna. **Continues.**

**Test: 12:6–7 (A− and U−).** Five sparrows sold for two pennies, not one forgotten by God; the hairs of your head are all numbered: providence in the least things, down to the ultimates. "Do not be afraid." **Continues.** It completes 12:4–5, which say what to fear, with what not to fear.

**12:8–12.** Acknowledging and denying before people; the word against the son of man forgiven, blasphemy against the holy spirit not; the spirit will teach you what to say. **Continues.**

**12:13–21.** "Tell my brother to divide the inheritance": the mind wants the Divine to judge its claims. "Who made me judge?" The rich fool: his land produces; he builds bigger barns; "soul, eat, drink, be merry"; "this night your soul is demanded". The proprium storing what flows through it as its own possession. **Continues.**

**12:22–32.** Do not worry: life more than food; the ravens (who neither sow nor store); the lilies (neither spin nor weave, clothed beyond Solomon); seek the kingdom; "do not be afraid, little flock". **Continues.** The answer to the rich fool: receiving instead of storing.

**Test: 12:33–34 (U?).** "Sell your possessions, give alms; make purses that do not wear out; where your treasure is, your heart will be." **Continues.**

**12:35–40.** Loins girded, lamps burning; servants waiting for the master to come from the wedding feast; "he will gird himself, have them recline at table, and serve them"; the thief at an unknown hour. **Continues.** In translation: the Divine itself serves the mind that waits. The line runs to 24:30 and 24:41–43.

**12:41–48.** The faithful steward set over the household; the servant who says "my master is delayed" and beats the servants is cut in pieces (separated: his outside from his inside) and placed with the unfaithful. Much given, much required. **Continues.**

**12:49–53.** "I came to cast fire": love kindled. "A baptism to be baptised with": the combat to come. Division in one house, father against son, mother against daughter: good separated from evil within the mind. **Continues.**

**12:54–59.** You read the sky but not the time. Settle with your accuser on the way; otherwise the judge, the officer, the prison, "until the last copper". **Continues.** In translation: evil not dealt with while there is time binds the mind until it has been paid out.

## Chapter 11 (13:1–28)

**Test: 13:1–9 (A−).** News of the Galileans whose blood Pilate mixed with their sacrifices, and of the eighteen killed by the tower in Siloam: "unless you repent, you will all likewise perish". Then the fig tree that bears no fruit for three years (natural good without works, through a whole state); "cut it down"; the vinedresser: "leave it one more year; I will dig and manure it". In translation: the end of a state without works, and the Divine patience that gives one more state. **Continues.** It carries 12:54–59 (settle while there is time) into release. It is also tied to the next passage by its number: the eighteen of the tower (13:4) and the woman bound eighteen years (13:11, 13:16). Translated, the eighteen die under a falling tower (truths in ultimates collapsing onto those who rely on them), and the woman bound eighteen years is freed. The two eighteens make a pair: judgement without turning, release with it. Inside the text the passage is woven, not attached.

**13:10–17 (10–13, 17 grey).** On a sabbath, in a synagogue, a woman bent double for eighteen years by a spirit of weakness: an affection that cannot look up, bound by Satan. "Woman, you are freed"; he lays his hands on her; she stands upright and glorifies God. The leader of the synagogue: "six days for work". "Do you not untie your ox or donkey from the manger on the sabbath to water it? And this daughter of Abraham whom Satan bound, should she not be freed?" **Continues.** The ox and donkey untied from the manger and led to water: the natural affection and the natural understanding loosed from their place of instruction and led to truth. Exact element by element.

**13:18–21.** The mustard seed sown in a garden becomes a tree, and the birds of the sky (thoughts, in their good sense) nest in its branches; leaven hidden in three measures of flour until all is leavened. The kingdom grows from the least within until the whole mind is pervaded. **Continues.** After the woman stands upright, the growth from within.

**Test: 13:22 (U?).** Through towns and villages, journeying toward Jerusalem. **Continues.**

**13:23–28 (23–24 grey).** "Will those saved be few?" The narrow door; the master rises and shuts it; "we ate and drank with you, you taught in our streets"; "I do not know where you are from"; weeping outside while the righteous are in the kingdom. External acquaintance with the Divine does not open the door. **Continues.**

**Test: 13:29–35 (A−).** People from east, west, north and south recline in the kingdom; the last first. Pharisees warn that Herod wants to kill him: "Tell that fox: today and tomorrow I cast out daimons and heal, and on the third day I am perfected." "Jerusalem, Jerusalem, killing the prophets… how often I wanted to gather your children as a hen her brood… your house is left to you desolate; you will not see me until you say, 'Blessed is he who comes'." In translation: goods from every quarter received; the course of glorification (two days of work, the third day completion); the church in the mind that kills its truths, and its house left empty. **Continues.** "On the third day I am perfected" is the road's own formula (9:22; 24:7).

## Chapter 12 (14:12–24)

**Test: 14:1–11 (U?).** At the house of a leading Pharisee, on a sabbath, a man with dropsy (a mind swollen with water: truths taken in without good) is healed; "which of you, if a son or an ox falls into a well, will not pull him out on the sabbath?" The places at table: do not take the first seat; "whoever exalts himself will be humbled". **Continues, and is needed.** The printed text begins this chapter with 14:12: "He said also to the man who had invited him." With 14:1–11 left out, no one has invited him: the printed 14:12 follows 13:28. **Literal break in the printed text (13:28 → 14:12).**

**14:12–14 (13 grey).** Invite the poor, the crippled, the lame, the blind, who cannot repay; repaid at the resurrection of the righteous. **Continues.**

**14:15–24 (15 grey).** "Blessed is anyone who eats bread in the kingdom." A man gives a great banquet (the conjunction offered). The invited excuse themselves: a field bought (the love of possessions), five yoke of oxen (natural affections to be tried), a wife married (the self's own affection). The poor, crippled, blind and lame are brought in from the streets, then those from the roads and hedges, "so that my house may be filled". None of the first invited will taste the banquet. **Continues.** The meal line, and every excuse translates.

**Test: 14:25–35 (U?).** Hating father, mother, wife, children, one's own life (the proprium's loves set second); carrying the cross; the tower that cannot be finished without counting the cost; the king who sends for peace against a stronger army; "whoever does not renounce all he has cannot be my disciple"; salt that has lost its taste (the desire of truth for good, gone). **Continues.**

## Chapter 13 (15:1–10)

**15:1–3 (1–2 grey).** Tax collectors and sinners come near to listen; Pharisees grumble: "he receives sinners and eats with them". **Continues.**

**15:4–7.** A hundred sheep (the full good of charity); one lost; the ninety-nine left in the wilderness; the one carried home on the shoulders (with all power); friends called to rejoice; more joy in heaven over one who repents. **Continues.**

**15:8–10.** Ten coins (the truths of the remnant); one lost; a lamp lit, the house swept (self-examination), found; joy before the angels. **Continues.**

**Test: 15:11–32 (A−).** The younger son takes his share and goes to a far country (the proprium claims what it received and departs); squanders it; famine; feeds pigs (serves filthy loves) and longs for their pods; "he came to himself" (the turning point of regeneration); "I will arise and go to my father"; the father sees him far off, runs, embraces him; robe (truths), ring (conjunction), sandals (the natural ordered), the fattened calf (natural good), a feast. The elder son, who never left, angry: "you never gave me a goat"; "son, you are always with me; all that is mine is yours; this your brother was dead and is alive, lost and is found". **Continues.** It completes the triad of the lost — the sheep that strays, the coin lost inside the house, the son who leaves and turns back — with the one element the first two lack: the human turning. Its words "was dead and is alive" are those of 24:5, "the Living One among the dead". The printed text carries the human turning elsewhere as events: the Samaritan leper "turned back" (17:15) and Zacchaeus (19:8). Inside the text the passage is woven.

## Chapter 14 (16:1–31)

**16:1–8 (1, 3, 8 grey).** A rich man's steward accused of squandering: called to account; "I cannot dig, I am ashamed to beg"; he reduces the debts (a hundred measures of oil to fifty: good; a hundred of wheat to eighty: good of the natural) so that he will be received into houses. The master praises his prudence. In translation: the mind that is about to lose its stewardship of what is not its own uses what it holds to make the goods of others its friends. **Continues.**

**16:9–13.** Make friends by the mammon of injustice, so that you are received into eternal dwellings; faithful in little, faithful in much; "if you were not faithful in what belongs to another, who will give you what is your own?"; no one serves two masters. **Continues.** The key to the steward: nothing held is one's own.

**16:14–18.** The Pharisees, lovers of money, scoff. "What is exalted among people is an abomination before God." The law and the prophets until John; since then the kingdom is proclaimed; "easier for heaven and earth to pass away than for one stroke of my words to fall". Whoever divorces his wife and marries another commits adultery. In translation: the letter of the Word (the law, John) gives way to the kingdom, but nothing of the Word falls; and good is not to be separated from its truth and joined to falsity (marriage, divorce, adultery). **Continues.** The divorce saying, abrupt in the letter, is continuous in translation: the marriage of good and truth that the words of the Word preserve.

**16:19–31.** A rich man in purple and fine linen (rich in truths of the Word, in good and truth as display) feasts daily; at his gate lies Lazarus covered with sores (those outside who lack truth and know their wounds), longing for the crumbs; dogs lick his sores. Both die. Lazarus is carried to Abraham's bosom (conjunction with the Lord in heaven); the rich man in Hades. "Send Lazarus to dip his finger in water (the least truth) to cool my tongue." "You received your good things; he his bad things; a great chasm is fixed." "Send him to my father's house, five brothers." "They have Moses and the prophets; let them hear them." "If someone goes to them from the dead, they will repent." "If they do not hear Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded if someone rises from the dead." **Continues.** It states the law that the end of the text carries out: the report of the rising is taken as an idle tale (24:11), and recognition comes only at the breaking of bread (24:30–31).

## Chapter 15 (17:1–37)

**17:1–4.** Stumbling-blocks must come, woe to the one through whom they come; the millstone; rebuke and forgive, seven times in a day. **Continues.**

**Test: 17:5–6 (U?).** "Increase our faith." Faith like a mustard seed uproots the mulberry and plants it in the sea. **Continues.**

**Test: 17:7–10 (U?; 17:10b A−).** The servant who comes in from the field does not sit down first; he serves his master and eats afterwards. "So you also, when you have done all you were commanded, say: we are unworthy servants; we have done only what we ought." In translation: the mind's works do not earn; good done is the Lord's in it. **Continues.** It is the rule of non-merit, which the story holds everywhere else (6:35; 12:37; 16:12).

**17:11–12.** On the way to Jerusalem, between Samaria and Galilee; ten lepers meet him at a village. **Continues.**

**Test: 17:13 (A−).** "Jesus, master, have mercy on us." **Neutral.**

**4:27 (Marcionite order: here).** "There were many lepers in Israel in the time of Elisha the prophet, and none was cleansed but Naaman the Syrian." In translation: within the church, profanation is not cleansed; it is cleansed in the one from outside who turns. **Continues**: it announces what happens next. *Literal note: a saying of Jesus set into the narrative without a word introducing it. A seam in the letter, not a break in the inner story.*

**17:14–19.** "Go, show yourselves to the priests"; as they go they are cleansed. One, seeing he is healed, turns back, glorifying God, falls at his feet giving thanks: a Samaritan. "Were not ten cleansed? Where are the nine? Only this foreigner." "Rise and go; your faith has saved you." **Continues.** The turning back of the outsider: the Samaritan line from 9:52 and the human turning of the absent 15:11–32, here as event.

**17:20–21.** "The kingdom of God is not coming with observation; nor will they say 'here it is' or 'there'; the kingdom of God is within you." **Continues.** In the letter it states the premise of the method itself: the kingdom is a state, not a place or an event.

**17:22–37 (23–24, 27, 29–31, 33–35, 37 grey).** Days to long for the son of man's day; "here, there": do not follow; like lightning from one side of the sky to the other; first he must suffer. As in the days of Noah (eating, drinking, marrying until the flood) and of Lot (buying, building until fire): the end of a state that lived only in the natural. On that day do not go down for your goods; remember Lot's wife (looking back: the road again, 9:62). Whoever seeks to keep his life will lose it. Two in a bed, two grinding: one taken, one left (separation in the same place). "Where, Lord?" "Where the body is, there the eagles gather." **Continues.**

## Chapter 16 (18:1–43)

**18:1–8 (4, 6, 8 grey).** Pray always and do not lose heart. A judge who neither fears God nor respects people; a widow (good without truth) keeps coming: "give me justice against my adversary"; he gives way. "Will not God give justice to his chosen who cry to him day and night?" "When the son of man comes, will he find faith on earth?" **Continues.**

**18:9–14 (9 grey).** The Pharisee standing apart thanks God he is not like others, fasts, tithes; the tax collector far off, eyes down, beats his breast: "God, be merciful to me, a sinner". He went down justified. Whoever exalts himself will be humbled. **Continues.** The two states of 6:20–26 in one scene.

**18:15–17 (15, 17 grey).** Infants brought; the disciples rebuke; "let the children come; to such belongs the kingdom"; receive it like a child. **Continues.**

**18:18–23.** "Good teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?" "Why do you call me good? No one is good except one, God the Father." The commandments kept from youth. "One thing you lack: sell all you have and give to the poor; treasure in heaven; come, follow me." He becomes very sad, being very rich. **Continues.** On the wording "God the Father", see the note at the end.

**Test: 18:24–30 (U?).** "How hard for those who have riches to enter the kingdom; easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle." "Who then can be saved?" "What is impossible with people is possible with God." Peter: "we have left everything". "No one who has left house, wife, brothers, parents or children for the kingdom will fail to receive many times more." **Continues.** It completes 18:18–23.

**Test: 18:31–33 (A−).** Taking the twelve aside: "we are going up to Jerusalem, and everything written by the prophets about the son of man will be fulfilled": handed over, mocked, insulted, spat on, flogged, killed, rising on the third day. **Continues.** The third announcement on the road (after 9:22, 9:44): in translation, the combat foreseen as the road nears the city.

**Test: 18:34 (U−).** "They understood none of these things." **Continues.**

**18:35–43.** Near Jericho, a blind man begging by the road (the mind that does not see truth, sitting by the way of life, asking). "Jesus of Nazareth is passing by." "Son of David, have mercy!" Rebuked, he cries more. "What do you want?" "Lord, let me see again." "See again; your faith has saved you." He sees and follows, glorifying God. **Continues.** Sight given at the last stage before the city.

## Chapter 17 (19:1–26)

**19:1–10 (1, 3–5, 7 grey).** In Jericho, Zacchaeus, chief tax collector, rich, small in stature (the mind small in its own estimation), climbs a sycamore (natural good) to see. "Come down quickly; today I must stay at your house." He receives him with joy; the crowd grumbles. "Half of my possessions I give to the poor; if I have defrauded anyone, I restore fourfold." "Today salvation has come to this house; he too is a son of Abraham. The son of man came to seek and save the lost." **Continues.** The rich ruler (18:23) could not let go; Zacchaeus does. The turning and restoring that the absent prodigal tells as parable is here as event.

**19:11–26 (12, 14–21, 24–25 grey).** Near Jerusalem, "they thought the kingdom would appear immediately". A nobleman goes to a far country to receive a kingdom and return; ten servants, ten minas (knowledges of truth): "trade until I come". His citizens hate him: "we do not want this man to reign over us". On his return: one mina made ten (cities: ten); one made five; one kept it in a handkerchief, afraid of a severe master. "From your own words I judge you"; the mina taken and given to the one with ten; "to everyone who has, more will be given; from the one who has not, even what he has will be taken". **Continues.** It closes the teaching journey with the law of reception that 8:18 opened it with.

**Test: 19:27 (U−).** "But these enemies of mine, who did not want me to reign over them, bring them here and slaughter them before me." In translation: the evils in the mind that refused the Lord's rule are brought before him and destroyed. **Continues.** In the letter it is the most violent sentence of the road; in translation it is not a break. It completes the parable: the citizens of 19:14 who refused his reign are answered.

**Test: 19:28 (U?).** "He went on ahead, going up to Jerusalem." **Continues.**

**Test: 19:29–46 (A−).** From the Mount of Olives (love), a colt never ridden (the natural understanding, not yet used by the self) is untied and brought; garments spread (truths laid under him); he rides down; the disciples praise: "blessed is the king who comes; peace in heaven, glory in the highest"; "if these were silent, the stones would cry out". He weeps over the city: "if you had known the things that make for peace; your enemies will set a palisade around you and leave not one stone upon another, because you did not know the time of your visitation". In the temple he drives out those who sell: "my house shall be a house of prayer; you have made it a den of robbers". In translation: the Divine enters the church in the mind as its king, on the natural understanding made subject; it grieves over what the church has made of itself; it clears worship of trade. **Continues, and is needed.** Without it, the printed text goes from 19:26 (the minas, told "near Jerusalem", 19:11) directly to 20:1, "as he was teaching the people in the temple". The road reaches the temple without arriving: no entry, and no clearing of the temple before he teaches in it. **Gap in the printed text (19:26 → 20:1).** It is the turning point of the road, and the inner story needs it.

**Test: 19:47–48 (U?).** He teaches daily in the temple; the chief priests seek to destroy him; the people hang on his words. **Continues.**

## Chapter 18 (20:1–44)

**20:1–8.** Teaching in the temple; the chief priests, scribes and elders: "by what authority?" "Was John's baptism from heaven or from people?" They will not answer; "neither will I tell you". The heads of the falsified church cannot place the letter of the Word, and so cannot place him. **Continues.**

**Test: 20:9–17 (A−).** A man plants a vineyard (the church), lets it to tenants and goes away; he sends servants for fruit; they are beaten and sent away empty; he sends his beloved son: "perhaps they will respect him"; they kill him to take the inheritance. "He will destroy those tenants and give the vineyard to others." "The stone the builders rejected has become the head of the corner." In translation: the church in the mind, given the Word and asked for its fruit, rejects every truth sent and finally the Divine truth itself, to keep the inheritance as its own; the church passes to others; the rejected truth becomes the foundation. **Continues.** It tells in advance, as parable, the Passion that the text is about to tell as event.

**Test: 20:18 (U−).** "Everyone who falls on that stone will be broken; on whom it falls, it will crush." **Continues** with 20:17.

**20:19 (without its last clause).** The scribes and chief priests seek to lay hands on him at that hour, but fear the people. **Continues** from 20:8: the hostility answers the authority question. *With 20:9–18 in place, the canonical clause "for they perceived he had told this parable against them" follows. The parable and its clause stand or fall together; either way the inner story runs.*

**20:20–26 (20–23, 26 grey).** Spies, flattery: "is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar?" "Whose image is on the denarius?" "Give to Caesar what is Caesar's, and to God what is God's." In translation: the natural order has its due; the mind, which bears God's image, is owed to God. **Continues.**

**20:27–36.** The Sadducees, who deny the resurrection: seven brothers, one wife; "whose wife will she be in the resurrection?" "The sons of this age marry; those counted worthy of that age neither marry nor are given in marriage; they cannot die; they are like angels and are sons of God, being sons of the resurrection." In translation: the question of external marriage answered by the state of the living, where conjunction is no longer of the natural kind. **Continues.**

**Test: 20:37–38 (A−).** "That the dead are raised, Moses showed at the bush, when he calls the Lord the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. He is not God of the dead but of the living, for all live to him." **Continues.** It states the theme of the ending in its own words: the living, not the dead (24:5). It is a proof from Moses; the story runs without it as well.

**20:39–40 (40 grey).** "Teacher, you have spoken well." They no longer dare to ask. **Continues.**

**20:41–44 (42–43 grey).** "How can they say the Anointed One is David's son? David calls him Lord." In translation: the Divine Human is not the son of the church's natural lineage; it is its Lord. **Continues.**

**Test: 20:45–47 (U?).** "Beware of the scribes, who like to walk in long robes and love greetings and the best seats, who devour widows' houses and for show make long prayers." **Continues**, and leads into what follows.

**Test: 21:1–4 (U?).** Rich people putting gifts into the treasury; a poor widow puts in two small coins: "she has put in more than all; they gave from abundance, she from her poverty, all she had to live on." In translation: good longing for truth (the widow), robbed by the external church (20:47), gives everything it has; the rich give what they have over. **Continues.** It is the counter-image to 20:47.

**Test: 21:5–6 (U?).** Some speak of the temple adorned with beautiful stones and offerings: "the days will come when not one stone will be left upon another". The external worship of the church, admired for its stones (truths in ultimates), is to be torn down. **Continues, and is needed.** The printed text continues with 21:7: "And they asked him, 'Teacher, when will these things be, and what will be the sign when these things are about to take place?'" In the printed text 21:7 follows 20:44. "These things" refers to nothing. **Literal break in the printed text (20:44 → 21:7).**

## Chapter 19 (21:7–38)

**21:7–17.** "When will these things be?" Do not be led astray by those who say "I am he"; wars, earthquakes, famines, signs from heaven: the church in the mind falling into conflict, its goods and truths failing. Before that, persecution: brought before synagogues, kings and governors; "I will give you a mouth and wisdom"; betrayed by parents and brothers; hated for my name. **Continues.**

**Test: 21:18 (A−).** "But not a hair of your head will perish." In the letter it contradicts 21:16 ("some of you they will put to death"). In translation it does not: the natural life may be destroyed, but not the least of the ultimate truths (hair) of the one who endures. **Continues.** It joins 21:16 to 21:19.

**21:19–20.** "By your endurance you will gain your lives." "When you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies, know that its desolation is near": the church in the mind besieged by falsities. **Continues.**

**Test: 21:21–22 (A−).** "Then let those in Judea flee to the mountains; let those inside the city leave; these are days of vengeance, to fulfil all that is written." In translation: from the besieged church, flight to love (the mountains). **Continues.**

**21:23–24 (grey).** Woe to the pregnant and nursing (new goods and truths in formation); captivity; Jerusalem trampled by the nations until their times are fulfilled. **Continues.**

**21:25–28.** Signs in sun, moon and stars (love, faith and knowledges darkened), the roaring sea (the natural in turmoil), the powers of the heavens shaken; "then they will see the son of man coming from the heavens with great power"; "straighten up and raise your heads, because your redemption is near". **Continues.** "Straighten up" is the word used for the bent woman (13:11–13): the same release.

**21:29–33.** The fig tree and all the trees: when they come into leaf, summer is near; so the kingdom. "Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not." **Continues.** The fig tree in leaf: the natural beginning to bear again (against the barren fig tree of 13:6–9).

**21:34–38 (36 grey).** Watch that your hearts are not weighed down; pray. By day teaching in the temple, by night on the Mount of Olives (love). **Continues.**

## Chapter 20 (22:1–71)

**22:1–6 (2, 6 grey).** Passover near; the chief priests seek to kill him; Judas Iscariot goes to them and agrees for money. The love of self within the church sells the Divine for knowledges turned to gain. **Continues.**

**22:7–13 (7, 9–13 grey).** Peter and John (faith and charity) sent to prepare; a man carrying a jar of water (truth) will lead them into a house (the mind); a large upper room, furnished (the interior, ready). **Continues.** Every element translates.

**22:14–15.** At the hour he reclines with the apostles: "I have earnestly desired to eat this Passover with you before I suffer." **Continues.**

**Test: 22:16 (A−).** "I will not eat it until it is fulfilled in the kingdom of God." **Neutral.** It doubles 22:18.

**22:17–20 (17–18 grey).** A cup, divided among them; "I will not drink of the fruit of the vine until the kingdom comes"; bread: "this is my body given for you; do this in remembrance of me"; the cup after supper: "the new covenant in my blood, poured out for you". In translation: the Divine good communicated to the mind, and the Divine truth that joins it (the new covenant). **Continues.** The new wine of 5:37–38 reaches the cup.

**22:21–23 (21, 23 grey).** The hand of the betrayer is on the table; "woe to that man"; they ask which of them. **Continues.**

**Test: 22:24–30 (U?).** A dispute about who is greatest; "the kings of the nations lord it over them; not so with you"; "who is greater, the one at table or the one who serves? I am among you as one who serves"; "you who have stood by me in my trials, I confer on you a kingdom, that you may eat and drink at my table and sit on thrones". **Continues.** It fulfils 12:37: the master who girds himself and serves.

**22:31–34 (31–32 grey).** "Simon, Satan has demanded to sift you like wheat; I have prayed that your faith may not fail; when you have turned back, strengthen your brothers." "I am ready to go to prison and death." "The cock will not crow today until you have denied three times that you know me." **Continues.** It sets up the denial and the turning.

**Test: 22:35–38 (A−).** "When I sent you without purse, bag or sandals, did you lack anything?" "Nothing." "Now let whoever has a purse take it; whoever has no sword buy one; for what is written must be fulfilled: he was counted among the lawless." "Here are two swords." "It is enough." In translation: the state has changed; before, the mind went out depending on the Divine alone; in the combat now beginning it must take up its truths (swords) and fight as if by itself. **Continues.** It refers back to an attested passage (10:4).

**22:39–42 (39–40, 42 grey).** To the Mount of Olives (love), as was his custom; "pray that you may not enter into temptation"; a stone's throw away, kneeling: "Father, if you are willing, remove this cup from me; yet not my will but yours be done". The Human's will yielding to the Divine. **Continues.**

**Test: 22:43–44 (U?).** An angel from heaven strengthens him; in agony he prays more earnestly; his sweat like drops of blood. The combat at its height, with Divine truth (the angel) from within. **Continues.**

**22:45–48 (45–46 grey).** He finds them asleep from sorrow; "rise and pray". The crowd; Judas draws near to kiss him: "would you betray the son of man with a kiss?" **Continues.**

**Test: 22:49–51 (U− and A−).** "Lord, shall we strike with the sword?" One strikes the high priest's slave and cuts off his right ear; "no more of this"; he touches the ear and heals it. In translation: truth used to wound cuts off obedience (the ear); the Divine restores obedience even in the enemy's servant. **Continues.**

**Test: 22:52–53 (U?).** "Have you come out with swords and clubs as against a robber? I was with you daily in the temple. But this is your hour, and the power of darkness." **Continues.**

**Test: 22:54–62 (U?).** They seize him and bring him into the high priest's house; Peter follows at a distance, sits by the fire in the courtyard; three times he denies: "I do not know him"; the cock crows; "the Lord turned and looked at Peter"; Peter remembers, goes out, weeps bitterly. In translation: faith, warming itself by the natural fire in the night, denies the Divine three times (completely); the new state begins (the cock); the Lord's look; faith remembers and weeps. **Continues, and is needed, twice over.** First, 22:34 announces the denial and 22:32 the turning; without this passage neither happens. Second, the printed text goes from the kiss (22:48) to 22:63, "the men who were holding Jesus in custody", but the arrest itself is in 22:54. **Literal break in the printed text (22:48 → 22:63).**

**22:63–71 (65, 68 grey).** Mocked and beaten; blindfolded: "prophesy, who struck you?"; at day the council: "if you are the Anointed One, tell us"; "from now on the son of man will be seated at the right hand of the power of God"; "you say that I am". **Continues.**

## Chapter 21 (23:1–56)

**23:1–5 (4–6 grey).** Brought before Pilate (the natural reason in power). The charges (with the Marcionite additions): misleading the nation, destroying the law and the prophets, forbidding tribute, turning away the women and the children, calling himself the Anointed One, a king. In translation: the falsified church sees the opening of the Word's inner sense as the destruction of the law and the prophets, and the drawing of affections and innocence (women, children) to the Divine as their seduction. **Continues.** "I find no guilt in this man."

**23:6–12 (6, 10–12 grey).** Sent to Herod (the ruler of the sensual), who has long wanted to see a sign; he questions him; no answer; mockery; a splendid robe (false display); Herod and Pilate become friends that day. In translation: the sensual and the natural reason, enemies before, unite against the Divine. **Continues.**

**Test: 23:13–16 (U?).** Pilate to the chief priests and people: "neither I nor Herod found him guilty; I will punish and release him". **Continues.**

**23:18–25 (20–21, 24 grey).** "Away with him; release Barabbas" (insurrection and murder); "crucify, crucify"; a third time "what evil has he done?"; their voices prevail; Barabbas released, Jesus handed over to their will. The church in the mind chooses its own evil over the Divine. **Continues.**

**Test: 23:26 (U?).** Simon of Cyrene, coming from the country, made to carry the cross behind Jesus: one from outside bears the combat, following. **Continues.**

**Test: 23:27–31 (U?).** Women lamenting; "daughters of Jerusalem, do not weep for me, weep for yourselves and your children; if they do this when the wood is green, what will happen when it is dry?" **Continues.**

**23:32–33.** Two criminals led with him; at the place called the Skull they crucify him, one criminal on the right and one on the left. **Continues.**

**Test: 23:34 (34a in NA27 double brackets; 34b A−).** "Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing." They divide his garments by casting lots. In translation: the mercy of the Divine at the extreme; and the church dividing and dispersing the truths of the Word (his garments). **Continues.**

**Test: 23:35–38 (U?).** The rulers sneer, "let him save himself"; soldiers offer sour wine (falsified truth); the inscription "the King of the Jews". **Continues.**

**Test: 23:39–43 (U?; 23:43 A−).** One criminal: "save yourself and us"; the other: "we receive what our deeds deserve; this man has done nothing wrong; Jesus, remember me when you come into your kingdom"; "today you will be with me in paradise". In translation: of two evils at the end of a state, one claims, one acknowledges its evil and is received at once. **Continues, and is needed.** The printed text introduces the two criminals (23:32–33) and then does nothing with them. **Weak break in the printed text (23:33 → 23:44).**

**23:44–46.** From the sixth to the ninth hour darkness over the land (no truth and no good left in the church); the sun's light fails (love extinguished); the curtain of the temple torn in two (the inmost opened, the veil between the Divine and the Human removed); "Father, into your hands I commit my spirit"; he breathes his last. **Continues.** The climax of the road: union of the Human with the Divine.

**Test: 23:47–49 (U?).** The centurion: "certainly this man was righteous"; the crowds go home beating their breasts; his acquaintances and the women from Galilee stand at a distance, watching. **Continues.**

**23:50–56 (54 grey).** Joseph, a good and righteous councillor who did not consent, awaiting the kingdom; he asks for the body; wraps it in linen (pure truth); a tomb hewn in stone where no one had been laid (truth in ultimates, new); the day of preparation, the sabbath beginning; the women see where he is laid; spices and ointments (goods); they rest on the sabbath. **Continues.**

## Chapter 22 (24:1–47)

**24:1–3 (2 grey).** The first day of the week at early dawn (the new state at its beginning); spices; the stone rolled away (the letter removed); the body of the Lord Jesus not found (the merely natural is no longer there). **Continues.**

**24:4–8 (8 grey).** Two men in dazzling clothing (Divine truth, inner and outer); the women bow their faces (humility); "why do you seek the Living One among the dead? He has been raised. Remember all that he told you while still in Galilee: the son of man must be handed over, crucified, and on the third day rise." They remember. **Continues.** The state, not the place.

**24:9–11 (10 grey).** They tell the eleven and the rest; Mary Magdalene, Joanna, Mary of James and the others; "these words seemed an idle tale, and they did not believe them". The affections tell; the intellect does not believe. **Continues.** 16:31 is carried out.

**Test: 24:12 (U−).** Peter runs to the tomb, stoops, sees the linen cloths lying by themselves, goes home wondering. In translation: faith inspects the truths left behind and wonders, without recognition. **Neutral.**

**24:13–23 (14, 17, 20, 22–23 grey).** That same day two of them (good and truth) walk (progress of life) to Emmaus, sixty stadia from Jerusalem, talking; Jesus himself comes near and walks with them; their eyes are held (the understanding not yet opened). "What are you discussing?" They stand still, sad. Cleopas: "are you the only visitor in Jerusalem who does not know?" "What things?" "Concerning Jesus of Nazareth, a prophet mighty in deed and word before God and all the people; how our chief priests and rulers handed him over and crucified him; we thought he was the one to redeem Israel; this is the third day; some women astounded us; they did not find his body and said they had seen a vision of angels who said he was alive." **Continues.** The third day reached (completion) without recognition.

**Test: 24:24 (U−).** "Some of those with us went to the tomb and found it as the women said, but him they did not see." **Neutral.** It goes with 24:12.

**24:25–26.** "O foolish ones, slow of heart to believe all that he spoke to you! Was it not necessary that the Anointed One should suffer these things and enter into his glory?" In translation: glorification through combat, taught to the mind by the Divine itself. **Continues.**

**Test: 24:27 (U−).** "Beginning with Moses and all the prophets, he interpreted to them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself." In translation: the Divine opens the inner sense of the whole Word to the mind on its road. **Continues.** It is the stage of truth before good: understanding opened by the Word, then recognition in the bread.

**24:28–31 (28–29 grey).** Near the village he acts as if going on; "stay with us, it is toward evening" (the end of a state; the mind asks the Divine to remain); he goes in; at table he takes bread, blesses, breaks, gives; their eyes are opened and they recognise him; he vanishes from their sight. **Continues.** Recognition in the good communicated; and the perception is not held by sight.

**Test: 24:32 (U−).** "Did not our hearts burn within us while he talked with us on the road, while he opened the scriptures to us?" **Continues**, with 24:27: love kindled by truth.

**Test: 24:33–35 (U?).** They rise at once, return to Jerusalem, find the eleven gathered: "the Lord has risen indeed and has appeared to Simon"; they tell how he was known in the breaking of bread. In translation: the mind returns to its church; faith (Simon) too has seen. **Continues.** It also supplies the group of 24:36 ("he stood among them"); without it, the group is only the two of Emmaus, and the commission to all nations (24:47) is given to them. Not a break.

**24:36–38 (36 grey).** As they talk, he stands among them: "peace to you"; startled and afraid, they think they see a spirit; "why are you troubled, why do doubts rise in your hearts?" **Continues.**

**24:39–40 (40 grey).** "See my hands and my feet, that it is I myself; for a spirit does not have bones as you see that I have." He shows them his hands and feet. In translation: power (hands) and the natural (feet) present; the Divine present even in the truths of the ultimates (bones). **Continues.**

**Test: the canonical words at 24:39 (attested absent).** "Touch me and see", and "flesh" with the bones. In translation: perception by contact (touch: communication and conjunction), and good as well as truth in the ultimates (flesh and bones). **Continues.** With them, the Divine is present in the ultimates as good and truth both; without them, as truth. Neither form breaks the story. The method does not mark the canonical words as an intrusion.

**24:41–43 (42–43 grey).** While they still disbelieve for joy and marvel: "Have you anything here to eat?" They give him broiled fish (natural truth made good by fire); he takes it and eats before them. In translation: the Divine appropriates the natural of the mind; the conjunction completed in the ultimates. **Continues.** The meal line ends here (5:29; 9:16; 12:37; 14:16; 22:15; 24:30).

**Test: 24:44–46 (U−).** "Everything written about me in the law of Moses, the prophets and the psalms must be fulfilled." "Then he opened their minds to understand the scriptures." "Thus it is written, that the Anointed One should suffer and rise on the third day." In translation: the opening of the understanding to the inner sense of the whole Word, the completion of the truth line (as 24:43 completes the good line). **Continues, and is needed.** The printed text ends with 24:47: "… and that repentance for the forgiveness of sins should be proclaimed in his name to all the nations." This is a subordinate clause with no main clause; its governing verb is "it is written" in 24:46. **Literal break in the printed text (24:43 → 24:47).**

**24:47.** Repentance for the forgiveness of sins proclaimed in his name to all nations. In translation: the regeneration that the road has traced is offered to every state of every mind. **Continues.** It ends where the road began: repentance, the removal of evil (4:33–35).

**Test: 24:48–53 (U?).** "You are witnesses"; "stay in the city until you are clothed with power from on high" (influx); he leads them out to Bethany, lifts his hands and blesses them; while blessing he parts from them (and is carried up into heaven); they worship, return to Jerusalem with great joy, and are continually in the temple blessing God. In translation: the mind, clothed with influx, is blessed by the Divine, which is no longer seen but is worshipped; the road ends in worship in the inmost, where it began in the assembly on the sabbath (4:31). **Continues, and closes the road.** Without it, 9:51 ("the days drew near for him to be taken up") points to an end the text does not reach. **Weak break in the printed text (9:51 without its fulfilment).**

## The Marcionite wordings, read by the method

Each attested Marcionite wording that the reconstruction prints was translated in the same way as the rest.
- **18:19, "God the Father":** both forms translate as "the Divine love itself". The addition neither carries nor breaks the story.
- **11:2, the spirit petition in place of the hallowed name:** both translate coherently (the Name: the quality of the Divine; the holy spirit: influx).
- **11:3, "your bread":** both "your" and "our" bread translate coherently.
- **16:17, "my words" for "the law":** both translate coherently.
- **24:25, "all that he spoke" for "all that the prophets spoke":** both translate coherently.
- **23:2, the added charges:** these continue the story (see 23:1–5).

**Verdict: neutral.** The method does not mark these wordings, and does not decide between them and NA27. They stand or fall on the witnesses.

## What the reading found

### 1. The printed text breaks at eight points

Each break is mended by a passage the reconstruction does not print.

| Where the printed text breaks | Kind | Passage that mends it | Its class |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4:16 → 4:23: he stands up to read, and nothing is read | literal and inner | 4:17–21 (the Isaiah reading) | U− |
| 13:28 → 14:12: "the man who had invited him", with no host | literal | 14:1–11 | U? |
| 19:26 → 20:1: the road reaches the temple without arriving | inner (gap) | 19:29–46 (entry, lament, temple cleared) | A− |
| 20:44 → 21:7: "these things", with no antecedent | literal | 21:5–6 | U? |
| 22:34 → 22:63: the denial is announced and never happens; "the men holding him", with no arrest | literal and inner | 22:54–62 | U? |
| 23:32–33 → 23:44: two criminals introduced and dropped | inner (weak) | 23:39–43 | U?; 23:43 A− |
| 24:43 → 24:47: the last verse is a clause without a main clause | literal | 24:44–46 | U− |
| 9:51 → the end: "taken up", with no ascension; the road does not reach worship | inner (weak) | 24:48–53 | U? |

The literal breaks can be checked by anyone; they are grammar and narrative, not interpretation. Three of them (14:12, 21:7, 22:63) are produced by the reconstruction's own paragraph rule (METHOD §4.3): a whole NA27 paragraph with no attested verse is left out, even when the next printed verse depends on it.

### 2. Of all the passages put back, two break the inner story

- **11:30–32, the sign of Jonah:** it gives a sign directly after "no sign will be given", against the law of 16:31 and 24:11.
- **11:49–51, the blood of all the prophets required of this generation:** an imputation across all ages, against the law of reception that holds everywhere else (8:18; 12:47–48). This is a strain more than a clean break.

Both are attested absent: Epiphanius says outright that Marcion's text lacked them. There, the method and the witness agree.

### 3. Every other passage the witnesses leave out continues the story

That includes the parables (13:1–9, 15:11–32, 10:29–37, 20:9–17) and the other passages attested absent (A−):
- 3:2–4:15;
- 12:6;
- 13:29–35;
- 18:31–33;
- 19:29–46;
- 20:37–38;
- 21:18, 21:21–22;
- 22:16, 22:35–38, 22:50–51.

None of these is foreign to the inner story. 19:29–46 is needed by it.

### 4. The method and the witnesses disagree in a pattern

Where the method finds the story broken, the passage that mends it is in most cases one the witnesses do not mention (U? or U−). There the method may lead, because there is no direct witness. In two cases (19:29–46, and 23:43 within 23:39–43), the passage that mends the break is one that Epiphanius reports absent from Marcion's text. There, Marcion's text is the witness, and the method says that text has a hole.

### 5. Limits of this reading

It is one reader, with one key, applied once. The key is fixed but not yet referenced section by section to Swedenborg. The verdicts "continues" and "inner break" are judgements of coherence, which a second reader should repeat blind. The literal breaks in finding 1 do not depend on the key.
