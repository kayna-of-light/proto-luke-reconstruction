# Second pass: the reading on the final text

*Run on the text as printed after the first restorations (commit 44b842c), with the owner's working premise stated: the original Luke was composed by the Jamesian community, which taught in correspondence. The goal is the text closest to that original.*

## 1. What the first pass got wrong

The first pass sorted every unprinted passage into three groups:

- **continues** — it fits the line;
- **needed** — the printed text points to it or does not finish without it;
- **leans on** — the printed text opens a strand only the passage closes, but "the printed line still runs without it".

The third group rested on a test of the letter: does sentence X still run on to sentence Y? Most "continues, does not close a gap" verdicts rest on the same test ("18:23 → 18:35 runs").

The method asks something else: does each element, taken as its spiritual function, carry the movement on?

- A strand the text opens and never closes is a break in the inner narrative, even when the sentences still join.
- So "leans on" is not a category of its own. Each such passage is either needed or not, by the inner test.

The first restorations also added words to the text, and words can point backwards. So the second pass has two parts:

- the "leans on" passages and the three wordings marked as strains, re-tested on the final text (§2–3);
- every unprinted passage that the restored text now touches, re-tested (§4).

The test is the same as in METHOD §11. A passage is needed if either:

- (a) printed words have nothing to refer to or answer without it; or
- (b) a movement the text sets going does not complete without it.

## 2. The "leans on" passages, on the final text

### 12:28a — "if God so clothes the grass" (attested omission, Epiphanius sch. 31)

- **(a) By its words.** The printed 12:28 is "πόσῳ μᾶλλον ὑμᾶς, ὀλιγόπιστοι".
  - "You" is in the accusative, and the printed text gives it no verb.
  - Its verb is ἀμφιέζει, "clothes", which is in 12:28a.
- **(b) By its mechanics.** 12:22 sets two needs: "what you will eat" and "what you will put on (ἐνδύσησθε)".
  - 12:24 answers the first from its giver: "God feeds them".
  - Only 12:28a answers the second from its giver: "God clothes".
  - In the final text the clothing strand now has its end. The restored 24:49 says "until you are clothed (ἐνδύσησθε) with power from on high".
  - The same verb runs from the anxiety of 12:22 to the clothing from the height. The one place that names who clothes is 12:28a.
  - Without it, eating is given and clothing has no giver.

**Needed**, by (a) and (b). Printed against Marcion's text.

### 19:27 — the citizens who refused his reign (U−)

**(b) By its mechanics.** The parable sets two groups going at the departure:
- the servants given money (19:13);
- the citizens who send after him, "we do not want this man to reign over us" (19:14).

He returns "having received the kingdom" (19:15) and reckons with the servants (19:15–26). The citizens are never reckoned with. Their end is only 19:27.

In the final text the parable is the frame of what follows at once:
- he goes up to Jerusalem (19:28);
- he is hailed as "the king" (19:38);
- he weeps over the city that "did not know the time of its visitation", which "your enemies" will surround (19:42–44).

Read inwardly, the enemies who refuse his reign are what in the person refuses it. Their death "before him" is that refusal ended in his presence. This is:
- the enemy's power trodden (10:19);
- the enemies made a footstool (20:43, printed);
- "whoever loses his soul will make it live" (17:33).

The enemy as person is loved (6:27). The enemy as power is put to death.

**Needed**, by (b). Printed against the class U−.

### 23:13–16 — Pilate's second finding and first offer of release (U?)

**(a) By its words, three times.**
- The printed 23:18 "release (ἀπόλυσον) to us Barabbas" answers an offer of release. Only 23:16 makes one ("having disciplined him, I will release him").
- The printed 23:20 says Pilate addressed them "again (πάλιν), wishing to release Jesus". Without 23:13–16 there is no earlier wish to release.
- The attested 23:22 says "a third time (τρίτον) he said". Its words repeat 23:14–16 almost word for word: "I found nothing deserving death in him; having disciplined him, I will release him".
  - The first finding is 23:4 and the third is 23:22. The second is 23:14–15.

**(b) By its mechanics.** Release offered three times answers denial spoken three times (22:34; 22:57–60). The one who looses the bound (13:12, 13:16) is offered release, and the bound murderer is loosed in his place.

**Needed**, by (a) and (b).

### 23:39–43 — the two beside him (23:39–42 U?; 23:43 attested absent, Epiphanius sch. 72)

**(b) By its mechanics.**
- The printed text states the law of the two: "two in one bed, one taken along, the other left" (17:34–35).
- It brings two evil-doers to the cross, "one on the right and one on the left" (23:32–33), and does not bring them back.
- Tertullian's own reading of 23:32–33 is "two criminals fixed around him, that he might be reckoned among the wicked" (Marc. 4.42.4).

In the final text three restored strands meet here:
- **"With you to death" (22:33).** Faith promised it and, in the restored 22:54–62, denied him. The evil-doer is with him in death and is told "with me (μετ’ ἐμοῦ) in paradise".
- **Remembering.**
  - Peter "remembered the word of the Lord" (22:61, restored).
  - The evil-doer asks "remember me" (23:42).
  - The women are told "remember" and remember (24:6–8).
- **Today.**
  - "Today this scripture is fulfilled" (4:21, restored).
  - "Today salvation has come to this house" (19:9).
  - "Today you will be with me in paradise" (23:43).

"When you come into your kingdom" takes up the kingdom received (19:12, 19:15) and the king hailed (19:38, restored).

**Needed**, by (b). 23:43 is printed against Marcion's text.

## 3. The three wordings marked as strains, on the final text

Each is an adopted Marcionite reading (METHOD §5.2). The first pass found that each breaks the inner line where NA27's wording continues it. The final text strengthens each verdict:

- **10:21 "Lord of heaven"**, without "and of the earth".
  - The restored passages put the revealing below. The Lord stands on the earth in the midst (24:36), lifts his hands on earth and blesses (24:50).
  - The heights are given to the earth: "clothed with power from on high" (24:49), "peace in heaven, and glory in the highest" sung on the descent of the mountain (19:37–38).
  - "Lord of heaven" alone takes the lordship off the place where the revealing happens. "And of the earth" is printed.
  - Only the omission strains. The verb εὐχαριστῶ, the impersonal "was hidden" and the absent vocative were read as neutral, so they stay.
- **18:19 "God the Father"**, for "God". The restored 4:18 opens with "the spirit of the Lord", and 24:49 names "the promise of my Father". Father is a relation the son speaks of, never a title set against "me". "One, God" is printed.
- **21:27 "from the heavens, with great power"**, for "in a cloud with power and great glory".
  - The final text now has the taking up (24:51) and the return to the temple. The coming in glory that 9:26 announces and 24:26 names is the coming NA27's wording says.
  - The cloud of the mountain (9:34–35) is the covering the voice speaks from.
  - NA27 is printed.

*The premise gives the textual ground.* The Fathers report that Marcion's church went on altering its copies (Tertullian, Marc. 4.5.7; Adam. 96,6–8; METHOD §2). On the premise of a correspondential original, a wording that turns an inner relation into an outward title or separation belongs to that transmission, not to the original. The other adopted Marcionite readings were read at their places in the first pass. They continue the line or are neutral, and they stand.

## 6. Left open

Most first-pass verdicts of "continues, does not close a gap" were decided by the literal "X → Y runs". The second pass re-tested only the passages the restored text touches. Re-testing every one of them by the inner test alone would be a third pass over the whole text, and it is not done here. Under the premise, a passage that continues the inner line is consistent with the original. But continuing is not the same as being needed, and §11 prints only what is needed.
