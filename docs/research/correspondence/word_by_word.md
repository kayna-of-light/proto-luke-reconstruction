# Proto-Luke Read Word by Word in Correspondence

*Research reading, begun 2026-10-13. One reader. No structure is assumed in advance: each word is given its spiritual function as Swedenborg uses it, sentence by sentence, and whatever structure there is has to come out of the reading.*

*The earlier pericope reading (`proto_luke_in_correspondence.md`) worked from a key fixed beforehand, and partly from a scheme; this reading replaces it as the method. One correction to that key: in Swedenborg Judah and Judea stand for the celestial, the good of love, not for doctrine; Jerusalem is the church as to doctrine.*

*Confidence: a function marked (?) is not secure in Swedenborg's usage, or has not been found there; it is left open, not filled in.*

## Chapter 1

### 3:1

*Ἐν ἔτει πεντεκαιδεκάτῳ τῆς ἡγεμονίας Τιβερίου Καίσαρος, ἡγεμονεύοντος Ποντίου Πιλάτου τῆς Ἰουδαίας, καὶ τετρααρχοῦντος τῆς Γαλιλαίας Ἡρῴδου…*

- **"In the year"**: a state. Times are states.
- **"fifteenth"**: (?) not fixed.
- **"of the rule of Tiberius Caesar"**: the state is counted from a ruler outside the land, by worldly dominion. The mind measures its time by what governs it from outside.
- **"Pontius Pilate governing Judea"**: Judea, the good of love, the celestial, is governed by a foreign governor. The highest thing in the land is held by the world's officer.
- **"Herod tetrarch of Galilee"**: the natural is ruled by a quarter-ruler. **"Philip his brother"** rules the rough, stony country (Trachonitis: "rugged"). **"Lysanias"** rules Abilene (?). The land is divided among rulers who are none of them its own.
- **What the sentence names, and what it does not.** It lists only rulers. In this text there is no high priest (3:2a, Annas and Caiaphas, is absent), and no "word of God came to John" (3:2b–20 is absent). The whole land is governed from outside. Nothing in it is of the Lord yet, and no word has come to anyone.

### 4:31

*κατῆλθεν εἰς Καφαρναοὺμ πόλιν τῆς Γαλιλαίας. καὶ ἦν διδάσκων αὐτοὺς ἐν τοῖς σάββασιν·*

- **"he came down"** (κατῆλθεν): the Divine passes from above to below, of itself. The subject has no name. In this text the descent is the first coming of the word into the land. It does not come to a prophet; it comes down itself.
- **"to Capernaum, a city"**: a city is a doctrine, so the descent is into a doctrine, a place where the mind holds teaching. **"Capernaum"**: (?).
- **"of Galilee"**: of the natural, not of Judea. It comes down into the lowest governed part, not into the celestial that the governor holds.
- **"he was teaching them"**: truth imparted.
- **"on the sabbaths"** (plural): in the holy states, the states of rest and conjunction, each time. The teaching enters where the mind is at rest, not where it is busy.

### 4:32

*καὶ ἐξεπλήσσοντο ἐπὶ τῇ διδαχῇ αὐτοῦ, ὅτι ἐν ἐξουσίᾳ ἦν ὁ λόγος αὐτοῦ.*

- **"they were astonished"**: struck out of their own state by something not their own.
- **"his word was with authority"**: truth that has power in itself, because good is in it. Truth that is only learned has no authority; truth from good acts. The mind feels the difference before it understands it.

### 4:33

*καὶ ἐν τῇ συναγωγῇ ἦν ἄνθρωπος ἔχων πνεῦμα δαιμονίου ἀκαθάρτου, καὶ ἀνέκραξεν φωνῇ μεγάλῃ·*

- **"in the assembly"**: where what is taught is gathered and held, the mind's received doctrine.
- **"a man having a spirit of an unclean daimon"**: a falsity from evil (a daimon is a falsity; unclean, from evil love) sits inside a person, inside the gathering of truths itself. It was there before; nothing showed it.
- **"he cried out with a loud voice"**: the falsity becomes manifest only when the word with authority is present. It is not sought out; truth arrives, and falsity shows itself.

### 4:34

*Ἔα, τί ἡμῖν καὶ σοί, Ἰησοῦ Ναζαρηνέ; ἦλθες ἀπολέσαι ἡμᾶς; οἶδά σε τίς εἶ, ὁ ἅγιος τοῦ θεοῦ.*

- **"What have we to do with you"** (τί ἡμῖν καὶ σοί): no conjunction is possible. The falsity speaks as "we": falsities are many, though it is one daimon in one man.
- **"have you come to destroy us?"**: the falsity takes the Divine's presence as its own end, and rightly.
- **"I know who you are"** (οἶδα): the falsity knows. Knowledge of the Divine is here on the side of evil, and it is correct knowledge.
- **"the holy one of God"**: the holy is Divine truth. The falsity names truth correctly.

### 4:35

*καὶ ἐπετίμησεν αὐτῷ ὁ Ἰησοῦς λέγων· Φιμώθητι καὶ ἔξελθε ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ. καὶ ῥίψαν αὐτὸν τὸ δαιμόνιον εἰς τὸ μέσον ἐξῆλθεν ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ μηδὲν βλάψαν αὐτόν.*

- **"Jesus rebuked it"**: he is named here for the first time in the text, at the moment of separating.
- **"Be muzzled"** (φιμώθητι): the falsity's correct knowledge is not allowed to speak. A true confession that comes from evil is refused. The Divine is not to be acknowledged by what will not receive it.
- **"come out of him"** (ἔξελθε): separation. The falsity is taken out; the man is not.
- **"throwing him into the midst"** (ῥίψαν εἰς τὸ μέσον): the departing falsity's last act casts the man down, into the middle. The midst is the inmost. The man is laid low, at the centre of the gathering.
- **"it came out of him, having harmed him in nothing"**: what is removed is not the person. The person loses nothing of his own by losing the falsity.

### 4:36–37 (grey)

*Τίς ὁ λόγος οὗτος ὅτι ἐν ἐξουσίᾳ καὶ δυνάμει ἐπιτάσσει τοῖς ἀκαθάρτοις πνεύμασιν, καὶ ἐξέρχονται; … καὶ ἐξεπορεύετο ἦχος περὶ αὐτοῦ εἰς πάντα τόπον τῆς περιχώρου.*

- **"What is this word?"**: the word again, now "with authority and power". It does not argue with falsities; it commands them.
- **"they come out"** (ἐξέρχονται).
- **"a report went out into every place"** (ἐξεπορεύετο): what happened in one place spreads into every state round about. *Places are states.*

### 4:16

*Καὶ ἦλθεν εἰς Ναζαρά, οὗ ἦν τεθραμμένος, καὶ εἰσῆλθεν κατὰ τὸ εἰωθὸς αὐτῷ ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῶν σαββάτων εἰς τὴν συναγωγήν, καὶ ἀνέστη ἀναγνῶναι.*

- **"he came"**: not "came down" now. The movement is level, toward something that is already his.
- **"to Nazara, where he had been brought up"** (τεθραμμένος, "nourished, fed"): the place of nurture, what the mind has been fed on from its beginning: the familiar, its own.
- **"as was his custom… on the sabbath day, into the assembly"**: the same holy state and the same gathering as at Capernaum. The setting is the same, so the difference must lie in the receivers.
- **"he stood up to read"** (ἀνέστη ἀναγνῶναι): he rises in order to read the Word. *Rising (ἀνίστημι) is used here of rising to read the Word; the same verb comes back in 4:29.*

### 4:23

*Πάντως ἐρεῖτέ μοι τὴν παραβολὴν ταύτην· Ἰατρέ, θεράπευσον σεαυτόν· ὅσα ἠκούσαμεν γενόμενα εἰς τὴν Καφαρναοὺμ ποίησον καὶ ὧδε ἐν τῇ πατρίδι σου.*

- **"Physician, heal yourself"**: the familiar asks the healer to prove himself on himself. Healing is turned back on the healer.
- **"whatever we heard was done"** (ἠκούσαμεν): they have heard of the works; they have not received them.
- **"do here also, in your fatherland"** (πατρίς): the works are demanded as owed to the place that is his, as if belonging to the Divine made the Divine belong to them. The falsity at Capernaum knew him and was removed. The own at Nazareth knows where he comes from and claims him.

### 4:24 (grey)

*οὐδεὶς προφήτης δεκτός ἐστιν ἐν τῇ πατρίδι αὐτοῦ.*

- **"accepted"** (δεκτός): received. What has always been one's own cannot receive the one who comes out of it. It thinks it has him already.

### 4:28 (grey)

*καὶ ἐπλήσθησαν πάντες θυμοῦ ἐν τῇ συναγωγῇ…*

- **"all in the assembly were filled with rage"**: at Capernaum one man in the assembly held the falsity; here the whole assembly is filled. At Capernaum it was a falsity lodged in a person; here it is the gathering itself, the own as a whole.

### 4:29

*καὶ ἀναστάντες ἐξέβαλον αὐτὸν ἔξω τῆς πόλεως, καὶ ἤγαγον αὐτὸν ἕως ὀφρύος τοῦ ὄρους ἐφ’ οὗ ἡ πόλις ᾠκοδόμητο αὐτῶν, ὥστε κατακρημνίσαι αὐτόν·*

- **"rising up"** (ἀναστάντες): the same verb as 4:16. He rose to read the Word; they rise to cast him out. The own's rising is against the Divine.
- **"they cast him out of the city"** (ἐξέβαλον): out of their doctrine. At Capernaum the falsity was cast out of the man; here the Divine is cast out of the doctrine. It is the same act, reversed.
- **"to the brow of the hill on which their city was built"**: a hill or mountain is love; their city, their doctrine, is built on it. They lead him to the edge of the height on which their own teaching rests. The love they build on is their own, and it has an edge.
- **"to throw him down headlong"** (κατακρημνίσαι): to cast the Divine down from that height. He came down of himself (κατῆλθεν, 4:31); they would cast him down. The daimon at Capernaum threw the man down (ῥίψαν, 4:35); the own would throw the Divine down.

### 4:30

*αὐτὸς δὲ διελθὼν διὰ μέσου αὐτῶν ἐπορεύετο.*

- **"but he, passing through their midst"** (διὰ μέσου): through their inmost. At Capernaum the falsity threw the man "into the midst" and left; here the Divine passes "through the midst" of those who would throw him, and is not touched.
- **"went on"** (ἐπορεύετο): the going, the road, is named for the first time, at the point where the own has rejected him. The road begins from the rejection by the own.

### 4:40

*Δύνοντος δὲ τοῦ ἡλίου ἅπαντες ὅσοι εἶχον ἀσθενοῦντας νόσοις ποικίλαις ἤγαγον αὐτοὺς πρὸς αὐτόν· ὁ δὲ ἑνὶ ἑκάστῳ αὐτῶν τὰς χεῖρας ἐπιτιθεὶς ἐθεράπευεν αὐτούς.*

- **"as the sun was setting"**: the end of a state. The sun is love; its setting is the close of what that day was.
- **"all who had sick ones"**: those who have (εἶχον) the weak bring them. The disorders are brought by others, not by themselves. Every kind of illness is brought (νόσοις ποικίλαις, "of many colours").
- **"laying his hands on each one"** (ἑνὶ ἑκάστῳ): the hands are power, applied to each singly. No disorder is healed in general.

### 4:41

*ἐξήρχετο δὲ καὶ δαιμόνια ἀπὸ πολλῶν κραυγάζοντα καὶ λέγοντα ὅτι Σὺ εἶ ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ θεοῦ. καὶ ἐπιτιμῶν οὐκ εἴα αὐτὰ λαλεῖν, ὅτι ᾔδεισαν τὸν χριστὸν αὐτὸν εἶναι.*

- **"daimons came out of many, crying 'you are the son of God'"**: the same as 4:33–35, now in many. The falsities again know correctly (ᾔδεισαν, "they knew") and say so.
- **"he did not allow them to speak, because they knew"**: the reason for the silence is stated. It is not that they were wrong; it is that they knew. Knowledge of the Divine coming from what will not receive him is not let speak.

### 4:42

*Γενομένης δὲ ἡμέρας ἐξελθὼν ἐπορεύθη εἰς ἔρημον τόπον· καὶ οἱ ὄχλοι ἐπεζήτουν αὐτόν, καὶ ἦλθον ἕως αὐτοῦ, καὶ κατεῖχον αὐτὸν τοῦ μὴ πορεύεσθαι ἀπ’ αὐτῶν.*

- **"when day came"**: a new state.
- **"going out, he went"** (ἐξελθὼν ἐπορεύθη): the verb of the falsities (ἐξέρχομαι, "come out") is now his own, and so is the road (πορεύομαι).
- **"to a deserted place"**: a state where there is nothing of the mind's own.
- **"the crowds sought him… and held him back from going away from them"** (κατεῖχον… τοῦ μὴ πορεύεσθαι): those who received him now try to keep him. They do not reject him like Nazareth; they hold him. It is a gentler form of the same claim. They try to stop the road.

### 4:43

*Καὶ ταῖς ἑτέραις πόλεσιν εὐαγγελίσασθαί με δεῖ τὴν βασιλείαν τοῦ θεοῦ, ὅτι ἐπὶ τοῦτο ἀπεστάλην.*

- **"to the other cities also I must"** (δεῖ): the Divine cannot be kept by one doctrine. It must go to the others.
- **"the kingdom of God"**: the Lord's rule, set against the rulers of 3:1.
- **"for this I was sent"** (ἀπεστάλην): sent means proceeding. What came down came down because it was sent.

### 4:44 (grey)

*καὶ ἦν κηρύσσων εἰς τὰς συναγωγὰς τῆς Ἰουδαίας.*

- **"proclaiming in the assemblies of Judea"**: Judea is the celestial, the good of love. The proclamation goes on into the assemblies of the land whose good is governed by Pilate (3:1). *Many manuscripts read "Galilee" here; NA27 has "Judea". (?) whether this is a step or a variant.*

### What came out of the reading of chapter 1

Four movements, each defined by how the receiver stands to the Divine:
1. **The land under rulers.** No priest and no word: everything is governed from outside (3:1).
2. **The Divine descends of itself.** Its word has authority, and its arrival makes the hidden falsity in the mind's own gathering show itself. That falsity knows him correctly, is not allowed to speak, and is taken out without harm to the person (4:31–37, 4:40–41).
3. **The own, in its familiar place, claims him.** It hears of the works and demands them, cannot receive him, and rises to throw him down from the height its doctrine is built on. He passes through its midst (4:16–30).
4. **The receivers try to keep him, and he must go on** (4:42–44).

Threads the Greek carries, to be followed in the chapters ahead:
- **Down (κατ-).** He comes down (κατῆλθεν); they would throw him down (κατακρημνίσαι).
- **Rising (ἀνίστημι).** He rises to read (ἀνέστη); they rise against him (ἀναστάντες).
- **The midst (μέσον).** The falsity throws the man into the midst; he passes through their midst.
- **Coming out (ἐξέρχομαι).** The falsities come out; the report goes out; he goes out.
- **Going (πορεύομαι).** The road is first named at the rejection (4:30) and resisted by the crowds (4:42).
- **Knowing.** The falsities "know"; Nazareth has "heard".
- **Feeding (τρέφω).** "Where he had been fed", to be followed.

**What this chapter does not show yet: anything ascending.** The only rising in it is the own rising against the Divine (4:29). The movement so far is descent, the exposure and removal of falsity, rejection by the own, and the beginning of a road. What ascends, if anything, has to come out of the chapters that follow.
