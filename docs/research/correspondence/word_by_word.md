# Proto-Luke Read Word by Word in Correspondence

*Research reading, begun 2026-10-13. One reader. No structure is assumed in advance: each word is given its spiritual function as Swedenborg uses it, sentence by sentence, and whatever structure there is has to come out of the reading.*

*The earlier pericope reading (`proto_luke_in_correspondence.md`) worked from a key fixed beforehand, and partly from a scheme; this reading replaces it as the method. One correction to that key: in Swedenborg Judah and Judea stand for the celestial, the good of love, not for doctrine; Jerusalem is the church as to doctrine.*

*Confidence: a function marked (?) is not secure in Swedenborg's usage, or has not been found there; it is left open, not filled in.*

## Chapter 1

### 3:1

*Ἐν ἔτει πεντεκαιδεκάτῳ τῆς ἡγεμονίας Τιβερίου Καίσαρος, ἡγεμονεύοντος Ποντίου Πιλάτου τῆς Ἰουδαίας, καὶ τετρααρχοῦντος τῆς Γαλιλαίας Ἡρῴδου…*

- **"In the year"**: a state. Times are states.
- **"fifteenth"**: (?) not fixed.
- **"of the rule of Tiberius Caesar"**: the state is counted from a ruler outside the land, by worldly dominion. The mind measures its time by what governs it from outside.
- **"Pontius Pilate governing Judea"**: Judea, the good of love, the celestial, is governed by a foreign governor. The highest thing in the land is held by the world's officer.
- **"Herod tetrarch of Galilee"**: the natural is ruled by a quarter-ruler. **"Philip his brother"** rules the rough, stony country (Trachonitis: "rugged"). **"Lysanias"** rules Abilene (?). The land is divided among rulers who are none of them its own.
- **What the sentence names, and what it does not.** It lists only rulers. In this text there is no high priest (3:2a, Annas and Caiaphas, is absent), and no "word of God came to John" (3:2b–20 is absent). The whole land is governed from outside. Nothing in it is of the Lord yet, and no word has come to anyone.

### 4:31

*κατῆλθεν εἰς Καφαρναοὺμ πόλιν τῆς Γαλιλαίας. καὶ ἦν διδάσκων αὐτοὺς ἐν τοῖς σάββασιν·*

- **"he came down"** (κατῆλθεν): the Divine passes from above to below, of itself. The subject has no name. In this text the descent is the first coming of the word into the land. It does not come to a prophet; it comes down itself.
- **"to Capernaum, a city"**: a city is a doctrine, so the descent is into a doctrine, a place where the mind holds teaching. **"Capernaum"**: (?).
- **"of Galilee"**: of the natural, not of Judea. It comes down into the lowest governed part, not into the celestial that the governor holds.
- **"he was teaching them"**: truth imparted.
- **"on the sabbaths"** (plural): in the holy states, the states of rest and conjunction, each time. The teaching enters where the mind is at rest, not where it is busy.

### 4:32

*καὶ ἐξεπλήσσοντο ἐπὶ τῇ διδαχῇ αὐτοῦ, ὅτι ἐν ἐξουσίᾳ ἦν ὁ λόγος αὐτοῦ.*

- **"they were astonished"**: struck out of their own state by something not their own.
- **"his word was with authority"**: truth that has power in itself, because good is in it. Truth that is only learned has no authority; truth from good acts. The mind feels the difference before it understands it.

### 4:33

*καὶ ἐν τῇ συναγωγῇ ἦν ἄνθρωπος ἔχων πνεῦμα δαιμονίου ἀκαθάρτου, καὶ ἀνέκραξεν φωνῇ μεγάλῃ·*

- **"in the assembly"**: where what is taught is gathered and held, the mind's received doctrine.
- **"a man having a spirit of an unclean daimon"**: a falsity from evil (a daimon is a falsity; unclean, from evil love) sits inside a person, inside the gathering of truths itself. It was there before; nothing showed it.
- **"he cried out with a loud voice"**: the falsity becomes manifest only when the word with authority is present. It is not sought out; truth arrives, and falsity shows itself.

### 4:34

*Ἔα, τί ἡμῖν καὶ σοί, Ἰησοῦ Ναζαρηνέ; ἦλθες ἀπολέσαι ἡμᾶς; οἶδά σε τίς εἶ, ὁ ἅγιος τοῦ θεοῦ.*

- **"What have we to do with you"** (τί ἡμῖν καὶ σοί): no conjunction is possible. The falsity speaks as "we": falsities are many, though it is one daimon in one man.
- **"have you come to destroy us?"**: the falsity takes the Divine's presence as its own end, and rightly.
- **"I know who you are"** (οἶδα): the falsity knows. Knowledge of the Divine is here on the side of evil, and it is correct knowledge.
- **"the holy one of God"**: the holy is Divine truth. The falsity names truth correctly.

### 4:35

*καὶ ἐπετίμησεν αὐτῷ ὁ Ἰησοῦς λέγων· Φιμώθητι καὶ ἔξελθε ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ. καὶ ῥίψαν αὐτὸν τὸ δαιμόνιον εἰς τὸ μέσον ἐξῆλθεν ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ μηδὲν βλάψαν αὐτόν.*

- **"Jesus rebuked it"**: he is named here for the first time in the text, at the moment of separating.
- **"Be muzzled"** (φιμώθητι): the falsity's correct knowledge is not allowed to speak. A true confession that comes from evil is refused. The Divine is not to be acknowledged by what will not receive it.
- **"come out of him"** (ἔξελθε): separation. The falsity is taken out; the man is not.
- **"throwing him into the midst"** (ῥίψαν εἰς τὸ μέσον): the departing falsity's last act casts the man down, into the middle. The midst is the inmost. The man is laid low, at the centre of the gathering.
- **"it came out of him, having harmed him in nothing"**: what is removed is not the person. The person loses nothing of his own by losing the falsity.

### 4:36–37 (grey)

*Τίς ὁ λόγος οὗτος ὅτι ἐν ἐξουσίᾳ καὶ δυνάμει ἐπιτάσσει τοῖς ἀκαθάρτοις πνεύμασιν, καὶ ἐξέρχονται; … καὶ ἐξεπορεύετο ἦχος περὶ αὐτοῦ εἰς πάντα τόπον τῆς περιχώρου.*

- **"What is this word?"**: the word again, now "with authority and power". It does not argue with falsities; it commands them.
- **"they come out"** (ἐξέρχονται).
- **"a report went out into every place"** (ἐξεπορεύετο): what happened in one place spreads into every state round about. *Places are states.*

### 4:16

*Καὶ ἦλθεν εἰς Ναζαρά, οὗ ἦν τεθραμμένος, καὶ εἰσῆλθεν κατὰ τὸ εἰωθὸς αὐτῷ ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῶν σαββάτων εἰς τὴν συναγωγήν, καὶ ἀνέστη ἀναγνῶναι.*

- **"he came"**: not "came down" now. The movement is level, toward something that is already his.
- **"to Nazara, where he had been brought up"** (τεθραμμένος, "nourished, fed"): the place of nurture, what the mind has been fed on from its beginning: the familiar, its own.
- **"as was his custom… on the sabbath day, into the assembly"**: the same holy state and the same gathering as at Capernaum. The setting is the same, so the difference must lie in the receivers.
- **"he stood up to read"** (ἀνέστη ἀναγνῶναι): he rises in order to read the Word. *Rising (ἀνίστημι) is used here of rising to read the Word; the same verb comes back in 4:29.*

### 4:23

*Πάντως ἐρεῖτέ μοι τὴν παραβολὴν ταύτην· Ἰατρέ, θεράπευσον σεαυτόν· ὅσα ἠκούσαμεν γενόμενα εἰς τὴν Καφαρναοὺμ ποίησον καὶ ὧδε ἐν τῇ πατρίδι σου.*

- **"Physician, heal yourself"**: the familiar asks the healer to prove himself on himself. Healing is turned back on the healer.
- **"whatever we heard was done"** (ἠκούσαμεν): they have heard of the works; they have not received them.
- **"do here also, in your fatherland"** (πατρίς): the works are demanded as owed to the place that is his, as if belonging to the Divine made the Divine belong to them. The falsity at Capernaum knew him and was removed. The own at Nazareth knows where he comes from and claims him.

### 4:24 (grey)

*οὐδεὶς προφήτης δεκτός ἐστιν ἐν τῇ πατρίδι αὐτοῦ.*

- **"accepted"** (δεκτός): received. What has always been one's own cannot receive the one who comes out of it. It thinks it has him already.

### 4:28 (grey)

*καὶ ἐπλήσθησαν πάντες θυμοῦ ἐν τῇ συναγωγῇ…*

- **"all in the assembly were filled with rage"**: at Capernaum one man in the assembly held the falsity; here the whole assembly is filled. At Capernaum it was a falsity lodged in a person; here it is the gathering itself, the own as a whole.

### 4:29

*καὶ ἀναστάντες ἐξέβαλον αὐτὸν ἔξω τῆς πόλεως, καὶ ἤγαγον αὐτὸν ἕως ὀφρύος τοῦ ὄρους ἐφ’ οὗ ἡ πόλις ᾠκοδόμητο αὐτῶν, ὥστε κατακρημνίσαι αὐτόν·*

- **"rising up"** (ἀναστάντες): the same verb as 4:16. He rose to read the Word; they rise to cast him out. The own's rising is against the Divine.
- **"they cast him out of the city"** (ἐξέβαλον): out of their doctrine. At Capernaum the falsity was cast out of the man; here the Divine is cast out of the doctrine. It is the same act, reversed.
- **"to the brow of the hill on which their city was built"**: a hill or mountain is love; their city, their doctrine, is built on it. They lead him to the edge of the height on which their own teaching rests. The love they build on is their own, and it has an edge.
- **"to throw him down headlong"** (κατακρημνίσαι): to cast the Divine down from that height. He came down of himself (κατῆλθεν, 4:31); they would cast him down. The daimon at Capernaum threw the man down (ῥίψαν, 4:35); the own would throw the Divine down.

### 4:30

*αὐτὸς δὲ διελθὼν διὰ μέσου αὐτῶν ἐπορεύετο.*

- **"but he, passing through their midst"** (διὰ μέσου): through their inmost. At Capernaum the falsity threw the man "into the midst" and left; here the Divine passes "through the midst" of those who would throw him, and is not touched.
- **"went on"** (ἐπορεύετο): the going, the road, is named for the first time, at the point where the own has rejected him. The road begins from the rejection by the own.

### 4:40

*Δύνοντος δὲ τοῦ ἡλίου ἅπαντες ὅσοι εἶχον ἀσθενοῦντας νόσοις ποικίλαις ἤγαγον αὐτοὺς πρὸς αὐτόν· ὁ δὲ ἑνὶ ἑκάστῳ αὐτῶν τὰς χεῖρας ἐπιτιθεὶς ἐθεράπευεν αὐτούς.*

- **"as the sun was setting"**: the end of a state. The sun is love; its setting is the close of what that day was.
- **"all who had sick ones"**: those who have (εἶχον) the weak bring them. The disorders are brought by others, not by themselves. Every kind of illness is brought (νόσοις ποικίλαις, "of many colours").
- **"laying his hands on each one"** (ἑνὶ ἑκάστῳ): the hands are power, applied to each singly. No disorder is healed in general.

### 4:41

*ἐξήρχετο δὲ καὶ δαιμόνια ἀπὸ πολλῶν κραυγάζοντα καὶ λέγοντα ὅτι Σὺ εἶ ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ θεοῦ. καὶ ἐπιτιμῶν οὐκ εἴα αὐτὰ λαλεῖν, ὅτι ᾔδεισαν τὸν χριστὸν αὐτὸν εἶναι.*

- **"daimons came out of many, crying 'you are the son of God'"**: the same as 4:33–35, now in many. The falsities again know correctly (ᾔδεισαν, "they knew") and say so.
- **"he did not allow them to speak, because they knew"**: the reason for the silence is stated. It is not that they were wrong; it is that they knew. Knowledge of the Divine coming from what will not receive him is not let speak.

### 4:42

*Γενομένης δὲ ἡμέρας ἐξελθὼν ἐπορεύθη εἰς ἔρημον τόπον· καὶ οἱ ὄχλοι ἐπεζήτουν αὐτόν, καὶ ἦλθον ἕως αὐτοῦ, καὶ κατεῖχον αὐτὸν τοῦ μὴ πορεύεσθαι ἀπ’ αὐτῶν.*

- **"when day came"**: a new state.
- **"going out, he went"** (ἐξελθὼν ἐπορεύθη): the verb of the falsities (ἐξέρχομαι, "come out") is now his own, and so is the road (πορεύομαι).
- **"to a deserted place"**: a state where there is nothing of the mind's own.
- **"the crowds sought him… and held him back from going away from them"** (κατεῖχον… τοῦ μὴ πορεύεσθαι): those who received him now try to keep him. They do not reject him like Nazareth; they hold him. It is a gentler form of the same claim. They try to stop the road.

### 4:43

*Καὶ ταῖς ἑτέραις πόλεσιν εὐαγγελίσασθαί με δεῖ τὴν βασιλείαν τοῦ θεοῦ, ὅτι ἐπὶ τοῦτο ἀπεστάλην.*

- **"to the other cities also I must"** (δεῖ): the Divine cannot be kept by one doctrine. It must go to the others.
- **"the kingdom of God"**: the Lord's rule, set against the rulers of 3:1.
- **"for this I was sent"** (ἀπεστάλην): sent means proceeding. What came down came down because it was sent.

### 4:44 (grey)

*καὶ ἦν κηρύσσων εἰς τὰς συναγωγὰς τῆς Ἰουδαίας.*

- **"proclaiming in the assemblies of Judea"**: Judea is the celestial, the good of love. The proclamation goes on into the assemblies of the land whose good is governed by Pilate (3:1). *Many manuscripts read "Galilee" here; NA27 has "Judea". (?) whether this is a step or a variant.*

### What came out of the reading of chapter 1

Four movements, each defined by how the receiver stands to the Divine:
1. **The land under rulers.** No priest and no word: everything is governed from outside (3:1).
2. **The Divine descends of itself.** Its word has authority, and its arrival makes the hidden falsity in the mind's own gathering show itself. That falsity knows him correctly, is not allowed to speak, and is taken out without harm to the person (4:31–37, 4:40–41).
3. **The own, in its familiar place, claims him.** It hears of the works and demands them, cannot receive him, and rises to throw him down from the height its doctrine is built on. He passes through its midst (4:16–30).
4. **The receivers try to keep him, and he must go on** (4:42–44).

Threads the Greek carries, to be followed in the chapters ahead:
- **Down (κατ-).** He comes down (κατῆλθεν); they would throw him down (κατακρημνίσαι).
- **Rising (ἀνίστημι).** He rises to read (ἀνέστη); they rise against him (ἀναστάντες).
- **The midst (μέσον).** The falsity throws the man into the midst; he passes through their midst.
- **Coming out (ἐξέρχομαι).** The falsities come out; the report goes out; he goes out.
- **Going (πορεύομαι).** The road is first named at the rejection (4:30) and resisted by the crowds (4:42).
- **Knowing.** The falsities "know"; Nazareth has "heard".
- **Feeding (τρέφω).** "Where he had been fed", to be followed.

**What this chapter does not show yet: anything ascending.** The only rising in it is the own rising against the Divine (4:29). The movement so far is descent, the exposure and removal of falsity, rejection by the own, and the beginning of a road. What ascends, if anything, has to come out of the chapters that follow.

## Chapter 2

*A correction to one function used in the earlier pass: in Swedenborg Peter is faith, James charity, and John the goods of charity, works.*

### 5:1–3 (5:1, 5:3 grey)

*ἐν τῷ τὸν ὄχλον ἐπικεῖσθαι αὐτῷ καὶ ἀκούειν τὸν λόγον τοῦ θεοῦ καὶ αὐτὸς ἦν ἑστὼς παρὰ τὴν λίμνην… καὶ εἶδεν δύο πλοῖα ἑστῶτα… οἱ δὲ ἁλιεῖς… ἔπλυνον τὰ δίκτυα… καθίσας δὲ ἐκ τοῦ πλοίου ἐδίδασκεν*

- **"the crowd pressing on him to hear the word of God"**: in chapter 1 the crowds held him back (κατεῖχον); here they press in to hear. The wish to keep him has become a wish to hear.
- **"he was standing beside the lake"**: the lake, or sea, is the natural memory with its knowledges. He stands at its edge.
- **"two boats standing"**: boats are knowledges of truth and good from the Word, what carries the mind over its sea. They are standing, idle.
- **"the fishermen had got out and were washing their nets"**: those who gather natural truths have left their knowledges and are cleaning the instruments of gathering. (?) for the nets.
- **"he got into one of the boats, Simon's… sat down and taught from the boat"**: the Divine enters the mind's own knowledge and teaches from inside it. Simon is hearing and obedience; Simon's boat is the knowledge that belongs to the obedient.

### 5:4–5 (grey)

*Ἐπανάγαγε εἰς τὸ βάθος καὶ χαλάσατε τὰ δίκτυα ὑμῶν εἰς ἄγραν… Ἐπιστάτα, δι’ ὅλης νυκτὸς κοπιάσαντες οὐδὲν ἐλάβομεν, ἐπὶ δὲ τῷ ῥήματί σου χαλάσω τὰ δίκτυα.*

- **"put out into the deep"** (ἐπανάγαγε εἰς τὸ βάθος, "lead up into the depth"): (?) for the deep in its good sense. The gathering is to go further in.
- **"through the whole night we laboured and took nothing"**: night is the absence of faith's light. Gathering by its own labour in the dark yields nothing.
- **"but at your word I will let down the nets"** (ἐπὶ τῷ ῥήματί σου): obedience to the word in place of one's own labour.

### 5:6–7 (grey)

*συνέκλεισαν πλῆθος ἰχθύων πολύ, διερρήσσετο δὲ τὰ δίκτυα… καὶ ἔπλησαν ἀμφότερα τὰ πλοῖα ὥστε βυθίζεσθαι αὐτά.*

- **"a great multitude of fish"**: fish are natural truths. At the word they come in a quantity the instruments cannot hold: the nets tear.
- **"they filled both boats, so that they began to sink"**: the knowledges are filled past what they can carry. *Filled (πίμπλημι) is a thread: in 4:28 the assembly was filled with rage; here the boats are filled with fish.*

### 5:8 (grey)

*ἰδὼν δὲ Σίμων Πέτρος προσέπεσεν τοῖς γόνασιν Ἰησοῦ λέγων· Ἔξελθε ἀπ’ ἐμοῦ, ὅτι ἀνὴρ ἁμαρτωλός εἰμι, κύριε·*

- **"Simon Peter"**: hearing and obedience (Simon), and faith (Peter).
- **"fell at the knees of Jesus"**: humiliation.
- **"Go out from me"** (Ἔξελθε ἀπ’ ἐμοῦ): the exact words Jesus spoke to the daimon (Ἔξελθε ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ, 4:35). Faith, seeing the Divine, puts itself where the falsity stood, and asks the Divine to go out from it, "for I am a sinful man".
- **The contrast with 4:34.** The falsity knew who the Divine was and was silenced and removed. Simon knows who he himself is, and is not removed.

### 5:9–11

*θάμβος γὰρ περιέσχεν αὐτὸν… Μὴ φοβοῦ· ἀπὸ τοῦ νῦν ἀνθρώπους ἔσῃ ζωγρῶν. καὶ καταγαγόντες τὰ πλοῖα ἐπὶ τὴν γῆν ἀφέντες πάντα ἠκολούθησαν αὐτῷ.*

- **"amazement seized him"**: θάμβος, the word of 4:36.
- **"James and John, partners with Simon"**: charity and its works joined to faith.
- **"Do not fear; from now on you will be taking people alive"** (ζωγρῶν): in place of gathering natural truths that die in the net, faith is to gather people, alive.
- **"bringing the boats down to land"** (καταγαγόντες): the knowledges are brought down and set on the ground. They are not taken further.
- **"leaving everything"** (ἀφέντες πάντα): *ἀφίημι, "let go". The same verb is used of sins forgiven in 5:20–24.*
- **"they followed him"**: the first following.

### 5:12–14

*ἀνὴρ πλήρης λέπρας… Κύριε, ἐὰν θέλῃς δύνασαί με καθαρίσαι. καὶ ἐκτείνας τὴν χεῖρα ἥψατο αὐτοῦ λέγων· Θέλω, καθαρίσθητι· καὶ εὐθέως ἡ λέπρα ἀπῆλθεν ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ.*

- **"a man full of leprosy"**: leprosy is profanation. "Full" (πλήρης) again, a man filled with it. *(?) Whether this is the Levitical rule that one covered wholly is clean (Leviticus 13:12–13), meaning the evil wholly brought out to the surface; not yet established in Swedenborg.*
- **"falling on his face"**: humiliation, as Simon's.
- **"if you will, you can cleanse me"**: the will of the Divine is asked, not demanded. Against Nazareth: "do here".
- **"stretching out his hand he touched him"**: hands are power; touch is communication.
- **"I will"**.
- **"the leprosy went away from him"** (ἀπῆλθεν ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ): as the daimon came out from the man, without harm to him.
- **"show yourself to the priest, and offer for your cleansing as Moses commanded, for a testimony"**: the priest is the good of the church; Moses is the Word. The cleansed state is brought back into the order of the Word, and becomes a witness. Nothing of the law is set aside.

### 5:15–16 (grey)

*διήρχετο δὲ μᾶλλον ὁ λόγος… αὐτὸς δὲ ἦν ὑποχωρῶν ἐν ταῖς ἐρήμοις καὶ προσευχόμενος.*

- **The word spreads; crowds come to hear and be healed.**
- **He withdraws to deserted places and prays**: as at 4:42, where nothing of the mind's own is present.

### 5:17–19 (5:19 grey)

*ἦσαν καθήμενοι Φαρισαῖοι καὶ νομοδιδάσκαλοι… ἐκ πάσης κώμης τῆς Γαλιλαίας καὶ Ἰουδαίας καὶ Ἰερουσαλήμ· καὶ δύναμις κυρίου ἦν εἰς τὸ ἰᾶσθαι αὐτόν. καὶ ἰδοὺ ἄνδρες φέροντες ἐπὶ κλίνης ἄνθρωπον ὃς ἦν παραλελυμένος… ἀναβάντες ἐπὶ τὸ δῶμα διὰ τῶν κεράμων καθῆκαν αὐτὸν σὺν τῷ κλινιδίῳ εἰς τὸ μέσον ἔμπροσθεν τοῦ Ἰησοῦ.*

- **"Pharisees and teachers of the law sitting"**: from every village of Galilee, Judea and Jerusalem, every level of the land. They are sitting.
- **"the power of the Lord was there to heal"**.
- **"men carrying on a bed a man who was paralysed"**: he cannot move himself, cannot act. He is carried by others; it is their faith (5:20). The bed: (?) Swedenborg reads the bed as doctrine, or the natural on which one rests; it is not fixed here.
- **"going up onto the roof"** (ἀναβάντες): the first going up in the text. It is done by the bearers, in order to let the man down.
- **"they let him down… into the midst before Jesus"** (καθῆκαν… εἰς τὸ μέσον): into the midst, the inmost, for the third time. At Capernaum the daimon threw the man into the midst (4:35); at Nazareth he passed through their midst (4:30). Here the man is let down into the midst, carefully, by others, to be placed before the Divine.

### 5:20–21

*καὶ ἰδὼν τὴν πίστιν αὐτῶν εἶπεν· Ἄνθρωπε, ἀφέωνταί σοι αἱ ἁμαρτίαι σου… τίς δύναται ἁμαρτίας ἀφεῖναι εἰ μὴ μόνος ὁ θεός;*

- **"seeing their faith"**: the bearers' faith, seen.
- **"Man, your sins are let go from you"** (ἀφέωνται): sins are evils; they are let go, removed. First the evil is removed, before any movement.
- **"Who can let sins go but God alone?"**: the scribes know correctly, as the daimon knew correctly (4:34). Their knowledge is right and does not receive.

### 5:22–23 (grey)

*ἐπιγνοὺς δὲ ὁ Ἰησοῦς τοὺς διαλογισμοὺς αὐτῶν… τί ἐστιν εὐκοπώτερον, εἰπεῖν· Ἀφέωνταί σοι… ἢ εἰπεῖν· Ἔγειρε καὶ περιπάτει;*

- **"Jesus, knowing their reasonings"**: in chapter 1 the falsities knew him; here he knows them.
- **"which is easier: 'your sins are let go', or 'rise and walk'?"**: the two are set side by side, the removal and the rising.

### 5:24–26 (5:25 grey)

*ἵνα δὲ εἰδῆτε ὅτι ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου ἐξουσίαν ἔχει ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς ἀφιέναι ἁμαρτίας… Σοὶ λέγω, ἔγειρε καὶ ἆρον τὸν κράβαττόν σου καὶ πορεύου εἰς τὸν οἶκόν σου. καὶ παραχρῆμα ἀναστὰς ἐνώπιον αὐτῶν… ἀπῆλθεν εἰς τὸν οἶκον αὐτοῦ δοξάζων τὸν θεόν… Εἴδομεν παράδοξα σήμερον.*

- **"so that you may know"**: the falsity's knowing was muzzled (4:35, 4:41); the scribes' knowing is answered.
- **"authority on earth to let sins go"**: the authority of the word (4:32, 4:36), now over sin.
- **"Rise"** (ἔγειρε): **this is the first rising given to a person.** In chapter 1 the only human rising was the own rising against the Divine (ἀναστάντες, 4:29). Here the man who could not move is told to rise, and "rising" (ἀναστὰς) he does. The rising comes after the sins are let go, and by the word.
- **"take up your bed"**: what carried him, he now carries.
- **"go to your house"** (πορεύου): the road verb, given to him. The house is his own mind. He goes home, not away.
- **"glorifying God… we have seen things beyond belief today"** (παράδοξα, "beyond opinion"): beyond what was held.

### 5:27–28 (5:28 grey)

*ἐθεάσατο τελώνην ὀνόματι Λευὶν καθήμενον ἐπὶ τὸ τελώνιον, καὶ εἶπεν αὐτῷ· Ἀκολούθει μοι. καὶ καταλιπὼν πάντα ἀναστὰς ἠκολούθει αὐτῷ.*

- **"a tax collector, sitting at the tax booth"**: one who lives by taking toll on what passes, sitting. The Pharisees were sitting too (5:17).
- **"Follow me"**.
- **"leaving everything, rising, he followed"** (καταλιπὼν πάντα ἀναστὰς ἠκολούθει): the second rising, again after a letting go. The one who sat takes up the road.

### 5:29–32 (5:29, 5:32 grey)

*Καὶ ἐποίησεν δοχὴν μεγάλην Λευὶς αὐτῷ ἐν τῇ οἰκίᾳ αὐτοῦ… Διὰ τί μετὰ τῶν τελωνῶν καὶ ἁμαρτωλῶν ἐσθίετε καὶ πίνετε;… Οὐ χρείαν ἔχουσιν οἱ ὑγιαίνοντες ἰατροῦ ἀλλὰ οἱ κακῶς ἔχοντες· οὐκ ἐλήλυθα καλέσαι δικαίους ἀλλὰ ἁμαρτωλοὺς εἰς μετάνοιαν.*

- **"Levi made a great reception (δοχή) for him in his house"**: δοχή is the noun of δέχομαι, "receive". At Nazareth no prophet is "received" (δεκτός, 4:24). Here a tax collector makes a great reception in his own house. The text uses the same word for refusal and for reception.
- **"eating and drinking with tax collectors and sinners"**: appropriation and conjunction, the first meal. Those who know they are sinners receive.
- **"Those who are well do not need a physician, but the sick"**: this answers Nazareth's "Physician, heal yourself" (4:23). The physician is not for himself, and not for those who think themselves well; he is for those who know they are ill.
- **"to call sinners to repentance"** (εἰς μετάνοιαν): the first mention of repentance in this text. The text ends with repentance proclaimed to all nations (24:47).

### 5:33–35

*Οἱ μαθηταὶ Ἰωάννου νηστεύουσιν πυκνὰ… οἱ δὲ σοὶ ἐσθίουσιν καὶ πίνουσιν… τοὺς υἱοὺς τοῦ νυμφῶνος ἐν ᾧ ὁ νυμφίος μετ’ αὐτῶν ἐστιν… ὅταν ἀπαρθῇ ἀπ’ αὐτῶν ὁ νυμφίος τότε νηστεύσουσιν*

- **"John's disciples and the Pharisees' fast and pray; yours eat and drink"**: John appears here for the first time in the text. He is known only through his disciples' fasting.
- **"the sons of the bridal chamber, while the bridegroom is with them"**: the wedding is the conjunction of good and truth; the bridegroom is the Divine. While he is present, there is no fasting.
- **"when the bridegroom is taken away (ἀπαρθῇ), they will fast"**: the first indication that he will be taken away.

### 5:36–38

*Οὐδεὶς ἐπίβλημα ἀπὸ ἱματίου καινοῦ σχίσας ἐπιβάλλει ἐπὶ ἱμάτιον παλαιόν… οὐδεὶς βάλλει οἶνον νέον εἰς ἀσκοὺς παλαιούς… ἀλλὰ οἶνον νέον εἰς ἀσκοὺς καινοὺς βλητέον.*

- **"No one tears a patch from a new garment to put on an old one"**: garments are the truths that clothe the mind. A piece of the new torn off to mend the old ruins the new and does not match the old.
- **"new wine into old skins"**: wine is truth from good; skins are the vessels that hold it, the knowledges of the natural. New wine bursts old vessels; it is lost and they are lost.
- **"new wine must be put into new skins"**: what has been received cannot be added to what the mind already was. The vessel itself must be new.
- **The chapter's movements read this way.** The boats sank under the catch (5:7); the patch, the old garment and the old skins cannot take the new; the one who was carried now carries his bed. The text has already shown vessels that could not hold what came.

### Test: 5:39 (U−)

*καὶ οὐδεὶς πιὼν παλαιὸν θέλει νέον· λέγει γάρ· Ὁ παλαιὸς χρηστός ἐστιν.*

- **"No one who has drunk old wine wants new; he says, 'the old is good'"**: the mind settled in the old truths prefers what it has. This is Nazareth's position in one sentence.

**Verdict: continues.** It completes the saying.

### 6:1–5

*Ἐγένετο δὲ ἐν σαββάτῳ διαπορεύεσθαι αὐτὸν διὰ σπορίμων, καὶ ἔτιλλον οἱ μαθηταὶ αὐτοῦ καὶ ἤσθιον τοὺς στάχυας ψώχοντες ταῖς χερσίν… ὡς εἰσῆλθεν εἰς τὸν οἶκον τοῦ θεοῦ καὶ τοὺς ἄρτους τῆς προθέσεως λαβὼν ἔφαγεν καὶ ἔδωκεν… Κύριός ἐστιν ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου καὶ τοῦ σαββάτου.*

- **"on a sabbath he was going through the sown fields"** (διαπορεύεσθαι): the road again, now through what has been sown.
- **"the disciples plucked the ears and ate, rubbing them in their hands"**: grain is good, the stuff of bread. They take it with their own hands and eat: appropriation of good, in the state of rest.
- **"What is not lawful on the sabbaths"**.
- **"David… entered the house of God, took the bread of the presence, ate and gave to those with him"**: the bread set before God, holy good, given to the hungry. What is given is passed on.
- **"The son of man is lord of the sabbath"**: the state of rest and conjunction belongs to the Divine, not to the rule that guards it.

### 6:6–7

*εἰσελθεῖν αὐτὸν εἰς τὴν συναγωγὴν καὶ διδάσκειν· καὶ ἦν ἄνθρωπος ἐκεῖ καὶ ἡ χεὶρ αὐτοῦ ἡ δεξιὰ ἦν ξηρά· παρετηροῦντο δὲ οἱ γραμματεῖς… ἵνα εὕρωσιν κατηγορεῖν αὐτοῦ.*

- **"on another sabbath he entered the assembly and taught; a man was there"**: the same setting as the first scene of the text (4:31–33): a sabbath, the assembly, teaching, and a man.
- **"his right hand was dry"**: the right hand is power from good; dry, without life. He has no power to act. At 4:33 the man was held by a falsity; this man is lacking.
- **"they watched to find how to accuse him"**.

### 6:8 (grey)

*αὐτὸς δὲ ᾔδει τοὺς διαλογισμοὺς αὐτῶν, εἶπεν δὲ τῷ ἀνδρὶ… Ἔγειρε καὶ στῆθι εἰς τὸ μέσον· καὶ ἀναστὰς ἔστη.*

- **"he knew their reasonings"**: as at 5:22.
- **"Rise and stand into the midst"** (Ἔγειρε καὶ στῆθι εἰς τὸ μέσον): the midst for the fourth time, and now the man stands in it. In the first synagogue (4:35) the man was thrown down into the midst by the falsity; at the house (5:19) the paralysed man was let down into the midst by others; here the man rises and stands in the midst himself.
- **"and rising, he stood"** (ἀναστὰς ἔστη): the third rising given to a person.

### 6:9

*Ἐπερωτῶ ὑμᾶς, εἰ ἔξεστιν τῷ σαββάτῳ ἀγαθοποιῆσαι ἢ κακοποιῆσαι, ψυχὴν σῶσαι ἢ ἀπολέσαι;*

- **"to save a soul or to destroy it"** (ἀπολέσαι): the daimon's word. "Have you come to destroy (ἀπολέσαι) us?" (4:34). The falsity feared destruction and was right; the soul is to be saved. What the Divine destroys is the falsity, never the soul.

### 6:10–11 (grey)

*καὶ περιβλεψάμενος πάντας αὐτοὺς εἶπεν αὐτῷ· Ἔκτεινον τὴν χεῖρά σου· ὁ δὲ ἐποίησεν, καὶ ἀπεκατεστάθη ἡ χεὶρ αὐτοῦ. αὐτοὶ δὲ ἐπλήσθησαν ἀνοίας…*

- **"Stretch out your hand"** (Ἔκτεινον τὴν χεῖρά σου): the gesture Jesus made to cleanse the leper (ἐκτείνας τὴν χεῖρα, 5:13). Now the man is told to make it himself.
- **"he did so, and his hand was restored"**: the power that was dry is given back. The man acts.
- **"they were filled with folly"** (ἀνοίας, "without mind"): filled again. The assembly at Nazareth was filled with rage (4:28); this one with folly.

### What came out of the reading of chapter 2

**Rising begins, and it begins as a gift.** In chapter 1 the only person who rose was the own, rising against the Divine (4:29). In this chapter three people rise:
- the paralysed man, after his sins are let go (5:24–25);
- Levi, after leaving everything (5:28);
- the man with the dry hand, into the midst (6:8).

Each rising comes after a letting go, and by the word, never of the man's own accord.

**The sequence of the midst.**
- Thrown down into it by a falsity (4:35).
- Let down into it by others' faith (5:19).
- Standing up in it oneself (6:8).

The midst is the place of the inmost, and the person comes to stand there.

**Letting go (ἀφίημι).** "Leaving everything" (5:11) and "your sins are let go" (5:20–24) are one verb. What is let go of is both the evil and everything one held.

**Reception (δέχομαι).** Nazareth does not receive (δεκτός, 4:24); Levi makes a great reception (δοχή, 5:29).

**Knowing.** The falsities knew him and were muzzled (chapter 1). The scribes know correctly and are answered (5:21, 5:24). Simon knows himself (5:8) and is called.

**The physician.** Nazareth demanded that the physician heal himself (4:23). Here the physician is for those who know they are ill (5:31).

**Filling.** With rage (4:28), with fish (5:7), with leprosy (5:12), with fear (5:26), with folly (6:11).

**The vessel.** Nothing new can be put into the old: the sinking boats, the torn nets, the old garment and the old skins.

**What is ascending.** It is beginning to show. The person, cast down by falsity, is brought to stand at the centre. He rises only after what holds him (sin, possessions) is let go, and only at the word. The own rose too, in chapter 1, but against the Divine; here the rising is received.

**Test: 4:38–39 (U?), read again by its words.** *ἐπιστὰς ἐπάνω αὐτῆς ἐπετίμησεν τῷ πυρετῷ, καὶ ἀφῆκεν αὐτήν· παραχρῆμα δὲ ἀναστᾶσα διηκόνει αὐτοῖς* ("standing over her, he rebuked the fever, and it let her go; at once rising, she served them").
- "Rebuked" (ἐπετίμησεν) is the verb used of the daimon (4:35).
- "Let her go" (ἀφῆκεν) is the verb of letting go (5:11, 5:20).
- "Rising" (ἀναστᾶσα) is the rising verb.

At its place, after 4:37, it would be the first rising of a person in the text, by a woman (an affection), into service. **Verdict: continues.** Every one of its verbs belongs to the threads, and it would open the line that chapter 2 develops.

### Threads to carry forward

- rising (ἀνίστημι, ἐγείρω);
- the midst (μέσον);
- letting go (ἀφίημι);
- reception (δέχομαι);
- knowing;
- filling;
- the vessel, new and old;
- the road (πορεύομαι);
- the meal (eating and drinking);
- the bridegroom "taken away";
- repentance (μετάνοια);
- the sabbath and the assembly;
- hands, stretched out.

## Chapter 3

### 6:12

*ἐξελθεῖν αὐτὸν εἰς τὸ ὄρος προσεύξασθαι, καὶ ἦν διανυκτερεύων ἐν τῇ προσευχῇ τοῦ θεοῦ.*

- **"he went out to the mountain"** (εἰς τὸ ὄρος): the same word as the hill at Nazareth on which "their city was built" (ὄρους, 4:29). There the own's mountain was the edge from which the Divine was to be thrown down. Here he goes up the mountain himself. A mountain is love; this is the first upward movement of Jesus in the text.
- **"to pray"**: conjunction with the Divine.
- **"he spent the whole night in the prayer of God"** (διανυκτερεύων): night again. Simon "laboured the whole night and took nothing" (5:5); here the whole night is spent in prayer. The night of one's own labour and the night of prayer are set against each other.

### 6:13–16 (6:15 grey)

*καὶ ὅτε ἐγένετο ἡμέρα, προσεφώνησεν τοὺς μαθητὰς… ἐκλεξάμενος ἀπ’ αὐτῶν δώδεκα, οὓς καὶ ἀποστόλους ὠνόμασεν, Σίμωνα ὃν καὶ ὠνόμασεν Πέτρον… καὶ Ἰούδαν Ἰσκαριὼθ ὃς ἐγένετο προδότης.*

- **"when day came"**: a new state, out of the night of prayer.
- **"he chose twelve"**: twelve is all truths and goods together. What the night of prayer produces is order: the whole set of principles chosen and called around him.
- **"whom he also named apostles"** (ἀποστόλους, "sent ones"): named with his own word. He was "sent" (ἀπεστάλην, 4:43); now he names others as sent.
- **"Simon, whom he also named Peter"** (Πέτρον): a name is a quality. Hearing and obedience (Simon) is given the name of the rock (πέτρος); Peter is faith. *Thread: the chapter ends with a house founded on the rock (πέτραν, 6:48).*
- **"Judas Iscariot, who became a traitor"**: within the chosen order, from the start, the one who will hand him over.

### 6:17–19 (6:18 grey)

*Καὶ καταβὰς μετ’ αὐτῶν ἔστη ἐπὶ τόπου πεδινοῦ… οἳ ἦλθον ἀκοῦσαι αὐτοῦ καὶ ἰαθῆναι… καὶ πᾶς ὁ ὄχλος ἐζήτει ἅπτεσθαι αὐτοῦ, ὅτι δύναμις παρ’ αὐτοῦ ἐξήρχετο καὶ ἰᾶτο πάντας.*

- **"coming down with them"** (καταβὰς): descent again. Down (κατ-) is now the movement of the Divine with the twelve. He went up alone; he comes down with all the principles.
- **"he stood on a level place"** (ἔστη): he stands, as the man with the restored hand stood (ἔστη, 6:8). A level place: the plain, the natural, where the work is done.
- **"from all Judea and Jerusalem, and the coast of Tyre and Sidon"**: the celestial, the church as to doctrine, and the coast of the nations outside. Every part comes.
- **"to hear and to be healed"**: in this order.
- **"the whole crowd sought to touch him"**: in chapter 1 the crowds held him back (4:42); here they seek to touch him, which is communication.
- **"for power went out from him"** (δύναμις… ἐξήρχετο): the verb of coming out once more. Falsities came out of people (4:35, 4:41); now power comes out of him. What comes out of the person is evil; what comes out of the Divine is power.

### 6:20

*Καὶ αὐτὸς ἐπάρας τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς αὐτοῦ εἰς τοὺς μαθητὰς αὐτοῦ ἔλεγεν· Μακάριοι οἱ πτωχοί, ὅτι αὐτῶν ἐστιν ἡ βασιλεία τοῦ θεοῦ.*

- **"lifting up his eyes to his disciples"** (ἐπάρας τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς): the first lifting (ἐπαίρω) in the text, of the eyes, which are understanding. He lifts them toward the disciples, not toward heaven. *Thread: lifting the eyes or the head; to be followed.*
- **"Blessed are the poor, for theirs is the kingdom of God"**: the poor are those who know they have nothing of truth of their own. The kingdom, set against the rulers of 3:1, is theirs, not as a reward to come but in the present tense: "is".

### 6:21

*μακάριοι οἱ πεινῶντες νῦν, ὅτι χορτασθήσεσθε. μακάριοι οἱ κλαίοντες νῦν, ὅτι γελάσετε.*

- **"blessed are you who hunger now, for you will be filled"** (χορτασθήσεσθε, "fed to fullness"): hunger is the desire for good; to be fed to fullness is to receive it. *Thread: χορτάζω returns at the feeding, "they all ate and were filled" (9:17).*
- **"weep now… laugh"**.

### 6:22–23

*ὅταν μισήσωσιν ὑμᾶς… καὶ ἐκβάλωσιν τὸ ὄνομα ὑμῶν ὡς πονηρὸν… κατὰ τὰ αὐτὰ γὰρ ἐποίουν τοῖς προφήταις οἱ πατέρες αὐτῶν.*

- **"when they cast out your name as evil"** (ἐκβάλωσιν): Nazareth "cast him out" (ἐξέβαλον, 4:29). What was done to him will be done to the quality (name) of those who are his.
- **"so their fathers did to the prophets"**: the prophet not received in his fatherland (4:24). The fathers are the own's line.
- **"leap for joy… your reward is great in heaven"**.

### 6:24–26

*πλὴν οὐαὶ ὑμῖν τοῖς πλουσίοις, ὅτι ἀπέχετε τὴν παράκλησιν ὑμῶν. οὐαὶ ὑμῖν, οἱ ἐμπεπλησμένοι νῦν, ὅτι πεινάσετε…*

- **"woe to you rich, for you have received your consolation"** (ἀπέχετε): the rich are full of what they claim as their own; they already have, and so cannot receive. As Nazareth already "had" him as its own and could not receive him.
- **"woe to you who are filled now"** (ἐμπεπλησμένοι): the filling thread turns here. Until now the text showed people filled with rage, with leprosy, with fear, with folly (4:28; 5:12; 5:26; 6:11). Now the principle is stated: to be filled now is the woe, and to be empty now is the condition of being fed.
- **"when all speak well of you"**: false prophets, received by everyone.

### 6:27–31

*Ἀλλὰ ὑμῖν λέγω τοῖς ἀκούουσιν, ἀγαπᾶτε τοὺς ἐχθροὺς ὑμῶν… τῷ τύπτοντί σε ἐπὶ τὴν σιαγόνα πάρεχε καὶ τὴν ἄλλην, καὶ ἀπὸ τοῦ αἴροντός σου τὸ ἱμάτιον καὶ τὸν χιτῶνα μὴ κωλύσῃς. παντὶ αἰτοῦντί σε δίδου, καὶ ἀπὸ τοῦ αἴροντος τὰ σὰ μὴ ἀπαίτει.*

- **"to you who hear I say"**: the hearers, the condition of the whole sermon.
- **"love your enemies… bless those who curse you"**: love that does not depend on what comes back.
- **"from the one who takes your garment, do not withhold your tunic"** (ἱμάτιον): the garment of 5:36, the truths that clothe the mind. What clothes the mind is not to be held against the one who takes it. The outer truths are not one's own property.
- **"from the one who takes what is yours, do not ask it back"** (τὰ σά, "your things"): what is "yours". Nazareth said "your fatherland" (4:23) and claimed him; here "your things" are to be let go without return. *Thread: letting go (5:11, 5:20).*

### 6:32–35 (6:32–33 grey)

*ποία ὑμῖν χάρις ἐστίν;… πλὴν ἀγαπᾶτε τοὺς ἐχθροὺς ὑμῶν καὶ ἀγαθοποιεῖτε καὶ δανίζετε μηδὲν ἀπελπίζοντες· καὶ ἔσται ὁ μισθὸς ὑμῶν πολύς, καὶ ἔσεσθε υἱοὶ Ὑψίστου, ὅτι αὐτὸς χρηστός ἐστιν ἐπὶ τοὺς ἀχαρίστους καὶ πονηρούς.*

- **"what grace (χάρις) is that to you?"**: love that returns what it receives is no grace. Sinners do the same.
- **"lend, expecting nothing back"**.
- **"and you will be sons of the Most High"** (υἱοὶ Ὑψίστου): **the first time the text names the height.** It names it as a sonship, not a place. Its condition is giving without return. *Thread: the Most High (8:28; 1:32 is absent).*
- **"for he is kind (χρηστός) to the ungrateful and the evil"**: χρηστός, the word of the left-out saying "the old is good (χρηστός)" (5:39). In that saying the mind settled in the old calls its old wine χρηστός; here the Most High is χρηστός to those without grace (ἀχαρίστους). The two sentences answer each other across one paragraph. This bears on the test of 5:39: the word joins it to its context.

### 6:36–38

*γίνεσθε οἰκτίρμονες καθὼς ὁ πατὴρ ὑμῶν… ἀπολύετε, καὶ ἀπολυθήσεσθε· δίδοτε, καὶ δοθήσεται ὑμῖν· μέτρον καλὸν πεπιεσμένον σεσαλευμένον ὑπερεκχυννόμενον δώσουσιν εἰς τὸν κόλπον ὑμῶν· τῷ γὰρ αὐτῷ μέτρῳ ᾧ μετρεῖτε ἀντιμετρηθήσεται ὑμῖν.*

- **"be merciful, as your Father is merciful"**: the Father named for the first time in the text, as the measure of mercy.
- **"release, and you will be released"** (ἀπολύετε): letting go, in another verb.
- **"give, and it will be given to you"**: what is passed on is received.
- **"a good measure, pressed down, shaken, running over"** (ὑπερεκχυννόμενον, "poured out over"): the verb of the new wine that is "poured out" and lost from old skins (ἐκχυθήσεται, 5:37). The same pouring is loss when the vessel holds on, and fullness when the vessel gives.
- **"into your bosom"** (εἰς τὸν κόλπον): *thread: Lazarus is carried into Abraham's bosom (κόλπον, 16:22–23).*
- **"with the measure you measure it will be measured back"**: reception is in the measure of what is given. This states the law of the vessel.

### 6:39–42

*Μήτι δύναται τυφλὸς τυφλὸν ὁδηγεῖν; οὐχὶ ἀμφότεροι εἰς βόθυνον ἐμπεσοῦνται;… Ἀδελφέ, ἄφες ἐκβάλω τὸ κάρφος τὸ ἐν τῷ ὀφθαλμῷ σου… ὑποκριτά, ἔκβαλε πρῶτον τὴν δοκὸν ἐκ τοῦ ὀφθαλμοῦ σου, καὶ τότε διαβλέψεις…*

- **"can a blind man lead a blind man? Will they not both fall into a pit?"**: the eyes are understanding, and blindness is not seeing truth. To fall into a pit is the downward movement that Nazareth intended for him (4:29), now befalling those who lead without sight.
- **"the disciple is not above the teacher; fully trained, he will be like his teacher"**.
- **"the speck in your brother's eye, the beam in your own"** (τῷ ἰδίῳ ὀφθαλμῷ): "own" (ἴδιος) again.
- **"let me cast out the speck… first cast out the beam from your own eye"** (ἐκβάλω… ἔκβαλε): the verb of casting out.
  - Nazareth cast the Divine out of its city (4:29).
  - Those who are his will have their name cast out (6:22).
  - Here the casting out is turned inward, on one's own eye.
  - What the Divine did in the assembly (taking a falsity out of a man) is now asked of the disciple toward himself, first, before his brother.
- **"then you will see clearly"** (διαβλέψεις): sight follows the removal.

### 6:43–45 (6:44 grey)

*Οὐ γάρ ἐστιν δένδρον καλὸν ποιοῦν καρπὸν σαπρόν… ἕκαστον γὰρ δένδρον ἐκ τοῦ ἰδίου καρποῦ γινώσκεται… ὁ ἀγαθὸς ἄνθρωπος ἐκ τοῦ ἀγαθοῦ θησαυροῦ τῆς καρδίας προφέρει τὸ ἀγαθόν… ἐκ γὰρ περισσεύματος καρδίας λαλεῖ τὸ στόμα αὐτοῦ.*

- **"a good tree does not bear rotten fruit"**: a tree is a person; its fruit is works.
- **"each tree is known by its own fruit"**: knowing, now from the works. The falsities knew him by name (4:34); a tree is known by its fruit. Knowledge from works replaces knowledge from words.
- **"figs from thorns… grapes from brambles"**: natural good (figs) and spiritual good (grapes) do not come from what is born of cares and lusts (thorns).
- **"from the good treasure of the heart… from the overflow of the heart the mouth speaks"** (περισσεύματος): the overflow, as the measure "running over" (6:38). What comes out is what fills.

### 6:46–49 (6:47–49 grey)

*Τί δέ με καλεῖτε· Κύριε κύριε, καὶ οὐ ποιεῖτε ἃ λέγω; πᾶς ὁ ἐρχόμενος πρός με καὶ ἀκούων μου τῶν λόγων καὶ ποιῶν αὐτούς… ὅμοιός ἐστιν ἀνθρώπῳ οἰκοδομοῦντι οἰκίαν ὃς ἔσκαψεν καὶ ἐβάθυνεν καὶ ἔθηκεν θεμέλιον ἐπὶ τὴν πέτραν· πλημμύρης δὲ γενομένης προσέρηξεν ὁ ποταμὸς τῇ οἰκίᾳ ἐκείνῃ, καὶ οὐκ ἴσχυσεν σαλεῦσαι αὐτὴν… ὁ δὲ ἀκούσας καὶ μὴ ποιήσας… οἰκοδομήσαντι οἰκίαν ἐπὶ τὴν γῆν χωρὶς θεμελίου, ᾗ προσέρηξεν ὁ ποταμός, καὶ εὐθὺς συνέπεσεν, καὶ ἐγένετο τὸ ῥῆγμα τῆς οἰκίας ἐκείνης μέγα.*

- **"why do you call me 'Lord, Lord', and do not do what I say?"**: naming without doing. The falsities named him correctly (4:34, 4:41) and were silenced.
- **"coming to me, hearing my words and doing them"**: coming, hearing, doing. Three steps.
- **"a man building a house"**: a house is the mind.
- **"he dug and went deep"** (ἐβάθυνεν): the deep of 5:4 ("put out into the deep", εἰς τὸ βάθος). The catch came from going into the deep at the word; the house stands by going deep to the rock. Before anything rises, it goes down.
- **"and laid the foundation on the rock"** (ἐπὶ τὴν πέτραν): the rock is the name given to Simon at the start of this chapter (Πέτρον, 6:14). The rock is faith, from the Divine. The chapter is bounded by the naming of the rock and the foundation on the rock.
- **"a flood came, the river burst against that house, and could not shake it"**: a flood or river is temptation. "Shake" (σαλεῦσαι) is the word of the measure "shaken" (σεσαλευμένον, 6:38). The measure that is given is shaken down to hold more; the house that is founded is not shaken.
- **"because it was well built"**.
- **"the one who heard and did not do… built on the ground, without foundation"**: the ground is the natural alone, with nothing beneath.
- **"the river burst against it, and at once it fell, and the ruin (ῥῆγμα) of that house was great"**: ῥήγνυμι, "burst", the verb of:
  - the nets "breaking" (διερρήσσετο, 5:6);
  - the new wine that "bursts" (ῥήξει) old skins (5:37);
  - the river that "burst against" (προσέρηξεν) the house.
- **What the bursting shows.** What cannot hold, bursts. The torn nets, the burst skins and the ruined house are one image across three passages: a vessel that has no ground to receive what comes.

### What came out of the reading of chapter 3

**The first ascent in the text is the Divine's own.** He goes up the mountain, the word used for the own's hill at Nazareth, and prays through the night. At day he chooses and names the twelve and gives Simon the name of the rock. Then he comes down with them and stands on the level ground, and power comes out from him. Going up is for prayer; coming down is with the order he has chosen.

**The height is named, as sonship.** "You will be sons of the Most High" (6:35). The way to it is stated inside the sentence: love enemies, do good, lend expecting nothing. The height is reached by giving without return.

**The laws of the vessel are stated.**
- To be filled now is the woe; to be empty now is the condition of being filled (6:21, 6:25).
- What is given is poured back over into the bosom (6:38), the same verb as the wine lost from old skins (5:37).
- What cannot hold, bursts: the nets, the skins, the house (5:6; 5:37; 6:49).
- The measure given is shaken down to hold more; the founded house is not shaken (6:38; 6:48).

**Down before up.** The catch came from the deep (5:4). The house stands because it was dug deep to the rock (6:48). The rock is the name given to faith at the chapter's start.

**Casting out turns inward.** The own cast the Divine out (4:29); the disciple must cast out the beam from his own eye first (6:42). The removal that the Divine performs in the assembly becomes the disciple's own act toward himself, and sight follows (6:42).

**Knowing.** It now comes from fruit (6:44), not from naming (6:46).

**The test of 5:39 is confirmed by a word.** The own calls its old wine χρηστός (5:39); the Most High is χρηστός to the ungrateful (6:35).

### 7:2–5 (7:3–5 grey)

Nazareth's city was built on the hill (4:29); the wise man built his house on the rock (6:48); this ruler from outside built the assembly itself.

### 7:6–8 (grey)

*ὁ δὲ Ἰησοῦς ἐπορεύετο σὺν αὐτοῖς… Κύριε, μὴ σκύλλου, οὐ γὰρ ἱκανός εἰμι ἵνα ὑπὸ τὴν στέγην μου εἰσέλθῃς· διὸ οὐδὲ ἐμαυτὸν ἠξίωσα πρὸς σὲ ἐλθεῖν· ἀλλὰ εἰπὲ λόγῳ… καὶ γὰρ ἐγὼ ἄνθρωπός εἰμι ὑπὸ ἐξουσίαν τασσόμενος… καὶ λέγω τούτῳ· Πορεύθητι, καὶ πορεύεται, καὶ ἄλλῳ· Ἔρχου, καὶ ἔρχεται, καὶ τῷ δούλῳ μου· Ποίησον τοῦτο, καὶ ποιεῖ.*

- **"Jesus went with them"** (ἐπορεύετο): the road goes toward the outsider's house.

- **"therefore I did not count myself worthy (ἠξίωσα) to come to you"**: the elders said he was worthy (ἄξιος); he does not count himself so. The worth is given by others; he takes none of it. As Simon: "I am a sinful man" (5:8).

- **"I say to one 'Go', and he goes; to another 'Come', and he comes; to my slave 'Do this', and he does it"**: going, coming, doing. The sermon ended with "coming to me, hearing my words and doing them" (6:47). The order of obedience that the centurion commands is the order the sermon asked for.

### 7:9–10 (7:10 grey)

*ἐθαύμασεν αὐτόν, καὶ στραφεὶς τῷ ἀκολουθοῦντι αὐτῷ ὄχλῳ εἶπεν· Λέγω ὑμῖν, τοσαύτην πίστιν οὐδὲ ἐν τῷ Ἰσραὴλ εὗρον. καὶ ὑποστρέψαντες εἰς τὸν οἶκον οἱ πεμφθέντες εὗρον τὸν δοῦλον ὑγιαίνοντα.*

- **"he marvelled at him"**: the only time in the text so far that the Divine marvels. Until now others marvelled at him.

- **"not even in Israel have I found such faith"**: faith is found outside.
- **"those sent, turning back to the house"** (ὑποστρέψαντες): *thread: the Samaritan leper "turned back" (ὑπέστρεψεν, 17:15).*

### 7:11–15 (7:11, 7:13 grey)

*ἐπορεύθη εἰς πόλιν καλουμένην Ναΐν… ὡς δὲ ἤγγισεν τῇ πύλῃ τῆς πόλεως, καὶ ἰδοὺ ἐξεκομίζετο τεθνηκὼς μονογενὴς υἱὸς τῇ μητρὶ αὐτοῦ, καὶ αὐτὴ ἦν χήρα… καὶ ἰδὼν αὐτὴν ὁ κύριος ἐσπλαγχνίσθη ἐπ’ αὐτῇ καὶ εἶπεν αὐτῇ· Μὴ κλαῖε. καὶ προσελθὼν ἥψατο τῆς σοροῦ, οἱ δὲ βαστάζοντες ἔστησαν, καὶ εἶπεν· Νεανίσκε, σοὶ λέγω, ἐγέρθητι. καὶ ἀνεκάθισεν ὁ νεκρὸς καὶ ἤρξατο λαλεῖν, καὶ ἔδωκεν αὐτὸν τῇ μητρὶ αὐτοῦ.*

- **"as he came near the gate of the city"**: a gate is the way in. He comes to the way in.
- **"a dead man was being carried out"** (ἐξεκομίζετο): carried out, as he is coming in. The two movements meet at the gate.
- **"the only son (μονογενής) of his mother, and she was a widow"**: a widow is good without truth, longing for it; a son is truth. Her only truth has died and is being carried out of the city. *Thread: the only child (an only daughter, 8:42; an only son, 9:38).*
- **"the Lord, seeing her, was moved in his inward parts"** (ἐσπλαγχνίσθη).
- **"Do not weep"** (μὴ κλαῖε): "blessed are you who weep now, for you will laugh" (6:21). *Thread: weeping (κλαίω).*
- **"he touched the bier, and the bearers stood still"** (ἔστησαν): touch is communication. Those carrying death out stand.
- **"Young man, I say to you, be raised"** (ἐγέρθητι): **rising has reached the dead.**
  - The own rose against him (4:29).
  - The one who could not move was told to rise (5:24); the one who sat rose (5:28); the man with no power rose and stood (6:8).

- **"the dead sat up and began to speak"**: the dead speaks. The falsity that spoke was muzzled (4:35); the raised truth speaks.
- **"he gave him to his mother"**: truth given back to the good it belongs to.

  - "the deaf hear";
  - "the dead are raised" (ἐγείρονται; 7:14);

- **"blessed is whoever is not made to stumble in me"** (σκανδαλισθῇ).

- **"I send my messenger before your face, who will prepare your way (ὁδόν) before you"**: **the way is named.** The road (πορεύομαι) has been walked since 4:30. Here it is called ὁδός, and it is prepared by John, the letter of the Word, ahead of the Divine.
- **"none born of women is greater than John; yet the least in the kingdom of God is greater than he"**: the letter is the greatest of what is born naturally. The least state of the kingdom within is greater. *Thread: greatest and least (9:46–48).*

### 7:36–38

*Ἠρώτα δέ τις αὐτὸν τῶν Φαρισαίων ἵνα φάγῃ μετ’ αὐτοῦ… καὶ ἰδοὺ γυνὴ ἥτις ἦν ἐν τῇ πόλει ἁμαρτωλός, καὶ ἐπιγνοῦσα ὅτι κατάκειται ἐν τῇ οἰκίᾳ τοῦ Φαρισαίου, κομίσασα ἀλάβαστρον μύρου καὶ στᾶσα ὀπίσω παρὰ τοὺς πόδας αὐτοῦ κλαίουσα, τοῖς δάκρυσιν ἤρξατο βρέχειν τοὺς πόδας αὐτοῦ καὶ ταῖς θριξὶν τῆς κεφαλῆς αὐτῆς ἐξέμασσεν, καὶ κατεφίλει τοὺς πόδας αὐτοῦ καὶ ἤλειφεν τῷ μύρῳ.*

### 7:39–43 (grey)

*Οὗτος εἰ ἦν προφήτης, ἐγίνωσκεν ἂν τίς καὶ ποταπὴ ἡ γυνὴ ἥτις ἅπτεται αὐτοῦ… Σίμων, ἔχω σοί τι εἰπεῖν… δύο χρεοφειλέται ἦσαν δανιστῇ τινι… μὴ ἐχόντων αὐτῶν ἀποδοῦναι ἀμφοτέροις ἐχαρίσατο. τίς οὖν αὐτῶν πλεῖον ἀγαπήσει αὐτόν;… Ὀρθῶς ἔκρινας.*

- **"if this man were a prophet, he would know who and what kind of woman this is who is touching him"**: the Pharisee reasons about knowing. In chapter 2 Jesus knew the Pharisees' reasonings (5:22; 6:8); this one reasons that a prophet would know. He does.
- **"who is touching him"**: the crowd sought to touch him (6:19). Here it is a sinner who touches him.
- **"Simon"**: the Pharisee is named Simon, the name of hearing, like Simon who was named Peter (6:14). Two Simons: one who heard and became the rock, and one who hears and is now told something.
- **"a lender had two debtors"** (δανιστῇ): lending, as in "lend, expecting nothing back" (6:35).
- **"since they had nothing to repay, he graced both"** (ἐχαρίσατο): grace, the word of the sight "granted" to the blind (7:21). The Most High is kind to the ungrateful (6:35).
- **"which will love him more?"… "You have judged rightly"** (ἔκρινας): "do not judge" (6:37); here a judgement asked for and found right.

### 7:44–46

*καὶ στραφεὶς πρὸς τὴν γυναῖκα… εἰσῆλθόν σου εἰς τὴν οἰκίαν, ὕδωρ μοι ἐπὶ πόδας οὐκ ἔδωκας· αὕτη δὲ τοῖς δάκρυσιν ἔβρεξέν μου τοὺς πόδας… φίλημά μοι οὐκ ἔδωκας· αὕτη δὲ… οὐ διέλιπεν καταφιλοῦσά μου τοὺς πόδας. ἐλαίῳ τὴν κεφαλήν μου οὐκ ἤλειψας· αὕτη δὲ μύρῳ ἤλειψεν τοὺς πόδας μου.*

- **"turning to the woman"** (στραφεὶς): he turned to the crowd at the centurion's faith (7:9); here he turns to the woman.

  - a kiss; she gave kisses without ceasing (conjunction);
  - oil for the head; she gave ointment for the feet (oil is the good of love).
- **The host's gifts would have been for the head; hers are all for the feet.** Truth, conjunction and good, given by the one who knows herself sinful, from her own body, to the lowest of him.

### 7:47–50 (7:49 grey)

*οὗ χάριν, λέγω σοι, ἀφέωνται αἱ ἁμαρτίαι αὐτῆς αἱ πολλαί, ὅτι ἠγάπησεν πολύ· ᾧ δὲ ὀλίγον ἀφίεται, ὀλίγον ἀγαπᾷ… Ἀφέωνταί σου αἱ ἁμαρτίαι… Τίς οὗτός ἐστιν ὃς καὶ ἁμαρτίας ἀφίησιν;… Ἡ πίστις σου σέσωκέν σε· πορεύου εἰς εἰρήνην.*

- **"for which reason (οὗ χάριν)"**: χάρις again.
- **"her many sins are let go, because she loved much; the one to whom little is let go loves little"** (ἀφέωνται): letting go (5:11; 5:20). The measure of 6:38 stated as love: as much as is let go, so much love. The Pharisee, who thinks he has little to be let go, loves little.
- **"Who is this who even lets sins go?"**: as at 5:21.
- **"Your faith has saved you"** (σέσωκεν): saving (6:9; 7:3).
- **"go into peace"** (πορεύου): the road verb, given to her, as it was given to the paralysed man ("go to your house", 5:24).

### What came out of the reading of chapter 4

**Rising reaches the dead, and the verb becomes the prophet's word.**
- The widow's only son, being carried out at the gate as the Divine comes in, is told "be raised" (7:14).
- The people say "a great prophet has been raised among us" (7:16), the verb the angels will use at the tomb (24:6).
- The sequence of rising is now: the own against him (4:29); the one who cannot move, the one who sits, the one with no power (5–6); the dead (7:14).

**Two outsiders receive what the insiders do not, each by counting themselves unworthy.**
- The centurion is called worthy by others and does not count himself so (7:4, 7:7). He knows that he is under authority, so he knows the word. His faith is greater than Israel's.
- The woman knows herself a sinner. She gives truth, conjunction and good to the feet, where the host gave nothing to the head.
- Both are measured by 6:38: love in the measure of what is let go (7:47).

**The way is named, and the one who prepares it.** "He will prepare your way (ὁδόν)" (7:27). The letter of the Word (John) is the greatest of the natural, and the least of the kingdom is greater.

**Sight as rising.** "The blind look up" (ἀναβλέπουσιν, 7:22).

**Grace (χαρίζομαι).** Sight given as grace (7:21); debts given as grace (7:42–43).

**The road is given to people, not only walked by him.** "Go" to the centurion's slave, by the centurion's own word (7:8); "go into peace" to the woman (7:50).

**Filling.** The hearing is filled with the word (7:1).

**The test of 7:29–35.** Its words take up 5:30–34 and the threads of weeping, sitting, friends and justifying. It continues.

### Threads, carried forward and added

- rising (now: the dead raised; the prophet raised);
- looking up (ἀναβλέπω);
- the way (ὁδός) prepared;
- greatest and least;
- worth (ἄξιος) given by others and not taken;
- grace (χάρις, χαρίζομαι);
- turning (στρέφω, ὑποστρέφω);
- the feet;
- the only child;
- weeping;
- saving: "your faith has saved you";
- touching;
- the meal;
- letting go;
- the measure;
- authority, and being under it.

## Chapter 5

### 8:1–3 (8:1 grey)

*καὶ αὐτὸς διώδευεν κατὰ πόλιν καὶ κώμην κηρύσσων… καὶ οἱ δώδεκα σὺν αὐτῷ, καὶ γυναῖκές τινες αἳ ἦσαν τεθεραπευμέναι ἀπὸ πνευμάτων πονηρῶν καὶ ἀσθενειῶν, Μαρία ἡ καλουμένη Μαγδαληνή, ἀφ’ ἧς δαιμόνια ἑπτὰ ἐξεληλύθει, καὶ Ἰωάννα γυνὴ Χουζᾶ ἐπιτρόπου Ἡρῴδου… αἵτινες διηκόνουν αὐτοῖς ἐκ τῶν ὑπαρχόντων αὐταῖς.*

- **"he went through city and village"** (διώδευεν, from ὁδός): the way, walked through every doctrine and every smaller place.
- **"the twelve with him, and some women healed of evil spirits and weaknesses"**: all the principles, and the affections that have been freed.
- **"Mary called Magdalene, from whom seven daimons had gone out"** (ἐξεληλύθει): seven is the whole, the holy. An affection from which falsity has wholly gone out. The verb of coming out (4:35, 4:41).
- **"Joanna, wife of Chuza, Herod's steward"**: from the household of the ruler of the natural (3:1). What served the ruler now serves him.
- **"who served (διηκόνουν) them out of their own means"** (ἐκ τῶν ὑπαρχόντων αὐταῖς): service, given from what is one's own. *This is the verb of the left-out 4:39: Simon's mother-in-law, "rising at once, served them" (ἀναστᾶσα διηκόνει). The healed affections serve; the test passage would have been the first instance.* What is one's own (τὰ ὑπάρχοντα) is not held; it is given in service (6:30).

### 8:4–5 (8:5 grey)

*Συνιόντος δὲ ὄχλου πολλοῦ καὶ τῶν κατὰ πόλιν ἐπιπορευομένων πρὸς αὐτὸν εἶπεν διὰ παραβολῆς· Ἐξῆλθεν ὁ σπείρων τοῦ σπεῖραι τὸν σπόρον αὐτοῦ. καὶ ἐν τῷ σπείρειν αὐτὸν ὃ μὲν ἔπεσεν παρὰ τὴν ὁδόν, καὶ κατεπατήθη καὶ τὰ πετεινὰ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ κατέφαγεν αὐτό.*

- **"those from city after city journeying to him"** (ἐπιπορευομένων): the road, now taken toward him by others.
- **"through a parable"**: the first parable told as a story.
- **"The sower went out to sow"** (ἐξῆλθεν): coming out, now of the sower. The seed is the truth of the Word.
- **"some fell"** (ἔπεσεν): falling. *Thread: Simon fell at his knees (5:8); the leper fell on his face (5:12); the blind fall into a pit (6:39); the house fell (6:49). Falling down to be received, and falling down in ruin, are one verb.*
- **"beside the way"** (παρὰ τὴν ὁδόν): the way that John prepares (7:27). The seed that falls beside the way, not into it, is trodden down (κατεπατήθη) and the birds of heaven eat it down (κατέφαγεν). Birds are thoughts; here thoughts that take the truth away before it enters. Trodden down and eaten down: the downward of loss.

### 8:6–7 (grey)

*καὶ ἕτερον κατέπεσεν ἐπὶ τὴν πέτραν, καὶ φυὲν ἐξηράνθη διὰ τὸ μὴ ἔχειν ἰκμάδα. καὶ ἕτερον ἔπεσεν ἐν μέσῳ τῶν ἀκανθῶν, καὶ συμφυεῖσαι αἱ ἄκανθαι ἀπέπνιξαν αὐτό.*

- **"other fell down on the rock"** (ἐπὶ τὴν πέτραν): the rock on which the house was founded (6:48), the name given to Simon (6:14). A house stands on rock, but seed does not grow in it. Faith is a foundation, not soil.
- **"growing up, it dried, for lack of moisture"** (ἐξηράνθη): dry, the word of the man whose right hand "was dry" (ξηρά, 6:6). Without moisture (good that comes with truth) what grows dries.
- **"other fell in the midst of thorns"** (ἐν μέσῳ τῶν ἀκανθῶν): the midst once more.
  - In the earlier passages the midst was the inmost where a person was thrown, let down and stood up (4:35; 5:19; 6:8).
  - Here the midst is already occupied by thorns (6:44: no figs from thorns).
  - The thorns grow up with it and choke it.

### 8:8

*καὶ ἕτερον ἔπεσεν εἰς τὴν γῆν τὴν ἀγαθήν, καὶ φυὲν ἐποίησεν καρπὸν ἑκατονταπλασίονα. ταῦτα λέγων ἐφώνει· Ὁ ἔχων ὦτα ἀκούειν ἀκουέτω.*

- **"into the good earth"** (εἰς τὴν γῆν τὴν ἀγαθήν): the earth on which the foolish man built without foundation (ἐπὶ τὴν γῆν, 6:49) is, when good, the place where seed grows. The boats were brought down to the earth (ἐπὶ τὴν γῆν, 5:11). The earth itself, the natural, is not the fault; what matters is whether it is good.
- **"growing up, it made fruit a hundredfold"**: growth is the first rising that is not of a person. A hundred is fullness; the centurion was "ruler of a hundred" (7:2).
- **"he who has ears to hear, let him hear"**: hearing is the condition of the whole.

**The parable gathers the places the text has already named:** the way (7:27), the rock (6:14; 6:48), the midst (4:35; 5:19; 6:8), the earth (5:11; 6:49). In the parable they become four states of reception.

### Test: 8:9–10 (U−)

*Ἐπηρώτων δὲ αὐτὸν οἱ μαθηταὶ αὐτοῦ τίς αὕτη εἴη ἡ παραβολή. ὁ δὲ εἶπεν· Ὑμῖν δέδοται γνῶναι τὰ μυστήρια τῆς βασιλείας τοῦ θεοῦ, τοῖς δὲ λοιποῖς ἐν παραβολαῖς, ἵνα βλέποντες μὴ βλέπωσιν καὶ ἀκούοντες μὴ συνιῶσιν.*

- **"to you it is given to know the mysteries of the kingdom of God"**: giving and knowing.
- **"to the rest in parables, so that seeing they may not see and hearing may not understand"**: the parable as a covering, given so that they will not see.

**Verdict: strain.** The reason is the text's own mechanics, not its letter. Up to here, everything the Divine does with sight gives it:
- "first cast out the beam, and then you will see clearly" (6:42);
- "to many blind he granted to see" (7:21);
- "the blind look up" (7:22).

The next printed verses say that a lamp is not put under a vessel, and that nothing hidden will not be made manifest (8:16–17). This passage says the opposite: the parable is given "so that" seeing they may not see. That is withholding sight on purpose. In Swedenborg's doctrine the inner sense is veiled to keep it from being profaned, and that would reconcile the two. But in this text the mind is always being opened, and this is the only place where it is closed on purpose. It strains against the movement on both sides of it.

**Revised at 9:45: continues.** The ground for the strain was that the text never closes the mind on purpose. That is not so. The printed text does it at 9:45 (grey):
- The saying about the handing-over "was veiled (παρακεκαλυμμένον) from them, so that (ἵνα) they should not perceive it".
- The verb is the lamp's "cover" (καλύπτει, 8:16), with "so that", said of the disciples themselves.
- The veil is lifted at the end: "their eyes were held (ἐκρατοῦντο) so that they did not recognize him" (24:16); "their eyes were opened (διηνοίχθησαν) and they recognized him" (24:31).

So the text has a law of veiling: what cannot yet be received is covered, and it is uncovered when it can be. 8:17 states the second half: "nothing hidden that will not become manifest". Read with 8:17 and 9:45, 8:9–10 states the first half, and continues the line.

### Test: 8:11–15 (U?)

*Ἔστιν δὲ αὕτη ἡ παραβολή· Ὁ σπόρος ἐστὶν ὁ λόγος τοῦ θεοῦ… εἶτα ἔρχεται ὁ διάβολος καὶ αἴρει τὸν λόγον ἀπὸ τῆς καρδίας αὐτῶν… οἱ δὲ ἐπὶ τῆς πέτρας οἳ ὅταν ἀκούσωσιν μετὰ χαρᾶς δέχονται τὸν λόγον, καὶ οὗτοι ῥίζαν οὐκ ἔχουσιν… καὶ ἐν καιρῷ πειρασμοῦ ἀφίστανται… ὑπὸ μεριμνῶν καὶ πλούτου καὶ ἡδονῶν τοῦ βίου πορευόμενοι συμπνίγονται… τὸ δὲ ἐν τῇ καλῇ γῇ, οὗτοί εἰσιν οἵτινες ἐν καρδίᾳ καλῇ καὶ ἀγαθῇ ἀκούσαντες τὸν λόγον κατέχουσιν καὶ καρποφοροῦσιν ἐν ὑπομονῇ.*

- **"the seed is the word of God"**: the parable translated inside the text itself.
- **"the devil takes (αἴρει) the word from their heart"**: taking. *Thread: "from the one who takes (αἴροντος) your garment" (6:29); "even what he seems to have will be taken (ἀρθήσεται)" (8:18).*
- **"those on the rock receive (δέχονται) the word with joy, but have no root"**: reception (δέχομαι; 4:24; 5:29). On rock, received with joy but without root; in temptation they stand away.
- **"going on (πορευόμενοι), they are choked by cares and riches (πλούτου)"**: the road walked with riches: "woe to you who are rich" (6:24).
- **"in a good heart (καρδίᾳ), hearing the word, they hold it fast (κατέχουσιν)"**: κατέχω is the verb of the crowds who "held him back" from going (κατεῖχον, 4:42). Holding the Divine back from his road is the wrong holding. Holding the word fast in the heart is the right one.
- **"and bear fruit in endurance"** (ὑπομονῇ): *thread: "by your endurance (ὑπομονῇ) you will gain your lives" (21:19).*

**Verdict: continues.** Its words join it to the threads, and the holding of 4:42 is corrected here. It would sit between 8:8 and 8:16.

### 8:16–18

*Οὐδεὶς δὲ λύχνον ἅψας καλύπτει αὐτὸν σκεύει ἢ ὑποκάτω κλίνης τίθησιν, ἀλλ’ ἐπὶ λυχνίας τίθησιν, ἵνα οἱ εἰσπορευόμενοι βλέπωσιν τὸ φῶς. οὐ γάρ ἐστιν κρυπτὸν ὃ οὐ φανερὸν γενήσεται… βλέπετε οὖν πῶς ἀκούετε· ὃς ἂν γὰρ ἔχῃ, δοθήσεται αὐτῷ, καὶ ὃς ἂν μὴ ἔχῃ, καὶ ὃ δοκεῖ ἔχειν ἀρθήσεται ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ.*

- **"no one who has lit a lamp covers it with a vessel (σκεύει), or puts it under a bed (κλίνης)"**: a lamp is truth that gives light.
  - The vessel: the vessels of chapter 2 burst when they could not hold (5:37). Here a vessel can also hide.
  - The bed: the paralysed man lay on his bed and was told to take it up (κλίνη, 5:18–24). The lamp is not put under what one lies on.
- **"on a lampstand, so that those coming in (εἰσπορευόμενοι) may see the light"**: the road, coming in. Sight.
- **"nothing hidden that will not be made manifest"**: the hidden falsity in the assembly became manifest when the word came (4:33). Here it is stated as a law.
- **"See then how you hear"**: seeing and hearing joined.
- **"whoever has, to him will be given; whoever has not, even what he seems (δοκεῖ) to have will be taken from him"**: the measure (6:38). "Seems to have": the own that thought it had him (Nazareth), the rich who have their consolation (6:24). What only seems one's own is taken. *Thread: this sentence comes back at 19:26.*

### Test: 8:19 (A−)

*His mother and brothers came and could not reach him because of the crowd.*

**Verdict: neutral.** 8:20 reports it.

### 8:20–21

*Ἡ μήτηρ σου καὶ οἱ ἀδελφοί σου ἑστήκασιν ἔξω ἰδεῖν θέλοντές σε… Τίς μοι μήτηρ καὶ τίνες μοι ἀδελφοί, εἰ μὴ οἱ τοὺς λόγους μου ἀκούοντες καὶ ποιοῦντες αὐτούς;*

- **"your mother and your brothers are standing outside (ἔξω)"**: outside, as the own put him "outside" (ἔξω) its city (4:29). Now natural kinship stands outside, wanting to see.
- **"my mother and brothers are those who hear my words and do them"**: the church (mother) and its truths (brothers) by hearing and doing (6:47), not by descent. Nazareth, where he was brought up, claimed him as its own (4:23); here the kinship of origin is set outside, and the kinship of reception is named.

### 8:22

*αὐτὸς ἐνέβη εἰς πλοῖον καὶ οἱ μαθηταὶ αὐτοῦ, καὶ εἶπεν πρὸς αὐτούς· Διέλθωμεν εἰς τὸ πέραν τῆς λίμνης, καὶ ἀνήχθησαν.*

- **"he got into a boat, and his disciples"**: the boat of 5:3, the knowledge from the Word, now with all of them in it.
- **"let us go through (διέλθωμεν) to the other side of the lake"**: διέρχομαι, the verb of "passing through their midst" (4:30). Passing through, and to the other side, across the natural memory.
- **"and they were led up"** (ἀνήχθησαν): ἀνάγω, "lead up"; the verb of "lead out (ἐπανάγαγε) into the deep" (5:4). Putting out to sea is said as going up.

### 8:23

*πλεόντων δὲ αὐτῶν ἀφύπνωσεν. καὶ κατέβη λαῖλαψ ἀνέμου εἰς τὴν λίμνην, καὶ συνεπληροῦντο καὶ ἐκινδύνευον.*

- **"as they sailed, he fell asleep"**: the Divine seems absent while the mind crosses.
- **"a squall of wind came down (κατέβη) onto the lake"**: down. He came down (κατῆλθεν, 4:31; καταβάς, 6:17); now the storm comes down. A storm is temptation; it comes down on the natural.
- **"they were being filled"** (συνεπληροῦντο): filling. The boats were filled with fish until they began to sink (5:7); now the boat fills with water.
- **"they were in danger"**.

### 8:24

*προσελθόντες δὲ διήγειραν αὐτὸν λέγοντες· Ἐπιστάτα ἐπιστάτα, ἀπολλύμεθα· ὁ δὲ διεγερθεὶς ἐπετίμησεν τῷ ἀνέμῳ καὶ τῷ κλύδωνι τοῦ ὕδατος, καὶ ἐπαύσαντο, καὶ ἐγένετο γαλήνη.*

- **"they woke him up"** (διήγειραν): ἐγείρω, the rising verb.
  - Before this it was said to the paralysed man (5:23–24), to the man with the dry hand (6:8) and to the dead young man (7:14).
  - Here it is used of him for the first time: they raise him from sleep.
- **"Master, master, we are perishing"** (ἀπολλύμεθα): the verb of the daimon's fear, "have you come to destroy (ἀπολέσαι) us?" (4:34), and of "to save a soul or destroy (ἀπολέσαι) it" (6:9). The disciples fear destruction as the falsity did.
- **"and being raised"** (διεγερθεὶς): he rises.
- **"he rebuked the wind and the surge of the water"** (ἐπετίμησεν): the verb used for the daimon (4:35) and the daimons (4:41). The temptation is rebuked as falsity is rebuked.
- **"they ceased, and there was calm"**.

### 8:25

*Ποῦ ἡ πίστις ὑμῶν; φοβηθέντες δὲ ἐθαύμασαν, λέγοντες πρὸς ἀλλήλους· Τίς ἄρα οὗτός ἐστιν ὅτι καὶ τοῖς ἀνέμοις ἐπιτάσσει καὶ τῷ ὕδατι, καὶ ὑπακούουσιν αὐτῷ;*

- **"Where is your faith?"**: the centurion's faith was not found in Israel (7:9); the woman's faith saved her (7:50); in the storm, the disciples' faith is not to be found.
- **"he commands (ἐπιτάσσει) even the winds and the water"**: the word of 4:36, "with authority and power he commands (ἐπιτάσσει) the unclean spirits, and they come out". The same command over falsities and over temptation.
- **"and they obey him"** (ὑπακούουσιν, "hear under"): obedience is a hearing. The thread of hearing (6:47; 8:8; 8:18; 8:21) ends this chapter with wind and water "hearing under" him.
- **"Who then is this?"**: the question of 5:21 and 7:49, now asked by the disciples themselves.

### What came out of the reading of chapter 5

**The affections that were freed serve.** Mary Magdalene and the others, healed, serve from their own means (8:2–3). The verb is the one of the left-out 4:39, and that passage's place in the line shows again here.

**The parable of the sower gathers the places the text has named** — the way, the rock, the midst, the earth — and makes them states of reception:
- the way, where the seed is trodden and taken;
- the rock, where faith is a foundation but not soil, and what grows dries;
- the midst, already full of thorns;
- the good earth, where it grows and bears a hundredfold.

Growth is the first rising that is not a person's.

**The law of reception is stated outright.** "Whoever has, more will be given; whoever has not, even what he seems to have will be taken" (8:18). It comes back at 19:26. What only seems one's own is lost.

**Kinship by reception.** The family of origin stands outside; mother and brothers are those who hear and do (8:20–21).

**In the storm, rising is first said of him.**
- He goes to sleep; the storm comes down; the boat fills.
- They raise him (διήγειραν), and being raised (διεγερθεὶς) he rebukes the wind as he rebuked the daimons.
- The winds and the water "hear under" him.
- The verb that raised the dead in chapter 4 is applied to the Divine himself, and only after the disciples have come to the point of perishing.

**Holding.** The crowds held him back (4:42); the good heart holds the word fast (8:15, left out). Read by its words, 8:11–15 continues the line.

**Hiding.** At first reading, 8:9–10 seemed the one place where the Divine withholds sight on purpose, and it was marked as a strain. *Revised at 9:45:* the printed text veils on purpose too, and lifts the veil at 24:16 and 24:31. 8:9–10 continues the line.

## Chapter 6

*8:26–9:17. Every verse in this range is printed, so there are no tests. Grey verses: 8:26, 8:29, 8:33–41, 8:47, 8:49–56, 9:4, 9:9–11, 9:15.*

### 8:26–27 (8:26 grey)

*Καὶ κατέπλευσαν εἰς τὴν χώραν τῶν Γερασηνῶν, ἥτις ἐστὶν ἀντιπέρα τῆς Γαλιλαίας. ἐξελθόντι δὲ αὐτῷ ἐπὶ τὴν γῆν ὑπήντησεν ἀνήρ τις ἐκ τῆς πόλεως ἔχων δαιμόνια· καὶ χρόνῳ ἱκανῷ οὐκ ἐνεδύσατο ἱμάτιον, καὶ ἐν οἰκίᾳ οὐκ ἔμενεν ἀλλ’ ἐν τοῖς μνήμασιν.*

- **"they sailed down (κατέπλευσαν)"**: they set out by being "led up" (ἀνήχθησαν, 8:22), and they arrive by sailing *down*. The crossing goes up and comes down.
- **"opposite Galilee"** (ἀντιπέρα): the other side (πέραν) of 8:22, reached. It lies over against the place where the word first came down.
- **"as he came out onto the earth"** (ἐπὶ τὴν γῆν): the earth of the parable (8:8) and of the house built "on the earth" (6:49).
- **"a man from the city, having daimons"** (ἔχων): having. *Thread: "whoever has… even what he seems to have" (8:18).* This man "has" what has him.
- **"for a long time he had not put on a garment"** (ἱμάτιον): a garment is truth clothing the good. *Thread: the old garment and the patch (5:36); "the one who takes your garment" (6:29).* Without truths he is naked.
- **"and did not remain (ἔμενεν) in a house, but in the tombs"** (μνήμασιν): he has no house, the dwelling of the mind (6:48–49). He lives among the dead. *Thread: "into whatever house you enter, there remain (μένετε)" (9:4).*

### 8:28

*ἰδὼν δὲ τὸν Ἰησοῦν ἀνακράξας προσέπεσεν αὐτῷ καὶ φωνῇ μεγάλῃ εἶπεν· Τί ἐμοὶ καὶ σοί, Ἰησοῦ υἱὲ τοῦ θεοῦ τοῦ ὑψίστου; δέομαί σου, μή με βασανίσῃς·*

- **"crying out, he fell before him"** (ἀνακράξας προσέπεσεν): the cry of the falsity in the assembly (ἀνέκραξεν, 4:33), and falling (8:5–8; 5:8; 5:12).
- **"What to me and to you"**: the falsity's words of 4:34, repeated exactly.
- **"Jesus, Son of God the Most High"** (ὑψίστου): the height is named by the falsity. The only other place the text names the Most High is the sonship reached by giving without return (6:35). The falsity knows him as it knew him in 4:34 and 4:41, and names the height to keep him away.
- **"do not torment me"**: the presence of the Divine is torment to falsity.

### 8:29 (grey)

*παρήγγελλεν γὰρ τῷ πνεύματι τῷ ἀκαθάρτῳ ἐξελθεῖν ἀπὸ τοῦ ἀνθρώπου. πολλοῖς γὰρ χρόνοις συνηρπάκει αὐτόν, καὶ ἐδεσμεύετο ἁλύσεσιν καὶ πέδαις φυλασσόμενος, καὶ διαρρήσσων τὰ δεσμὰ ἠλαύνετο ὑπὸ τοῦ δαιμονίου εἰς τὰς ἐρήμους.*

- **"he was commanding the unclean spirit to come out"**: coming out (4:35; 4:41; 8:2).
- **"he was bound with chains and fetters, guarded"**: bonds from outside. These are external restraints, a binding laid on him by others.
- **"and bursting (διαρρήσσων) the bonds"**: διαρρήγνυμι, the verb of the nets that "were bursting" (διερρήσσετο, 5:6). *Thread of bursting:*
  - the nets (5:6);
  - the new wine bursting the old skins (ῥήξει, 5:37);
  - the river bursting on the house (προσέρηξεν, 6:48–49), and the great ruin (ῥῆγμα).

  Here falsity bursts the bonds put on it from outside. External binding does not hold it.
- **"he was driven by the daimon into the deserts"** (ἐρήμους): the desert is where there is no good and no truth.
  - He went himself into a desert place (4:42), and withdrew into the deserts to pray (5:16).
  - This man is driven there.
  - *Thread: "here we are in a desert place" (9:12), where the crowd is fed.*

### 8:30–31

*Τί σοι ὄνομά ἐστιν; ὁ δὲ εἶπεν· Λεγιών, ὅτι εἰσῆλθεν δαιμόνια πολλὰ εἰς αὐτόν. καὶ παρεκάλουν αὐτὸν ἵνα μὴ ἐπιτάξῃ αὐτοῖς εἰς τὴν ἄβυσσον ἀπελθεῖν.*

- **"What is your name?"**: a name is a quality. The question makes the hidden manifest (8:17).
- **"Legion, for many daimons had entered into him"**: a multitude, entered from outside. The man has no name of his own; the name he gives is theirs.
- **"they begged him not to command (ἐπιτάξῃ) them to go away into the abyss"**: command, the verb of 4:36 and 8:25. Falsities and winds obey the same command. The abyss is hell. The falsities know where the command would send them.

### 8:32–33 (8:33 grey)

*Ἦν δὲ ἐκεῖ ἀγέλη χοίρων ἱκανῶν βοσκομένη ἐν τῷ ὄρει· καὶ παρεκάλεσαν αὐτὸν ἵνα ἐπιτρέψῃ αὐτοῖς εἰς ἐκείνους εἰσελθεῖν· καὶ ἐπέτρεψεν αὐτοῖς. ἐξελθόντα δὲ τὰ δαιμόνια ἀπὸ τοῦ ἀνθρώπου εἰσῆλθον εἰς τοὺς χοίρους, καὶ ὥρμησεν ἡ ἀγέλη κατὰ τοῦ κρημνοῦ εἰς τὴν λίμνην καὶ ἀπεπνίγη.*

- **"a herd of swine feeding on the mountain"** (ἐν τῷ ὄρει): swine are filthy and avaricious loves (?).
  - *Thread of the mountain:* Nazareth's city was built on the brow of the hill (τοῦ ὄρους, 4:29); he went out to the mountain to pray (6:12).
  - Here the height is a pasture for swine. A height can be held by prayer, or grazed by the lowest loves.
- **"he permitted them"** (ἐπέτρεψεν): permission. Falsity goes where it belongs: out of the man and into the loves that match it.
- **"the herd rushed down the cliff (κατὰ τοῦ κρημνοῦ) into the lake"**: κρημνός, the cliff. *This is the word of Nazareth's intent: "to cast him down the cliff" (κατακρημνίσαι, 4:29), the only other place it occurs.* The downward fall that the own meant for him happens to the falsities and the swine. He passed through the midst (4:30); they go down.
- **"and was choked"** (ἀπεπνίγη): *thread of choking:*
  - the thorns choked the seed (ἀπέπνιξαν, 8:7);
  - the swine are choked in the lake;
  - the crowds choke him (συνέπνιγον, 8:42).
- **The lake:** it was the lake where the disciples cried "we are perishing" (8:24); there wind and water obeyed him. Here the swine perish in it.

### 8:34–36 (grey)

*Ἰδόντες δὲ οἱ βόσκοντες τὸ γεγονὸς ἔφυγον… ἐξῆλθον δὲ ἰδεῖν τὸ γεγονὸς καὶ ἦλθον πρὸς τὸν Ἰησοῦν, καὶ εὗρον καθήμενον τὸν ἄνθρωπον ἀφ’ οὗ τὰ δαιμόνια ἐξῆλθεν ἱματισμένον καὶ σωφρονοῦντα παρὰ τοὺς πόδας τοῦ Ἰησοῦ, καὶ ἐφοβήθησαν. ἀπήγγειλαν δὲ αὐτοῖς οἱ ἰδόντες πῶς ἐσώθη ὁ δαιμονισθείς.*

- **"those feeding them fled"**: whatever fed the swine flees.
- **"they found the man sitting, clothed, and of sound mind"**: three states replace three:
  - **sitting** replaces being driven into the deserts;
  - **clothed** (ἱματισμένον) replaces having no garment: truths put on;
  - **of sound mind** (σωφρονοῦντα) replaces being seized: the understanding is his own again.
- **"at the feet of Jesus"**: the place of the woman who stood behind at his feet (7:38). The lowest place is the place of the restored.
- **"how the one possessed by daimons was saved"** (ἐσώθη): saving. *Thread: "to save a soul" (6:9); "your faith has saved you" (7:50).*

### 8:37 (grey)

*καὶ ἠρώτησεν αὐτὸν ἅπαν τὸ πλῆθος τῆς περιχώρου τῶν Γερασηνῶν ἀπελθεῖν ἀπ’ αὐτῶν, ὅτι φόβῳ μεγάλῳ συνείχοντο· αὐτὸς δὲ ἐμβὰς εἰς πλοῖον ὑπέστρεψεν.*

- **"they asked him to go away from them"** (ἀπελθεῖν ἀπ’ αὐτῶν): the daimons asked not to "go away" (ἀπελθεῖν) into the abyss (8:31). The region asks the Divine to go away from it.
  - This is Simon's "go out from me" (5:8) said by a whole region.
  - Simon said it at his knees and was kept. The region says it out of fear, and he goes.
- **"they were held fast by great fear"** (φόβῳ μεγάλῳ συνείχοντο): συνέχω.
  - *These are the words of the left-out 4:38: Simon's mother-in-law "was held fast by a great fever" (συνεχομένη πυρετῷ μεγάλῳ).*
  - Fever there and fear here hold the same way. The fever was rebuked and let go (4:39); the fear is not.
  - *Thread: "the crowds hold you fast" (συνέχουσιν, 8:45).*
- **"getting into the boat, he returned"** (ὑπέστρεψεν): returning. *Thread of returning (ὑποστρέφω):* 7:10, 8:39, 8:40, 9:10, then 17:18, 19:12, 23:56, 24:9.

### 8:38–39 (grey)

*ἐδεῖτο δὲ αὐτοῦ ὁ ἀνὴρ… εἶναι σὺν αὐτῷ· ἀπέλυσεν δὲ αὐτὸν λέγων· Ὑπόστρεφε εἰς τὸν οἶκόν σου, καὶ διηγοῦ ὅσα σοι ἐποίησεν ὁ θεός. καὶ ἀπῆλθεν καθ’ ὅλην τὴν πόλιν κηρύσσων ὅσα ἐποίησεν αὐτῷ ὁ Ἰησοῦς.*

- **"to be with him"** (σὺν αὐτῷ): the twelve and the women were "with him" (8:1).
- **"Return to your house"**: the man who "did not remain in a house" (8:27) is sent to his house. The restored state goes back to its own dwelling instead of following. It is not taken along on the road.
- **"recount what God did for you"** → **"proclaiming (κηρύσσων) through the whole city what Jesus did for him"**:
  - He is told "God"; he proclaims "Jesus". The text sets the two side by side without comment.
  - κηρύσσω is his own verb (4:44; 8:1). The one freed from Legion proclaims as he does.

### 8:40–42 (8:40–41 grey)

*Ἐν δὲ τῷ ὑποστρέφειν τὸν Ἰησοῦν ἀπεδέξατο αὐτὸν ὁ ὄχλος, ἦσαν γὰρ πάντες προσδοκῶντες αὐτόν. καὶ ἰδοὺ ἦλθεν ἀνὴρ ᾧ ὄνομα Ἰάϊρος, καὶ οὗτος ἄρχων τῆς συναγωγῆς ὑπῆρχεν, καὶ πεσὼν παρὰ τοὺς πόδας τοῦ Ἰησοῦ παρεκάλει αὐτὸν εἰσελθεῖν εἰς τὸν οἶκον αὐτοῦ, ὅτι θυγάτηρ μονογενὴς ἦν αὐτῷ ὡς ἐτῶν δώδεκα καὶ αὐτὴ ἀπέθνῃσκεν. Ἐν δὲ τῷ ὑπάγειν αὐτὸν οἱ ὄχλοι συνέπνιγον αὐτόν.*

- **"the crowd welcomed (ἀπεδέξατο) him"**: reception.
  - Nazareth would not receive him (οὐδεὶς προφήτης δεκτός, 4:24); Levi made a reception (δοχή, 5:29).
  - On this side of the lake he is received. *The same verb returns in 9:11 with him as subject.*
- **"for all were expecting (προσδοκῶντες) him"**: John's question, "or do we expect (προσδοκῶμεν) another?" (7:19–20). Here they expect him.
- **"a ruler of the synagogue"** (ἄρχων τῆς συναγωγῆς):
  - The first ruler who comes to him. The rulers of 3:1 were only named.
  - The synagogue is the assembly where the falsity cried out (4:33).
  - He falls at the feet, the place of 7:38 and 8:35.
- **"to come into his house"**: the centurion, "I am not worthy that you come under my roof" (7:6).
- **"an only-begotten (μονογενὴς) daughter, about twelve years, and she was dying"**:
  - μονογενής is the word of the widow's only son (7:12). The only one that is left, at the point of death.
  - A daughter is the affection of truth in the church (?).
  - Twelve is all, the whole.
- **"the crowds were choking him"** (συνέπνιγον): choking (8:7; 8:33). The thorns choked the seed. Now the press of the crowd chokes the word on its way.

### 8:43–44

*καὶ γυνὴ οὖσα ἐν ῥύσει αἵματος ἀπὸ ἐτῶν δώδεκα, ἥτις ἰατροῖς προσαναλώσασα ὅλον τὸν βίον οὐκ ἴσχυσεν ἀπ’ οὐδενὸς θεραπευθῆναι, προσελθοῦσα ὄπισθεν ἥψατο τοῦ κρασπέδου τοῦ ἱματίου αὐτοῦ, καὶ παραχρῆμα ἔστη ἡ ῥύσις τοῦ αἵματος αὐτῆς.*

- **"a woman in a flow of blood for twelve years"**: blood is truth. A flow of blood is truth running out and not held (?). Twelve years again: the same span as the daughter's whole life.
- **"having spent her whole living (βίον) on physicians"** (ἰατροῖς): physicians.
  - "Physician, heal yourself" (4:23); "those who are well have no need of a physician" (5:31).
  - Physicians other than him take her whole living and cannot heal.
- **"from behind"** (ὄπισθεν): the woman "stood behind" (ὀπίσω, 7:38).
- **"she touched the fringe of his garment"** (κρασπέδου τοῦ ἱματίου): the garment, the truth that clothes. Its fringe is the outermost part, the ultimate. She reaches the Divine at its outermost, and that is enough.
- **"at once the flow of her blood stood"** (ἔστη): standing.
  - *Thread: "rise and stand (στῆθι) into the midst… and rising he stood (ἔστη)" (6:8); the house that was not shaken (6:48).*
  - What was running out stands.

### 8:45–46

*Τίς μου ἥψατο; ἀρνουμένων δὲ πάντων εἶπεν ὁ Πέτρος· Ἐπιστάτα, οἱ ὄχλοι συνέχουσίν σε καὶ ἀποθλίβουσιν. ὁ δὲ Ἰησοῦς εἶπεν· Ἥψατό μού τις, ἐγὼ γὰρ ἔγνων δύναμιν ἐξεληλυθυῖαν ἀπ’ ἐμοῦ.*

- **"Who touched me?"**: touching (5:13, he touched the leper; 6:19, all sought to touch him; 7:14, he touched the bier; 7:39, "who is this who touches him").
- **"all denying"** (ἀρνουμένων): the first denial. *Thread: "let him deny (ἀρνησάσθω) himself" (9:23); "whoever denies me" (12:9); "you will deny me three times" (22:34).*
- **"Peter said: Master, the crowds hold you fast and press you"**: faith says the press is touching. It does not tell crowding from touching.
- **"I knew power (δύναμιν) gone out (ἐξεληλυθυῖαν) from me"**:
  - Coming out, now said of power.
  - Daimons go out of a person (4:35; 8:2; 8:33); power goes out of the Divine (6:19: "power went out from him and healed all").
  - He knows the difference between crowding and touching.

### 8:47–48 (8:47 grey)

*ἰδοῦσα δὲ ἡ γυνὴ ὅτι οὐκ ἔλαθεν τρέμουσα ἦλθεν καὶ προσπεσοῦσα αὐτῷ… ἀπήγγειλεν ἐνώπιον παντὸς τοῦ λαοῦ… ὁ δὲ εἶπεν αὐτῇ· Θυγάτηρ, ἡ πίστις σου σέσωκέν σε· πορεύου εἰς εἰρήνην.*

- **"seeing that she was not hidden"** (οὐκ ἔλαθεν): "nothing hidden that will not be made manifest" (8:17). The law of the lamp, applied to one woman.
- **"trembling, she fell before him and declared before all the people"**: falling; the hidden made manifest.
- **"Daughter"**: the woman of twelve years of flowing is named a daughter, as the girl of twelve years is a daughter.
- **"your faith has saved you; go into peace"**: the words to the sinful woman (7:50), word for word. The two women at the feet are given one sentence.

### 8:49–50 (grey)

*Τέθνηκεν ἡ θυγάτηρ σου, μηκέτι σκύλλε τὸν διδάσκαλον. ὁ δὲ Ἰησοῦς ἀκούσας ἀπεκρίθη αὐτῷ· Μὴ φοβοῦ, μόνον πίστευσον, καὶ σωθήσεται.*

- **"do not trouble (σκύλλε) the teacher any more"**: the centurion's "do not trouble yourself" (μὴ σκύλλου, 7:6). There it was humility; here it is giving up.
- **"Do not fear; only believe, and she will be saved"**: fear.
  - *Thread:* "do not fear" to Simon (5:10); the disciples feared (8:25); the Gerasenes feared (8:35, 8:37); the woman trembled (8:47).
  - Fear is turned into believing. Saving: the word given to the woman (8:48) is promised for the daughter.

### 8:51–53 (grey)

*ἐλθὼν δὲ εἰς τὴν οἰκίαν οὐκ ἀφῆκεν εἰσελθεῖν τινα σὺν αὐτῷ εἰ μὴ Πέτρον καὶ Ἰωάννην καὶ Ἰάκωβον καὶ τὸν πατέρα τῆς παιδὸς καὶ τὴν μητέρα. ἔκλαιον δὲ πάντες… Μὴ κλαίετε, οὐ γὰρ ἀπέθανεν ἀλλὰ καθεύδει. καὶ κατεγέλων αὐτοῦ, εἰδότες ὅτι ἀπέθανεν.*

- **"he let no one go in with him except Peter and John and James, and the father and mother"**:
  - The first time three are taken apart: faith, the goods of charity (works), charity.
  - With them go the father and mother of the child, the good and the truth (?) from which the affection was born.
  - Into the inmost room only these go in.
- **"Do not weep"**: the word to the widow at Nain (μὴ κλαῖε, 7:13).
- **"she did not die, but sleeps"** (καθεύδει): he slept in the boat (ἀφύπνωσεν, 8:23) and was raised. What looks like death is sleep.
- **"they laughed him down"** (κατεγέλων): laughing, with "down" (κατα-).
  - "Blessed who weep now, for you will laugh" (6:21); "woe to you who laugh now" (6:25).
  - Those who weep (ἔκλαιον) turn to laughing down.
- **"knowing (εἰδότες) that she had died"**: knowing.
  - The falsity "knew" him (οἶδα, 4:34; 4:41).
  - The knowing that holds to what appears laughs at what it cannot see.

### 8:54–56 (grey)

*αὐτὸς δὲ κρατήσας τῆς χειρὸς αὐτῆς ἐφώνησεν λέγων· Ἡ παῖς, ἔγειρε. καὶ ἐπέστρεψεν τὸ πνεῦμα αὐτῆς, καὶ ἀνέστη παραχρῆμα, καὶ διέταξεν αὐτῇ δοθῆναι φαγεῖν. καὶ ἐξέστησαν οἱ γονεῖς αὐτῆς· ὁ δὲ παρήγγειλεν αὐτοῖς μηδενὶ εἰπεῖν τὸ γεγονός.*

- **"taking hold of her hand"** (χειρὸς): the hand is power.
  - *Thread:* the hand stretched out to the leper (5:13); the dry right hand, stretched out (6:6–10).
  - He takes the hand, the power to act.
- **"he called out (ἐφώνησεν)"**: the calling of 8:8, "he who has ears to hear".
- **"Child, rise"** (ἔγειρε): rising. It has been said:
  - to the paralysed man (5:23–24);
  - to the man with the dry hand (6:8);
  - to the dead young man (7:14);
  - and of him, raised from sleep (8:24).
- **"her spirit returned (ἐπέστρεψεν)"**: turning back. The spirit returns as he returned (8:37, 8:40).
- **"she rose up at once"** (ἀνέστη): ἀνίστημι, the second rising verb. *Thread:* "rising (ἀναστάς), he stood" (6:8); Levi "rising, followed" (5:28).
- **"he ordered that she be given to eat"** (δοθῆναι φαγεῖν): *these are the words of 9:13: "you give them to eat" (δότε αὐτοῖς ὑμεῖς φαγεῖν).* The one who has been raised is fed. Then the crowd is fed.
- **"tell no one what has happened"**: the leper (5:14).

### 9:1–2

*Συγκαλεσάμενος δὲ τοὺς δώδεκα ἔδωκεν αὐτοῖς δύναμιν καὶ ἐξουσίαν ἐπὶ πάντα τὰ δαιμόνια καὶ νόσους θεραπεύειν, καὶ ἀπέστειλεν αὐτοὺς κηρύσσειν τὴν βασιλείαν τοῦ θεοῦ καὶ ἰᾶσθαι,*

- **"he gave them power and authority over all the daimons"**:
  - The power that went out from him (8:46) is now given.
  - "Authority and power" were the words the assembly used of his command (ἐν ἐξουσίᾳ καὶ δυνάμει, 4:36).
  - What was his alone is given to all the principles (the twelve).
- **"he sent (ἀπέστειλεν) them to proclaim"**:
  - "for this I was sent (ἀπεστάλην)" (4:43). The one sent sends.
  - κηρύσσειν, his own verb, given to them as it was taken up by the man from Legion (8:39).

### 9:3–5 (9:4 grey)

*Μηδὲν αἴρετε εἰς τὴν ὁδόν, μήτε ῥάβδον μήτε πήραν μήτε ἄρτον μήτε ἀργύριον, μήτε ἀνὰ δύο χιτῶνας ἔχειν. καὶ εἰς ἣν ἂν οἰκίαν εἰσέλθητε, ἐκεῖ μένετε καὶ ἐκεῖθεν ἐξέρχεσθε. καὶ ὅσοι ἂν μὴ δέχωνται ὑμᾶς, ἐξερχόμενοι ἀπὸ τῆς πόλεως ἐκείνης τὸν κονιορτὸν ἀπὸ τῶν ποδῶν ὑμῶν ἀποτινάσσετε εἰς μαρτύριον ἐπ’ αὐτούς.*

- **"take (αἴρετε) nothing for the way (εἰς τὴν ὁδόν)"**: taking and the way.
  - "Even what he seems to have will be taken (ἀρθήσεται)" (8:18). On the way, nothing of one's own is taken.
  - No staff (one's own power to lean on), no bag, no bread, no silver: no good or truth of one's own provision.
  - Not two tunics (χιτῶνας): "from the one who takes your garment, do not hold back the tunic" (6:29).
  - The road is walked with nothing one has.
- **"there remain (μένετε)"**: the Gerasene "did not remain in a house" (8:27).
- **"whoever does not receive (δέχωνται) you"**: reception (4:24; 5:29; 8:40).
- **"shake off the dust from your feet as a testimony (εἰς μαρτύριον)"**:
  - Feet are the natural, the lowest.
  - Dust is the damned, what is dead in the natural (?). What has not received is shaken off and not carried.
  - "As a testimony to them": the leper's offering (5:14).

### 9:6

*ἐξερχόμενοι δὲ διήρχοντο κατὰ τὰς κώμας εὐαγγελιζόμενοι καὶ θεραπεύοντες πανταχοῦ.*

- **"they passed through (διήρχοντο) the villages"**: passing through (4:30; 8:22). They now do what he did.

### 9:7–9 (9:9 grey)

*Ἤκουσεν δὲ Ἡρῴδης ὁ τετραάρχης τὰ γινόμενα πάντα, καὶ διηπόρει διὰ τὸ λέγεσθαι ὑπό τινων ὅτι Ἰωάννης ἠγέρθη ἐκ νεκρῶν, ὑπό τινων δὲ ὅτι Ἠλίας ἐφάνη, ἄλλων δὲ ὅτι προφήτης τις τῶν ἀρχαίων ἀνέστη. εἶπεν δὲ ὁ Ἡρῴδης· Ἰωάννην ἐγὼ ἀπεκεφάλισα· τίς δέ ἐστιν οὗτος περὶ οὗ ἀκούω τοιαῦτα; καὶ ἐζήτει ἰδεῖν αὐτόν.*

- **"Herod the tetrarch heard"**: the ruler of 3:1, the ruler of the natural, hears. The first ruler of the opening returns.
- **"he was perplexed"** (διηπόρει): with no way through (ἀ-πορία). He cannot pass through.
- **"John was raised (ἠγέρθη) from the dead… Elijah appeared (ἐφάνη)… a prophet of the ancients rose (ἀνέστη)"**:
  - Both rising verbs, ἐγείρω and ἀνίστημι, are put in the mouths of rumour.
  - "Raised from the dead" (ἐκ νεκρῶν) is here for the first time in the text, as opinion, and of the wrong person.
  - ἠγέρθη is the people's word at Nain (7:16) and the word of the end (24:6).
  - The ruler of the natural hears of rising and thinks of the past returning.
- **"John I beheaded"**: the head is the inmost and highest. The natural ruler cuts off the head of the one who prepared the way (7:27).
- **"who is this?"**: the question of the scribes (5:21), of the guests (7:49), and of the disciples (8:25), now asked by the ruler.
- **"and he sought to see him"** (ἐζήτει ἰδεῖν αὐτόν):
  - The mother and brothers "wanting to see you" stood outside (8:20).
  - *Thread: "Herod… had long been wanting to see him (ἰδεῖν αὐτόν)" (23:8).* The desire comes back at the end.

### 9:10–11 (grey)

*Καὶ ὑποστρέψαντες οἱ ἀπόστολοι διηγήσαντο αὐτῷ ὅσα ἐποίησαν. καὶ παραλαβὼν αὐτοὺς ὑπεχώρησεν κατ’ ἰδίαν εἰς πόλιν καλουμένην Βηθσαϊδά. οἱ δὲ ὄχλοι γνόντες ἠκολούθησαν αὐτῷ· καὶ ἀποδεξάμενος αὐτοὺς ἐλάλει αὐτοῖς περὶ τῆς βασιλείας τοῦ θεοῦ, καὶ τοὺς χρείαν ἔχοντας θεραπείας ἰᾶτο.*

- **"returning, the apostles recounted (διηγήσαντο) what they had done"**: returning; recounting (διηγοῦ, 8:39).
- **"he withdrew privately to a city called Bethsaida"**: a withdrawal apart (5:16). Bethsaida is "house of fishing" (?), which recalls the catch of 5:1–11.
- **"the crowds followed (ἠκολούθησαν)"**: following (5:11; 5:27–28).
- **"welcoming (ἀποδεξάμενος) them"**: *the crowd welcomed him (ἀπεδέξατο, 8:40); now he welcomes them.* Reception goes both ways.
- **"those who had need (χρείαν ἔχοντας) of healing"**: "not those who are well have need of a physician, but those who are sick" (5:31). Need is the condition for healing.

### 9:12

*Ἡ δὲ ἡμέρα ἤρξατο κλίνειν· προσελθόντες δὲ οἱ δώδεκα εἶπαν αὐτῷ· Ἀπόλυσον τὸν ὄχλον, ἵνα πορευθέντες εἰς τὰς κύκλῳ κώμας καὶ ἀγροὺς καταλύσωσιν καὶ εὕρωσιν ἐπισιτισμόν, ὅτι ὧδε ἐν ἐρήμῳ τόπῳ ἐσμέν.*

- **"the day began to decline (κλίνειν)"**: the day declining is a state drawing toward its end; evening.
  - *Thread of κλίνω:*
    - the bed (κλίνη) of the paralysed man (5:18), let down "with the little bed" (κλινιδίῳ, 5:19), and taken up;
    - the lamp not put under a bed (8:16);
    - here the day declines, and the crowd is made to recline (κατακλίνατε, 9:14).
  - *Forward:* "the Son of Man has nowhere to lay (κλίνῃ) his head" (9:58); "when he had reclined (κατακλιθῆναι) with them, taking the bread he blessed and broke" (24:30); the women "bowing (κλινουσῶν) their faces to the earth" (24:5).
- **"send the crowd away, that going (πορευθέντες) they may lodge (καταλύσωσιν)"**: lodging.
  - *Thread:* "he has gone in to lodge (καταλῦσαι) with a sinful man" (19:7); "where is the guest room (κατάλυμα) where I may eat the Passover?" (22:11).
  - The twelve would send them elsewhere to lodge and eat.
- **"for here we are in a desert place (ἐν ἐρήμῳ τόπῳ)"**: the desert of 4:42 (where the crowds held him back), and of 8:29 (where Legion drove the man). It is the place with no provision. Here they are fed.

### 9:13–14

*Δότε αὐτοῖς ὑμεῖς φαγεῖν. οἱ δὲ εἶπαν· Οὐκ εἰσὶν ἡμῖν πλεῖον ἢ ἄρτοι πέντε καὶ ἰχθύες δύο, εἰ μήτι πορευθέντες ἡμεῖς ἀγοράσωμεν εἰς πάντα τὸν λαὸν τοῦτον βρώματα. ἦσαν γὰρ ὡσεὶ ἄνδρες πεντακισχίλιοι. εἶπεν δὲ πρὸς τοὺς μαθητὰς αὐτοῦ· Κατακλίνατε αὐτοὺς κλισίας ὡσεὶ ἀνὰ πεντήκοντα.*

- **"You give them to eat"**: the girl's "to be given to eat" (8:55). Giving (6:30; 6:38: "give, and it will be given to you").
- **"we have no more than five loaves and two fish"**:
  - Bread is good. Fish are truths of the natural, the knowledges the fishermen were drawn from (5:6–10).
  - Five is a few, what is left (remains) (?). Two is conjunction (?).
  - What the disciples have is little.
- **"unless we go (πορευθέντες) and buy (ἀγοράσωμεν) food"**: buying is acquiring for oneself, making something one's own by payment. The disciples think of the road (πορεύομαι) as a way to buy. He says give.
- **"make them recline in companies of about fifty"**: reclining (κλίνω). The declining day, the crowd laid down.

### 9:15–16 (9:15 grey)

*καὶ ἐποίησαν οὕτως καὶ κατέκλιναν ἅπαντας. λαβὼν δὲ τοὺς πέντε ἄρτους καὶ τοὺς δύο ἰχθύας ἀναβλέψας εἰς τὸν οὐρανὸν εὐλόγησεν ἐπ’ αὐτοὺς καὶ κατέκλασεν καὶ ἐδίδου τοῖς μαθηταῖς παραθεῖναι τῷ ὄχλῳ.*

- **"looking up into heaven"** (ἀναβλέψας εἰς τὸν οὐρανόν):
  - *Thread of looking up:* "the blind look up (ἀναβλέπουσιν)" (7:22). He lifted his eyes onto the disciples (ἐπάρας τοὺς ὀφθαλμούς, 6:20).
  - Here, for the first time, he looks up into heaven.
  - *Forward:* the blind man, "Lord, that I may look up" (ἀναβλέψω, 18:41–42); "Jesus, looking up (ἀναβλέψας), saw" Zacchaeus in the tree (19:5).
- **"he blessed them"** (εὐλόγησεν): "bless those who curse you" (6:28). *Forward: 24:30, the same three acts at Emmaus.*
- **"and broke them down"** (κατέκλασεν): down (κατα-). The bread is broken down so that it can be given. *Forward:* "he broke (ἔκλασεν) and gave… this is my body given (διδόμενον) for you" (22:19); "breaking (κλάσας), he gave to them" (24:30).
- **"and gave to the disciples to set before the crowd"**: what he gives passes through the principles to the many.

### 9:17

*καὶ ἔφαγον καὶ ἐχορτάσθησαν πάντες, καὶ ἤρθη τὸ περισσεῦσαν αὐτοῖς κλασμάτων κόφινοι δώδεκα.*

- **"all were filled"** (ἐχορτάσθησαν): "blessed who hunger now, for you will be filled (χορτασθήσεσθε)" (6:21). The first beatitude to be kept in the letter.
- **"what was over (τὸ περισσεῦσαν)"**:
  - The overflow: "out of the overflow (περισσεύματος) of the heart the mouth speaks" (6:45).
  - The measure "running over" (ὑπερεκχυννόμενον, 6:38).
  - Giving produces surplus.
- **"was taken up"** (ἤρθη): αἴρω, the taking verb.
  - The devil takes the word (8:12, test); what one seems to have is taken (8:18); "take nothing for the way" (9:3).
  - Now what is left over from giving is taken up.
  - Taking from the one who holds is loss. Taking up the overflow of what was given is gathering.
- **"twelve baskets of fragments"** (κλασμάτων, from κλάω, the breaking): twelve, the whole.
  - What is broken and given comes back as a whole.
  - The twelve to whom power was given (9:1) and the twelve baskets.

### What came out of the reading of chapter 6

**The cliff.** The fall down the cliff that Nazareth meant for him (κατακρημνίσαι, 4:29) takes place here, and only here: the herd that the falsities entered rushes down the cliff (κατὰ τοῦ κρημνοῦ, 8:33). He passed through the midst; what is cast down is what belongs below. Swine graze on the mountain (8:32), the same word as Nazareth's hill and his place of prayer. A height can be grazed.

**Bonds from outside do not hold.** The man possessed bursts chains and fetters (διαρρήσσων, the bursting of nets and skins and houses). Only the command (ἐπιτάσσω, 4:36; 8:25; 8:31) frees him.

**What replaces possession.** He is then sitting, clothed, of sound mind, at the feet (8:35). He wants to go with him, but is sent back to his house. He proclaims (κηρύσσω, the Divine's own verb). The restored state goes home; it is not made to walk the road.

**Reception becomes mutual.** The crowd welcomes him (ἀπεδέξατο, 8:40); he welcomes them (ἀποδεξάμενος, 9:11). Nazareth did not receive (4:24). Whoever does not receive the twelve is shaken off like dust (9:5).

**Two daughters of twelve.**
- One is an only daughter, dying. The other is a woman whose truth has run out for twelve years.
- The press of crowds chokes him (the thorns' verb, 8:7, 8:33, 8:42).
- The woman reaches the outermost fringe; what was running out stands (ἔστη).
- Power goes out of him (as daimons go out of people). Nothing is hidden.
- "Daughter, your faith has saved you; go into peace": the words to the sinful woman (7:50).
- The girl: faith, works and charity alone go in with the father and mother. "Do not weep." Death is sleep, as his own sleep in the boat was. Those who know (εἰδότες) laugh him down.
- "Child, rise": the spirit returns, she stands up, and she is given to eat.

**Fear turns to believing.** The Gerasenes, held fast by great fear (συνείχοντο, the fever verb of the left-out 4:38), ask him to go away. The woman trembles and tells. To Jairus: "do not fear, only believe".

**What was his is given.** The power that went out from him (8:46) is given to the twelve (9:1). His sending (4:43) becomes theirs (9:2), and so does his proclaiming. The road is walked taking nothing one has (9:3: αἴρω, the verb of 8:18).

**The ruler of the natural hears of rising.** Herod hears "raised from the dead" and "rose" — both rising verbs, for the first time with "from the dead" — and takes them as the past coming back. He beheaded the one who prepared the way. He asks "who is this?", and seeks to see him (→ 23:8).

**The feeding.**
- The day declines (κλίνω) and the crowd is made to lie down (κατακλίνω), the family of the bed.
- In the desert, the twelve propose to buy. He says "you give".
- He looks up into heaven, the first time he does. He blesses, breaks down, and gives through the disciples. All are filled (6:21 kept).
- The overflow is taken up (αἴρω reversed), twelve baskets of the broken.
- The four acts — reclining, taking bread, blessing, breaking and giving — return at 24:30. The words "given" and "broke" return at 22:19.

**Movement in this chapter:**
- Led up to cross, sailed down to arrive. Falsity driven down the cliff.
- A girl raised (ἔγειρε, ἀνέστη). A woman's flow made to stand.
- The Divine looks up into heaven. The overflow is taken up.
- What is raised in this chapter is raised by him, and then it is fed.

### Threads, carried forward and added

- rising (ἐγείρω, ἀνίστημι), now with "from the dead" in rumour;
- the cliff (κρημνός), 4:29 and 8:33;
- the mountain: hill of the own, prayer, pasture of swine (→ 9:28);
- the desert: 4:42, 5:16, 8:29, 9:12;
- bursting (ῥήγνυμι);
- choking (πνίγω);
- holding fast (συνέχω), with the left-out 4:38;
- reception (δέχομαι), both ways;
- returning (ὑποστρέφω);
- house and remaining (μένω), against the tombs;
- the garment and its fringe; the tunic;
- standing (ἵστημι);
- going out of power and of daimons;
- touching;
- denying (ἀρνέομαι, → 9:23, 12:9, 22:34);
- daughter, only-begotten;
- sleep and death; weeping and laughing;
- the hand;
- giving to eat;
- taking (αἴρω): loss, nothing for the way, gathering the overflow;
- the κλίνω family (bed, declining, reclining, laying the head, bowing);
- looking up (ἀναβλέπω, → 18:41, 19:5);
- blessing and breaking (→ 22:19, 24:30);
- filling (χορτάζω);
- overflow (περισσεύω);
- buying against giving;
- lodging (καταλύω, → 19:7, 22:11);
- Herod seeking to see (→ 23:8);
- fear and believing;
- "who is this?".

## Chapter 7

*9:18–62. Grey: 9:23, 9:25, 9:27, 9:36–39, 9:42–43, 9:45, 9:51, 9:53, 9:56. Not printed: 9:31b (A−) and 9:49–50 (U?). Adopted Marcionite wordings: 9:22 "after three days"; 9:35 "the beloved"; 9:41 without "and perverse".*

### 9:18–19

*Καὶ ἐγένετο ἐν τῷ εἶναι αὐτὸν προσευχόμενον κατὰ μόνας συνῆσαν αὐτῷ οἱ μαθηταί, καὶ ἐπηρώτησεν αὐτοὺς λέγων· Τίνα με λέγουσιν οἱ ὄχλοι εἶναι; οἱ δὲ ἀποκριθέντες εἶπαν· Ἰωάννην τὸν βαπτιστήν, ἄλλοι δὲ Ἠλίαν, ἄλλοι δὲ ὅτι προφήτης τις τῶν ἀρχαίων ἀνέστη.*

- **"as he was praying alone"** (κατὰ μόνας): prayer.
  - *Thread:* the desert prayer (5:16); the night on the mountain (6:12).
  - Alone: at the end of 9:36 "Jesus was found alone (μόνος)". This chapter's first scene and its height both close on "alone".
- **"Who do the crowds say that I am?"**: the question "who is this?" (5:21; 7:49; 8:25; 9:9) is now his to ask.
- **"John… Elijah… a prophet of the ancients rose (ἀνέστη)"**: Herod's rumour (9:7–8), repeated. The crowds' answer is the same as the ruler's: the past rising again.

### 9:20–21

*Ὑμεῖς δὲ τίνα με λέγετε εἶναι; Πέτρος δὲ ἀποκριθεὶς εἶπεν· Τὸν χριστὸν τοῦ θεοῦ. Ὁ δὲ ἐπιτιμήσας αὐτοῖς παρήγγειλεν μηδενὶ λέγειν τοῦτο,*

- **"Peter answered: the Anointed One of God"**: faith names him.
  - The first naming by one of his own.
  - Until now, only the falsity knew it: the daimons "knew that he was the Anointed One" (ᾔδεισαν τὸν χριστὸν αὐτὸν εἶναι, 4:41).
- **"rebuking (ἐπιτιμήσας) them, he ordered them to tell no one"**: *the same two acts as 4:41: "rebuking, he did not let them speak, because they knew he was the Anointed One".*
  - What was done to the falsity's knowing is done to faith's knowing.
  - What is known before it is lived is held back, whether it is known by falsity or by faith. The law of veiling (8:10, revised; 9:45) works from both sides. Here the knower is silenced; at 9:45 the hearers are covered.

### 9:22

*Δεῖ τὸν υἱὸν τοῦ ἀνθρώπου πολλὰ παθεῖν καὶ ἀποδοκιμασθῆναι ἀπὸ τῶν πρεσβυτέρων καὶ ἀρχιερέων καὶ γραμματέων καὶ ἀποκτανθῆναι καὶ μετὰ τρεῖς ἡμέρας ἐγερθῆναι.*

- **"must"** (δεῖ): "I must (δεῖ) proclaim the kingdom to the other cities" (4:43). The necessity of the sending is now the necessity of suffering. *Forward:* "the Son of Man must (δεῖ) be handed over" (24:7); "was it not necessary (ἔδει) that the Anointed One suffer these things and enter into his glory?" (24:26).
- **"suffer many things and be rejected by the elders and chief priests and scribes"**:
  - The chief priests are named for the first time. The opening (3:1) named only rulers, no priest.
  - The scribes have been reasoning since 5:21. Elders, priests and scribes: the church as to its doctrine, in its heads (?).
  - The rejection comes from the height of the church, not from the natural.
- **"and be killed, and after three days be raised (ἐγερθῆναι)"**:
  - The rising verb, said of the Son of Man in the future, after killing. Three days: the full period, the complete state (?).
  - Herod and the crowds speak of a past figure rising (9:7–8; 9:19). He speaks of his own rising, through death.

### 9:23 (grey)

*Εἴ τις θέλει ὀπίσω μου ἔρχεσθαι, ἀρνησάσθω ἑαυτὸν καὶ ἀράτω τὸν σταυρὸν αὐτοῦ καθ’ ἡμέραν, καὶ ἀκολουθείτω μοι.*

- **"to come behind me"** (ὀπίσω μου): behind.
  - The woman stood behind, at his feet (ὀπίσω, 7:38); the woman came from behind (ὄπισθεν, 8:44).
  - Coming behind him is following. *Against it: "looking at the things behind (τὰ ὀπίσω)" (9:62).*
  - Behind him is the place to go; behind oneself is the place not to look.
- **"let him deny himself"** (ἀρνησάσθω ἑαυτόν): denial.
  - All denied that they had touched him (8:45).
  - Here what is denied is the self, the own (proprium). This is the first time the text names the own as something to be refused.
- **"and take up (ἀράτω) his cross daily"**: αἴρω, taking.
  - "Take nothing for the way" (9:3); the overflow taken up (9:17).
  - The one thing taken on the way is the cross: temptation, the fight against the own (?).
  - "Daily" (καθ’ ἡμέραν): day after day, state after state.
- **"and follow me"**: following (5:11; 5:27; 9:11).

### 9:24–25 (9:25 grey)

*ὃς γὰρ ἂν θέλῃ τὴν ψυχὴν αὐτοῦ σῶσαι, ἀπολέσει αὐτήν· ὃς δ’ ἂν ἀπολέσῃ τὴν ψυχὴν αὐτοῦ ἕνεκεν ἐμοῦ, οὗτος σώσει αὐτήν. τί γὰρ ὠφελεῖται ἄνθρωπος κερδήσας τὸν κόσμον ὅλον ἑαυτὸν δὲ ἀπολέσας ἢ ζημιωθείς;*

- **"whoever wills to save his soul will lose it"**:
  - *The pair of 6:9: "to save a soul or destroy it?" (ψυχὴν σῶσαι ἢ ἀπολέσαι).* There it was asked of the sabbath; here it is turned on the one who asks.
  - The own that saves itself destroys itself. Losing it "for my sake" saves it.
  - ἀπόλλυμι: the daimon's fear (4:34) and the disciples' cry (8:24).
- **"gaining the whole world, but losing himself"**:
  - Gaining is acquiring, the disciples' "buy" (9:13).
  - The world (κόσμος) is named for the first time.
  - "Himself" (ἑαυτόν) is the same self that is to be denied (9:23). Denied, it is kept; kept, it is lost.

### 9:26–27 (9:27 grey)

*ὃς γὰρ ἂν ἐπαισχυνθῇ με καὶ τοὺς ἐμοὺς λόγους, τοῦτον ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου ἐπαισχυνθήσεται, ὅταν ἔλθῃ ἐν τῇ δόξῃ αὐτοῦ καὶ τοῦ πατρὸς καὶ τῶν ἁγίων ἀγγέλων. λέγω δὲ ὑμῖν ἀληθῶς, εἰσίν τινες τῶν αὐτοῦ ἑστηκότων οἳ οὐ μὴ γεύσωνται θανάτου ἕως ἂν ἴδωσιν τὴν βασιλείαν τοῦ θεοῦ.*

- **"ashamed of me and of my words"**: the measure (6:38). As one does, it is done to one.
- **"when he comes in his glory"** (δόξῃ): glory.
  - Until now, glory was what people gave to God when they saw healing (5:25–26; 7:16).
  - Here it is his own, for the first time, and it comes in 9:31–32. *Forward: "to enter into his glory" (24:26).*
- **"some of those standing (ἑστηκότων) here… will see the kingdom"**: standing; seeing. Eight days later, three of those standing see his glory (9:32).

### 9:28

*Ἐγένετο δὲ μετὰ τοὺς λόγους τούτους ὡσεὶ ἡμέραι ὀκτὼ καὶ παραλαβὼν Πέτρον καὶ Ἰωάννην καὶ Ἰάκωβον ἀνέβη εἰς τὸ ὄρος προσεύξασθαι.*

- **"after these words, about eight days"**:
  - The eighth day is the beginning of a new state (?).
  - Days count the states in this chapter: "after three days" (9:22), "daily" (9:23), "eight days" (9:28), "in those days" (9:36), "the next day" (9:37), "the days of his taking up" (9:51).
- **"taking Peter and John and James"**: the three who went in to the girl (8:51) — faith, works, charity — taken up with him.
- **"he went up (ἀνέβη) into the mountain"**: *ἀναβαίνω, said of him, for the first time in the text.*
  - Others had gone up before: the friends "went up onto the roof" with the paralysed man (ἀναβάντες, 5:19).
  - He himself had "gone out" to the mountain (6:12) and come down (6:17). Now he goes *up*.
  - *Thread of the mountain:* Nazareth's hill (4:29); prayer (6:12); the swine's pasture (8:32); now the ascent.
  - *Forward:* "two men went up (ἀνέβησαν) into the temple to pray" (18:10). The same words, "went up… to pray".
- **"to pray"**: the going up is for prayer.

### 9:29

*καὶ ἐγένετο ἐν τῷ προσεύχεσθαι αὐτὸν τὸ εἶδος τοῦ προσώπου αὐτοῦ ἕτερον καὶ ὁ ἱματισμὸς αὐτοῦ λευκὸς ἐξαστράπτων.*

- **"as he prayed, the appearance of his face became other"**: the face is the interiors as they show (?). In prayer, the inner shows on the outside.
  - *Thread of the face (πρόσωπον):*
    - "I send my messenger before your face" (7:27);
    - here the face changes;
    - then three times in 9:51–53: he set his face; messengers before his face; "his face was going to Jerusalem".
  - The face that changes on the mountain is the face set toward Jerusalem.
- **"his clothing white, flashing"** (ἱματισμὸς λευκὸς ἐξαστράπτων): the garment.
  - The garment is truth; white is truth from the Divine shining (?).
  - The man freed from Legion was "clothed" (ἱματισμένον, 8:35). The fringe of this garment healed the woman (8:44).
  - *Forward: "two men stood by them in flashing (ἀστραπτούσῃ) clothing" (24:4).*

### 9:30–31

*καὶ ἰδοὺ ἄνδρες δύο συνελάλουν αὐτῷ, οἵτινες ἦσαν Μωϋσῆς καὶ Ἠλίας, οἳ ὀφθέντες ἐν δόξῃ.*

- **"two men were talking with him, Moses and Elijah"**: the Law and the Prophets, the Word in its two parts (?).
  - *Forward:* "two men (ἄνδρες δύο)… in flashing clothing" at the tomb (24:4); "they have Moses and the prophets; let them hear them" (16:29, 16:31).
  - The two men on the mountain and the two men at the tomb have the same words, and the same flashing.
- **"who appeared in glory"**: the glory of 9:26.

### Test: 9:31b (A−)

*ἔλεγον τὴν ἔξοδον αὐτοῦ ἣν ἤμελλεν πληροῦν ἐν Ἰερουσαλήμ.* "They spoke of his departure, which he was about to fulfil in Jerusalem."

- **"his departure"** (ἔξοδον): the noun of going out (ἐξέρχομαι). This is the coming-out thread (daimons 4:35, power 8:46) made into his own exit.
- **"fulfil"** (πληροῦν): filling (5:7; 8:23).
- **"in Jerusalem"**.

**Verdict: continues, but nothing is missing without it.** Every word of it is said again at 9:51, in the printed text: "when the days of his taking up were being fulfilled (συμπληροῦσθαι), he set his face to go to Jerusalem". 9:31 → 9:32 runs without a gap: they appeared in glory; Peter saw the glory. With the clause, the mountain would announce what 9:51 begins. Without it, 9:51 announces it.

### 9:32

*ὁ δὲ Πέτρος καὶ οἱ σὺν αὐτῷ ἦσαν βεβαρημένοι ὕπνῳ· διαγρηγορήσαντες δὲ εἶδον τὴν δόξαν αὐτοῦ καὶ τοὺς δύο ἄνδρας τοὺς συνεστῶτας αὐτῷ.*

- **"weighed down with sleep"**: sleep.
  - He slept in the boat (8:23); the girl "sleeps" (8:52). Now it is the three who are heavy with sleep.
  - On the height, the natural is heavy.
- **"but keeping fully awake (διαγρηγορήσαντες), they saw his glory"**: waking; seeing.
  - "Some standing here will see" (9:27): it is fulfilled for the ones who stay awake.
  - *Forward: "Watch (ἀγρυπνεῖτε) at all times… to stand before the Son of Man" (21:36).*
- **"the two men standing with him"** (συνεστῶτας): standing.

### 9:33

*καὶ ἐγένετο ἐν τῷ διαχωρίζεσθαι αὐτοὺς ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ εἶπεν ὁ Πέτρος πρὸς τὸν Ἰησοῦν· Ἐπιστάτα, καλόν ἐστιν ἡμᾶς ὧδε εἶναι, καὶ ποιήσωμεν σκηνὰς τρεῖς, μίαν σοὶ καὶ μίαν Μωϋσεῖ καὶ μίαν Ἠλίᾳ, μὴ εἰδὼς ὃ λέγει.*

- **"as they were parting from him"**: the state passes.
- **"Master, it is good for us to be here; let us make three tents"**: tents are dwellings.
  - Faith wants to stay on the height and to give the state a house.
  - *This is the holding of 4:42: the crowds "held him back from going".*
  - *Thread of the tent (σκηνή):*
    - "the birds of heaven have dwellings (κατασκηνώσεις), but the Son of Man has nowhere to lay his head" (9:58);
    - "that they may receive you into the eternal tents (σκηνάς)" (16:9).
  - A dwelling is not made on the mountain. It is given at the end of the way.
- **"not knowing what he said"**: faith's speech, without knowing.

### 9:34–35

*ἐγένετο νεφέλη καὶ ἐπεσκίαζεν αὐτούς· ἐφοβήθησαν δὲ ἐν τῷ εἰσελθεῖν αὐτοὺς εἰς τὴν νεφέλην. καὶ φωνὴ ἐγένετο ἐκ τῆς νεφέλης λέγουσα· Οὗτός ἐστιν ὁ υἱός μου ὁ ἀγαπητός, αὐτοῦ ἀκούετε.*

- **"a cloud came and overshadowed them"**: a cloud is the letter that covers the glory and holds it (?).
  - Faith asks for tents; it is given a cloud.
  - The covering again: the law of veiling (9:45), now as the shelter in which the voice is heard.
- **"they were afraid as they went into the cloud"**: fear (8:25; 8:35–37; 8:50).
- **"a voice from the cloud"**: out of the covering, a voice.
- **"This is my son, the beloved; hear him"** (αὐτοῦ ἀκούετε):
  - Hearing is the thread that holds everything: hearing and doing (6:47); "he who has ears" (8:8); "see how you hear" (8:18); "those who hear and do" (8:21); wind and water "hear under" (8:25).
  - The voice does not show; it tells them to hear.
  - *"The beloved" (ἀγαπητός)* is the adopted Marcionite reading. NA27 has "the chosen" (ἐκλελεγμένος). By their words both continue:
    - "the beloved" joins the thread of love (6:27–35; 7:42–47);
    - "the chosen" joins his choosing of the twelve (ἐκλεξάμενος, 6:13).
  - **Neutral.**

### 9:36 (grey)

*καὶ ἐν τῷ γενέσθαι τὴν φωνὴν εὑρέθη Ἰησοῦς μόνος. καὶ αὐτοὶ ἐσίγησαν καὶ οὐδενὶ ἀπήγγειλαν ἐν ἐκείναις ταῖς ἡμέραις οὐδὲν ὧν ἑώρακαν.*

- **"Jesus was found alone"**: the Law and the Prophets withdraw. What remains is him (9:18: alone at prayer).
- **"they were silent… in those days"**: the silence ordered in 9:21 is now kept by the three of their own accord.

### 9:37–39 (grey)

*Ἐγένετο δὲ τῇ ἑξῆς ἡμέρᾳ κατελθόντων αὐτῶν ἀπὸ τοῦ ὄρους συνήντησεν αὐτῷ ὄχλος πολύς. καὶ ἰδοὺ ἀνὴρ ἀπὸ τοῦ ὄχλου ἐβόησεν λέγων· Διδάσκαλε, δέομαί σου ἐπιβλέψαι ἐπὶ τὸν υἱόν μου, ὅτι μονογενής μοί ἐστιν, καὶ ἰδοὺ πνεῦμα λαμβάνει αὐτόν, καὶ ἐξαίφνης κράζει, καὶ σπαράσσει αὐτὸν μετὰ ἀφροῦ καὶ μόγις ἀποχωρεῖ ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ συντρῖβον αὐτόν·*

- **"as they came down (κατελθόντων) from the mountain"**:
  - *κατέρχομαι, the verb of the opening: "he came down (κατῆλθεν) to Capernaum" (4:31). The only two places it occurs.*
  - The first coming down was from where the word came from; this one is from the mountain of glory.
  - As in 6:12 → 6:17, ascent is followed by descent, and the descent meets the crowd and the sick.
- **"look upon my son, for he is my only-begotten"** (μονογενής): the third only one.
  - The widow's son (7:12), Jairus' daughter (8:42), this son.
  - The only one left, held by death or by a spirit.
- **"a spirit takes (λαμβάνει) him"**: taking.
  - *Against it, the noun of 9:51: ἀνάλημψις, "being taken up" (ἀνα-λαμβάνω).*
  - The spirit takes the boy; the days of his own being taken up are being filled.
- **"he cries out"** (κράζει): the cry of falsity (4:33; 8:28).
- **"it convulses him… and scarcely departs, crushing him"**.

### 9:40–41

*καὶ ἐδεήθην τῶν μαθητῶν σου ἵνα ἐκβάλωσιν αὐτό, καὶ οὐκ ἠδυνήθησαν. ἀποκριθεὶς δὲ ὁ Ἰησοῦς εἶπεν· Ὦ γενεὰ ἄπιστος, ἕως πότε ἔσομαι πρὸς ὑμᾶς καὶ ἀνέξομαι ὑμῶν; προσάγαγε ὧδε τὸν υἱόν σου.*

- **"your disciples could not (οὐκ ἠδυνήθησαν)"**:
  - They were given power (δύναμιν) over all daimons (9:1), and they could not. Power given is not power held.
  - Of the three who had gone up, Peter (faith) had named him and been silenced. The rest, below, could not.
- **"O faithless generation"** (ἄπιστος): faith. "Where is your faith?" (8:25); "only believe" (8:50).
  - The adopted shorter address, without "and perverse", is attested by omission (METHOD §5.3). **Neutral.**
- **"how long shall I be with you and bear with you?"**: the Divine bearing with the natural.
- **"bring your son here"**: to him.

### 9:42–43 (grey)

*ἔτι δὲ προσερχομένου αὐτοῦ ἔρρηξεν αὐτὸν τὸ δαιμόνιον καὶ συνεσπάραξεν· ἐπετίμησεν δὲ ὁ Ἰησοῦς τῷ πνεύματι τῷ ἀκαθάρτῳ, καὶ ἰάσατο τὸν παῖδα καὶ ἀπέδωκεν αὐτὸν τῷ πατρὶ αὐτοῦ. ἐξεπλήσσοντο δὲ πάντες ἐπὶ τῇ μεγαλειότητι τοῦ θεοῦ.*

- **"while he was coming, the daimon tore (ἔρρηξεν) him"**: ῥήγνυμι, bursting.
  - The nets (5:6), the skins (5:37), the house (6:48–49), Legion's bonds (8:29).
  - As the boy comes near, the falsity tears hardest. The approach is the moment of the worst tearing, as at 4:35, where the daimon threw the man "into the midst".
- **"Jesus rebuked the unclean spirit"**: rebuking (4:35; 4:41; 8:24; 9:21).
- **"he healed the child and gave him back (ἀπέδωκεν) to his father"**: the widow's son "he gave (ἔδωκεν) to his mother" (7:15). The only one, given back.
- **"at the greatness of God"** (μεγαλειότητι): the greatness. *Thread: "who is greater (μείζων)" (9:46); "the least… is great (μέγας)" (9:48).*

### 9:43b–45 (9:45 grey)

*Πάντων δὲ θαυμαζόντων… Θέσθε ὑμεῖς εἰς τὰ ὦτα ὑμῶν τοὺς λόγους τούτους, ὁ γὰρ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου μέλλει παραδίδοσθαι εἰς χεῖρας ἀνθρώπων. οἱ δὲ ἠγνόουν τὸ ῥῆμα τοῦτο, καὶ ἦν παρακεκαλυμμένον ἀπ’ αὐτῶν ἵνα μὴ αἴσθωνται αὐτό, καὶ ἐφοβοῦντο ἐρωτῆσαι αὐτὸν περὶ τοῦ ῥήματος τούτου.*

- **"while all were marvelling"**: amid the greatness, the saying about being handed over.
- **"put these words into your ears"**: hearing (9:35, "hear him").
- **"to be handed over into the hands of men"** (παραδίδοσθαι εἰς χεῖρας):
  - The hand. He took the leper with his hand (5:13), the girl's hand (8:54). Now the Son of Man is handed over into hands.
  - *Forward:* παραδίδωμι runs through Judas (22:4, 22:6, 22:21–22, 22:48); "must be handed over into the hands of sinful men" (24:7).
- **"it was veiled (παρακεκαλυμμένον) from them, so that they should not perceive it"**: the veil.
  - The lamp is not covered (καλύπτει, 8:16); the saying is.
  - Veiled so that it is not perceived until it can be. *It is lifted at 24:16 → 24:31.*
- **"they feared to ask"**: fear.

### 9:46–48

*Εἰσῆλθεν δὲ διαλογισμὸς ἐν αὐτοῖς, τὸ τίς ἂν εἴη μείζων αὐτῶν. ὁ δὲ Ἰησοῦς εἰδὼς τὸν διαλογισμὸν τῆς καρδίας αὐτῶν ἐπιλαβόμενος παιδίον ἔστησεν αὐτὸ παρ’ ἑαυτῷ, καὶ εἶπεν αὐτοῖς· Ὃς ἐὰν δέξηται τοῦτο τὸ παιδίον ἐπὶ τῷ ὀνόματί μου ἐμὲ δέχεται, καὶ ὃς ἂν ἐμὲ δέξηται δέχεται τὸν ἀποστείλαντά με· ὁ γὰρ μικρότερος ἐν πᾶσιν ὑμῖν ὑπάρχων οὗτός ἐστιν μέγας.*

- **"a reasoning (διαλογισμός) entered into them"**: "entered" (εἰσῆλθεν) is the verb of the daimons entering the man (8:30) and the swine (8:33).
  - Reasoning is that of the scribes (5:21–22) and of the sabbath (6:8).
  - The question is "who is greater". Straight after the saying of the handing-over, the own's question enters the disciples.
- **"knowing the reasoning of their heart"**: he knew their reasonings (5:22; 6:8).
- **"taking hold (ἐπιλαβόμενος) of a child, he stood (ἔστησεν) it beside himself"**:
  - The child is innocence (?).
  - He takes the child, as he took the girl's hand (8:54), and makes it stand: "stand into the midst" (6:8).
- **"whoever receives (δέξηται) this child in my name receives me, and… the one who sent me"**:
  - Reception (4:24; 8:40; 9:5; 9:11) as a chain: child → him → the one who sent.
  - "Sent" (ἀποστείλαντα) is the sending of 4:43 and 9:2.
- **"the lesser among you all, this one is great"**: "the lesser (μικρότερος) in the kingdom is greater (μείζων) than he" (7:28). Greatness is measured downward.

### Test: 9:49–50 (U?)

*Ἀποκριθεὶς δὲ Ἰωάννης εἶπεν· Ἐπιστάτα, εἴδομέν τινα ἐν τῷ ὀνόματί σου ἐκβάλλοντα δαιμόνια, καὶ ἐκωλύομεν αὐτὸν ὅτι οὐκ ἀκολουθεῖ μεθ’ ἡμῶν. εἶπεν δὲ πρὸς αὐτὸν ὁ Ἰησοῦς· Μὴ κωλύετε, ὃς γὰρ οὐκ ἔστιν καθ’ ὑμῶν, ὑπὲρ ὑμῶν ἐστιν.*

- **"John"**: the goods of charity, works.
- **"in your name"**: *the words of 9:48, "in my name".*
- **"casting out (ἐκβάλλοντα) daimons"**: *the verb of 9:40. The disciples could not cast out; someone else, in his name, does.*
- **"we forbade (ἐκωλύομεν) him, because he does not follow with us"**:
  - κωλύω: "do not withhold (μὴ κωλύσῃς) the tunic" (6:29).
  - *Forward:* "do not hinder (μὴ κωλύετε) them" (18:16, the children, the same command); "you hindered (ἐκωλύσατε) those entering" (11:52).
  - Following (9:23) is claimed for "us".
- **"Do not forbid; whoever is not against you is for you"**.

**Verdict: continues.**
- Its words take up 9:40 (casting out), 9:48 (in my name) and 9:23 (following), and they reach 18:16 with the same command.
- The inner movement is the one of 9:46–48, carried one step further. The own asks who is greater inside the group; then it claims the name and the power for the group. 9:54 is its third step: charity and works call down fire on those who do not receive.
- 9:48 → 9:51 runs without it, so it is not needed to close a gap.

### 9:51

*Ἐγένετο δὲ ἐν τῷ συμπληροῦσθαι τὰς ἡμέρας τῆς ἀναλήμψεως αὐτοῦ καὶ αὐτὸς τὸ πρόσωπον ἐστήρισεν τοῦ πορεύεσθαι εἰς Ἰερουσαλήμ,*

- **"as the days were being filled"** (συμπληροῦσθαι):
  - *συμπληρόω, the verb of the boat "being filled" (συνεπληροῦντο) in the storm (8:23). The only two places.* There the boat filled with water and they were in danger. Here the days fill.
  - The filling thread: the boats filled to sinking (5:7); the measure pressed down and running over (6:38).
- **"of his taking up"** (ἀναλήμψεως): ἀνα-λαμβάνω, "to take up".
  - *The text names the ascent with this one word.* It is his, and it is a being taken up.
  - The spirit "takes" (λαμβάνει) the boy (9:39); he "took" (λαβών) the bread (9:16), and "taking hold of" (ἐπιλαβόμενος) the child (9:47) he made it stand.
  - What is ascending, in the text's own word, is he, and the ascent is a being taken up, not a climbing.
  - *The taking up itself is not narrated in the printed text: 24:48–53 is unprinted, and the text ends at 24:47. To be followed at chapter 22.*
- **"he set firm (ἐστήρισεν) his face"**: the face that changed in prayer (9:29). It is set firm toward the goal.
  - *Thread of στηρίζω:* "a great chasm has been fixed (ἐστήρικται)" (16:26); "strengthen (στήρισον) your brothers" (22:32).
- **"to go (πορεύεσθαι) to Jerusalem"**: the road.
  - First named at Nazareth (ἐπορεύετο, 4:30). The crowds held him back "from going" (τοῦ μὴ πορεύεσθαι, 4:42): *the same infinitive, now with its goal.*
  - Jerusalem, the church as to its doctrine, had until now appeared only as a place people came *from* (5:17; 6:17).

### 9:52–53 (9:53 grey)

*καὶ ἀπέστειλεν ἀγγέλους πρὸ προσώπου αὐτοῦ. καὶ πορευθέντες εἰσῆλθον εἰς κώμην Σαμαριτῶν, ὡς ἑτοιμάσαι αὐτῷ· καὶ οὐκ ἐδέξαντο αὐτόν, ὅτι τὸ πρόσωπον αὐτοῦ ἦν πορευόμενον εἰς Ἰερουσαλήμ.*

- **"he sent messengers before his face… to prepare for him"**:
  - *The words of 7:27: "I send my messenger before your face (πρὸ προσώπου σου), who will prepare your way".* What was said of John is now done by him.
  - The messengers prepare (ἑτοιμάζω). *Forward: "prepare the Passover" (22:8–12); "Lord, I am ready (ἕτοιμος)" (22:33).*
- **"a village of the Samaritans"**: Samaria (?). *Forward:* the Samaritan who shows mercy (10:33, in the unprinted 10:29–37); the Samaritan who returns (17:16).
- **"they did not receive (ἐδέξαντο) him, because his face was going to Jerusalem"**: reception refused.
  - Nazareth would not receive him because he would not be theirs (4:24). Samaria will not receive him because of where he is going.
  - The direction of the face is itself what is refused.

### 9:54–56 (9:56 grey)

*ἰδόντες δὲ οἱ μαθηταὶ Ἰάκωβος καὶ Ἰωάννης εἶπαν· Κύριε, θέλεις εἴπωμεν πῦρ καταβῆναι ἀπὸ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ καὶ ἀναλῶσαι αὐτούς; στραφεὶς δὲ ἐπετίμησεν αὐτοῖς. καὶ ἐπορεύθησαν εἰς ἑτέραν κώμην.*

- **"James and John"**: charity and works.
- **"fire to come down from heaven"** (καταβῆναι): down.
  - Fire is love; in its opposite, burning anger.
  - Charity, refused, turns to anger and wants something to come down on the one who refused.
  - 9:5 had given the rule: whoever does not receive, shake off the dust. One is not to burn them.
- **"and consume (ἀναλῶσαι) them"**: *ἀναλίσκω, the woman who "had spent" (προσαναλώσασα) her whole living on physicians (8:43). The only two places.* What consumes, consumes the one who uses it.
- **"turning, he rebuked them"**:
  - Turning: he turned to the crowd (7:9), to the woman (7:44).
  - The rebuke: daimons, wind, fever, Peter's naming (9:21), and now charity's anger.
  - Everything he rebukes is something that must not speak or act yet. When it is good turned to anger, it is rebuked like a falsity.
- **"they went to another village"**: the road goes on.

### 9:57–58

*Καὶ πορευομένων αὐτῶν ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ εἶπέν τις πρὸς αὐτόν· Ἀκολουθήσω σοι ὅπου ἐὰν ἀπέρχῃ. καὶ εἶπεν αὐτῷ ὁ Ἰησοῦς· Αἱ ἀλώπεκες φωλεοὺς ἔχουσιν καὶ τὰ πετεινὰ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ κατασκηνώσεις, ὁ δὲ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου οὐκ ἔχει ποῦ τὴν κεφαλὴν κλίνῃ.*

- **"as they were going on the way"** (ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ): the first time the text puts them "on the way" with Jerusalem as its end.
  - The way: John prepared it (7:27); the seed beside it was trodden (8:5); nothing was to be taken for it (9:3).
- **"I will follow you wherever you go"**: the own's offer.
- **"foxes have holes, and the birds of heaven have dwellings"**:
  - Foxes are cunning (?).
  - The birds of heaven ate the seed by the way (8:5): thoughts that take the word away.
  - Both have dwellings (κατασκηνώσεις, the word of Peter's tents, 9:33).
- **"the Son of Man has nowhere to lay (κλίνῃ) his head"**:
  - *The κλίνω family:* the bed (5:18; 8:16), the day declining and the crowd reclining (9:12–15).
  - On the way, the Divine has no place to lay his head.
  - *Forward: "the day has already declined (κέκλικεν)… stay with us… and when he had reclined (κατακλιθῆναι) with them" (24:29–30).* At the end of the way there is a place to recline.
  - The head: John's was cut off (9:9); the host did not anoint his (7:46).

### 9:59–60

*εἶπεν δὲ πρὸς ἕτερον· Ἀκολούθει μοι. ὁ δὲ εἶπεν· Κύριε, ἐπίτρεψόν μοι ἀπελθόντι πρῶτον θάψαι τὸν πατέρα μου. εἶπεν δὲ αὐτῷ· Ἄφες τοὺς νεκροὺς θάψαι τοὺς ἑαυτῶν νεκρούς, σὺ δὲ ἀπελθὼν διάγγελλε τὴν βασιλείαν τοῦ θεοῦ.*

- **"permit me (ἐπίτρεψόν) first"**: permitting. He permitted the daimons to go into the swine (8:32). Here it is asked for the own's "first".
- **"to bury my father"**: the father is the natural origin. Burying is caring for what is dead in it.
- **"Let (ἄφες) the dead bury their own dead"**:
  - ἀφίημι: sins are let go (5:20–24); Levi left all (5:28); "she loved much, her sins are let go" (7:47).
  - The dead are left to the dead: the tombs (8:27); "she is not dead" (8:52).
- **"you go and announce (διάγγελλε) the kingdom"**: proclaiming, as the man from Legion did (8:39) and the twelve (9:2).

### 9:61–62

*Ἀκολουθήσω σοι, κύριε· πρῶτον δὲ ἐπίτρεψόν μοι ἀποτάξασθαι τοῖς εἰς τὸν οἶκόν μου. εἶπεν δὲ πρὸς αὐτὸν ὁ Ἰησοῦς· Οὐδεὶς ἐπιβαλὼν τὴν χεῖρα ἐπ’ ἄροτρον καὶ βλέπων εἰς τὰ ὀπίσω εὔθετός ἐστιν τῇ βασιλείᾳ τοῦ θεοῦ.*

- **"first, permit me to say farewell to those in my house"**: the house.
  - The man from Legion was sent back to his house (8:39). This one asks to go back to his.
  - What restores one is sent home; what wants to follow cannot turn home "first".
- **"putting his hand to the plough"**: the hand (power to act); the plough is the working of the earth so that it can receive seed (the good earth, 8:8).
- **"and looking (βλέπων) at the things behind (τὰ ὀπίσω)"**:
  - *Behind:* "come behind me" (9:23) against "the things behind".
  - *Forward:* "let him not turn back to the things behind (εἰς τὰ ὀπίσω)… remember Lot's wife" (17:31–32).
  - Looking: "see how you hear" (8:18).
- **"is fit (εὔθετος, 'well placed') for the kingdom"**.

### What came out of the reading of chapter 7

**The ascent is named.**
- At 9:51 the text names its own movement with one word: the days of "his taking up" (ἀναλήμψεως) are being filled. He sets his face to go to Jerusalem.
- In the text's own word, what ascends is he, and the ascent is a being taken up (ἀνα-λαμβάνω), not a climbing.
- The days "fill" with the verb of the boat that filled in the storm (8:23).
- The going (πορεύεσθαι) is the infinitive the crowds held him back from at 4:42, now given its goal.

**The first going up comes before it.**
- At 9:28, after eight days, he goes up (ἀνέβη, the first time the text says this of him) into the mountain to pray, with faith, works and charity.
- In prayer his face becomes other, and his garment flashes white.
- Two men speak with him in glory; the same two men in flashing clothing stand at the tomb (24:4).
- Faith, heavy with sleep, stays awake and sees the glory. It wants to build tents and hold the state. It is given a cloud, and from the cloud a voice says "hear him". He is found alone.
- Then they come down (κατελθόντων), the verb of 4:31.
- Going up is to pray, and it is followed by a coming down to the sick.

**How one follows.**
- Coming behind him (ὀπίσω), denying the self (the own named for the first time as something to refuse), taking up (αἴρω) the cross daily.
- Whoever wills to save his soul loses it (the pair of 6:9 turned on the one who asks).
- Not looking at the things behind (9:62). Letting the dead bury the dead. Not turning home "first".
- The Son of Man has nowhere to lay his head on the way. The tents and dwellings of 9:33 and 9:58 come only at the end (16:9; 24:29–30).

**Faith's knowing is silenced as falsity's was.** Peter names the Anointed One, and is rebuked and ordered to silence, with the acts and words of 4:41. The saying of the handing-over is veiled so that it is not perceived (9:45). This led to revising 8:9–10: the text veils on purpose, and unveils at 24:16 → 24:31.

**Necessity.** "The Son of Man must suffer, be rejected by the elders, chief priests and scribes, be killed, and after three days be raised" (9:22).
- The first time the rising verb is said of him in the future.
- The first time the heads of the church are named, as the ones who reject.
- Herod and the crowds think of rising as the past returning (9:7–8; 9:19). He speaks of it as passing through death.

**The own enters the disciples, three times, after each saying about the passion.**
- Who is greater (9:46).
- (In the test passage 9:49–50) the name and the power claimed for "us".
- Fire down on the village that did not receive (9:54).
- Each time the answer is the downward measure:
  - a child made to stand, the least is great;
  - "do not forbid";
  - a rebuke.

**Below the mountain.** The disciples, given power, cannot cast out. The daimon tears the boy as he comes near; he is healed and given back. The third only one (7:12; 8:42; 9:38).

**What is refused.** Samaria does not receive him because his face is going to Jerusalem: the direction itself is refused. Charity and works, refused, want fire to come down; that is rebuked.

## Chapter 8

*10:1–28. Grey: 10:2–3, 10:6, 10:17, 10:20. Not printed: 10:12–15 (U−), 10:18 (U−), 10:29–37 (U−), 10:38–42 (U?). Adopted Marcionite wordings: 10:1 "seventy"; 10:21 "I thank you", no "Father" in the address, no "and of the earth", "what was hidden"; 10:22 the Father named first; 10:25 "life" without "eternal".*

### 10:1

*Μετὰ δὲ ταῦτα ἀνέδειξεν ὁ κύριος ἑτέρους ἑβδομήκοντα καὶ ἀπέστειλεν αὐτοὺς ἀνὰ δύο δύο πρὸ προσώπου αὐτοῦ εἰς πᾶσαν πόλιν καὶ τόπον οὗ ἤμελλεν αὐτὸς ἔρχεσθαι.*

- **"the Lord showed forth (ἀνέδειξεν) seventy others"**: ἀνα-δείκνυμι, "show up".
  - Seventy is the full number (?). The Marcionite reading is adopted; NA27 brackets [δύο], "seventy-two". **Neutral.**
- **"he sent them two by two"**: two together, as the two men on the mountain (9:30).

### 10:21

*Ἐν αὐτῇ τῇ ὥρᾳ ἠγαλλιάσατο ἐν τῷ πνεύματι τῷ ἁγίῳ καὶ εἶπεν· Εὐχαριστῶ σοι, κύριε τοῦ οὐρανοῦ, ὅτι ἅτινα ἦν κρυπτὰ σοφοῖς καὶ συνετοῖς ἀπεκάλυψας νηπίοις· ναὶ ὁ πατήρ, ὅτι οὕτως εὐδοκία ἐγένετο ἔμπροσθέν σου.*

- **"in that hour he rejoiced"**: the joy of the seventy (10:17) becomes his.
- **"I thank you"** (εὐχαριστῶ):
  - The adopted Marcionite reading; NA27 has "I confess/praise you" (ἐξομολογοῦμαι).
  - εὐχαριστέω continues: the Samaritan who returns "thanking him" (17:16); "taking bread, having given thanks (εὐχαριστήσας), he broke" (22:19).
  - **The verb: neutral, with the thread.**
- **"Lord of heaven"**: the printed text, following both witnesses, lacks NA27's "and of the earth" (METHOD §5.3).
  - *The reading has to weigh this.* In this text, the earth is where the Divine has authority and where reception happens:
    - "the Son of Man has authority on the earth (ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς) to let go sins" (5:24);
    - the good earth bears a hundredfold (8:8);
    - the word came down.
  - Heaven and earth are paired elsewhere in the printed text (12:56; 16:17; 21:33).
  - "Lord of heaven" alone takes the lordship off the place where, in this very verse, the revealing happens: to infants, below.
  - **Strain.** The omission separates the Lord from the earth. That separation is one the text bridges from 4:31 on. NA27's "and of the earth" continues the line.
  - Like every other wording, it is recorded for the restorations (task 14), not changed here.
- **"what was hidden (κρυπτά) from the wise and understanding (συνετοῖς), you revealed (ἀπεκάλυψας) to infants"**:
  - *The law of veiling, stated as thanksgiving:*
    - "nothing hidden (κρυπτόν) that will not become manifest" (8:17);
    - "so that hearing they may not understand (συνιῶσιν)" (8:10);
    - "it was veiled (παρακεκαλυμμένον)" (9:45).
  - Revealing (ἀπο-καλύπτω) is the uncovering of that veil.
  - The hiding is impersonal ("was hidden"), as at 9:45 ("was veiled"). NA27 has "you hid". The adopted wording is Roth's retroversion. **Neutral.**
  - The infants are the child of 9:47–48, the least.
- **"yes, the Father"**:
  - Father: the Divine itself, from which he is sent (9:48: "the one who sent me").
  - *Thread of father:*
    - Jairus, the father, goes in (8:51); the boy is given back to his father (9:42);
    - "let me first bury my father" — "let the dead bury their dead" (9:59–60);
    - "the glory of the Father" (9:26).
  - The natural father is left to the dead. Then the Father is named.

*Ἐν δὲ τῷ πορεύεσθαι αὐτοὺς αὐτὸς εἰσῆλθεν εἰς κώμην τινά· γυνὴ δέ τις ὀνόματι Μάρθα ὑπεδέξατο αὐτόν. καὶ τῇδε ἦν ἀδελφὴ καλουμένη Μαριάμ, ἣ καὶ παρακαθεσθεῖσα πρὸς τοὺς πόδας τοῦ Ἰησοῦ ἤκουεν τὸν λόγον αὐτοῦ. ἡ δὲ Μάρθα περιεσπᾶτο περὶ πολλὴν διακονίαν… μόνην με κατέλειπεν διακονεῖν… Μάρθα Μάρθα, μεριμνᾷς καὶ θορυβάζῃ περὶ πολλά, ὀλίγων δέ ἐστιν χρεία ἢ ἑνός· Μαριὰμ γὰρ τὴν ἀγαθὴν μερίδα ἐξελέξατο ἥτις οὐκ ἀφαιρεθήσεται αὐτῆς.*

- **"as they were going (πορεύεσθαι), he entered a village"**: on the road (9:51).
- **"Martha received (ὑπεδέξατο) him"**: reception, *straight after the Samaritan village "did not receive him" (9:53).*
- **"Mary, sitting beside him at his feet, was hearing his word"**:
  - The feet (7:38; 8:35; 8:41).
  - Hearing the word: "hear him" (9:35); "those who hear the word" (8:21).
- **"Martha was distracted about much service (διακονίαν)"**: service, the healed women's serving (8:3) and the left-out 4:39.
- **"you are anxious (μεριμνᾷς)"**: the cares that choke (8:14, test). *Forward:* "do not be anxious" (12:22–26).
- **"of few things there is need (χρεία), or of one"**: need (5:31; 9:11).
- **"Mary has chosen (ἐξελέξατο) the good portion, which will not be taken away (ἀφαιρεθήσεται) from her"**:
  - Choosing: he "chose" the twelve (ἐκλεξάμενος, 6:13).
  - *Taking away:* "even what he seems to have will be taken" (8:18). *Forward:* "my master is taking away (ἀφαιρεῖται) the stewardship from me" (16:3).
  - What is chosen in hearing is not taken.

**Verdict: continues.**
- It answers 9:53 (reception after non-reception).
- Mary at the feet hearing is 8:21 and 9:35 as a scene. The good part that is not taken away is the law of 8:18 from its other side.
- 10:28 → 11:1 runs, so it does not close a gap.

### What came out of the reading of chapter 8

**The road is prepared, and the kingdom draws near along it.**
- The seventy go before his face (7:27 → 9:52 → 10:1). They go as lambs into the midst of wolves, with nothing of their own and greeting no one on the way.
- Peace is given first. It rests where it is received and comes back where it is not.
- What was set before the crowd (9:16) is set before them.
- The kingdom "has come near" (ἤγγικεν): this is the first time ἐγγίζω is said of the kingdom. The verb then runs to Jerusalem and to 24:15 ("Jesus himself drew near").

**Wiping.** The dust of non-reception is wiped off the feet (ἀπομάσσω). The woman wiped his feet with her hair (ἐκμάσσω, 7:38, 7:44). Love wipes the feet of the Divine; the messengers wipe their own feet of what did not receive. Nothing is burned (9:54–55).

**Joy moved upward.** The seventy rejoice that the daimons are ranked under them. They are told to rejoice instead that their names are written in the heavens. Power over falsity is his; the quality written in heaven is theirs as received. They tread snakes and the power of the enemy, while the enemy is loved (6:27).

**The law of veiling, stated as thanksgiving.** What was hidden from the wise is revealed to infants (10:21). All things are handed over (παραδίδωμι) to him from the Father, as he will be handed over into the hands of men (9:44). The knowing of the Father and the Son is mutual, and is given by revealing. Then: "blessed are the eyes that see".

**How do you read?**
- A lawyer stands up (ἀνέστη) to test him.
- The answer is love with the whole heart, soul, strength and mind, and the neighbour as oneself.
- "Do this and you will live": the first promise of life, which reaches to "the Living One" (24:5).

**The wordings.**
- Most of the adopted Marcionite readings are neutral by their words. Some carry a thread: εὐχαριστῶ (→ 17:16, 22:19), "life" (→ 10:28, 24:5).
- One strains: "Lord of heaven" without "and of the earth" (10:21). It separates the Lord from the earth, where in this text he has authority (5:24) and where the seed is received (8:8).

**The four unprinted passages all continue by their words, and none closes a gap.**
- *10:12–15:* Capernaum, where the word came down, is asked whether it will be lifted to heaven, and goes down to Hades. This is the law of the printed 18:14 said of the place of the beginning.
- *10:18:* Satan falls like lightning from heaven.
- *10:29–37:* a man going down from Jerusalem, the opposite direction to the road, is passed by the priesthood and received by a Samaritan into the "all-receiving" inn.
- *10:38–42:* Martha receives him straight after Samaria did not. Mary hears at his feet, and her chosen part is not taken.

In this chapter the passages that are not printed carry more of the up-and-down mechanics than the printed text does: the heights and the depths, the falling from heaven, the going down from Jerusalem.
