# Decisions on the doubts

Step 4 of the reading (README §2), and the doubts registered in `register.md`.

**Status:** proposals only. Nothing in `data/` is changed.

**Order of work:**
1. Part A fixes what each gap requires, from the certain text alone. It is committed before any candidate passage is read.
2. Part B tests the candidates at each position against those requirements and against the witness evidence.
3. Part B records one of four results:
   - supplied;
   - partly supplied;
   - not supplied;
   - more than one candidate fits (the doubt remains).

**Rules (README §1–2):**
- **Continuing is not evidence.** That a passage "continues" or "fits" counts for nothing. Only a requirement stated in Part A counts.
- **The burden is on change.** Nothing in a candidate passage is admitted beyond what the requirement asks for.
- **Witness evidence counts for the text before Marcion:**
  - `A−` (absent from Marcion's text) is a real doubt, not proof of absence;
  - `U−` (silence of a witness who would have used the passage) counts against the passage;
  - `U?` is unknown.

## Part A — Requirements, fixed before the candidates

### R-G1 — The preparing state (gap G1)

From the certain text alone, the step must:

1. **Stand before truth comes down** (4:31). It is the messenger sent "before your face, who will prepare your way before you" (7:27).
2. **Belong to the wilderness.** "What did you go out into the wilderness to look at?" (7:24): the soul goes out to it in a state empty of its structures.
3. **Carry a baptism.** "The baptism of John" (20:4) is something every part at the centre knows.
4. **End the law and the prophets.** "The law and the prophets were until John; since then the kingdom of God is proclaimed" (16:16).
5. **Be more than a prophet and less than the least in the kingdom** (7:26, 28).

The certain text does **not** require any of these:
- a genealogy;
- truth itself going through the preparing state;
- a voice or a naming at that point;
- a testing of truth;
- the end of the preparing state told as an event. 9:7 presupposes John's death, but does not require it to be narrated.

These are not requirements. Material that supplies only them is not admitted on G1.

### R-G4 — Faith's trial (gap G4)

From the certain text alone, the step must:

1. **Fulfil 22:34.** Faith (Peter) denies three times that it knows truth.
2. **Fall on "this day",** after 22:34 and before the herald of morning ("the cock will not crow").
3. **Be faith's own denial of knowing truth** ("that you know me"). It is not a denial of anything else.

Faith turning back afterwards rests on grey alone (22:32 g). It is a lower requirement and is recorded separately.

### R-G2 — The arrival at the centre (gap G2)

From the certain text alone, the step must:

1. **Lie between "he was near Jerusalem" (19:11) and "teaching the people in the temple" (20:1).**
2. **Show truth entering the centre,** and the centre's response to its entering, as at every other arrival (4:31–32; 4:16–30; 19:5–6).

20:8 is certain: "Neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things." "These things" are what truth does at the centre. Teaching and proclaiming the good news (20:1) already satisfy that reference, so it adds no further requirement.

### R-G5 — From two powers to all (gap G5)

From the certain text alone, the step must:

1. **Lie between 24:31 (the two recognise truth) and 24:37 (fright and disbelief).**
2. **Say who "they" are at 24:37,** or carry the recognition of the two to the other powers (the eleven, 24:9–11), so that 24:37–41 has a setting.

### R-G3 — The occasion of "these things" (gap G3)

From the certain text alone, the step must:

1. **Lie before 21:7.**
2. **Announce something about the centre** whose time and sign can be asked about. The answer that follows concerns the centre's desolation (21:20) and the shaking of heaven and earth (21:25–26).

### R-T1 and R-T2 — Marcion-only wordings in tension

The candidate is the canonical wording (NA27). It is admitted only if:

1. it removes the tension registered in `register.md` §3;
2. it opens no new tension elsewhere in the certain text;
3. the strength of the witness evidence for Marcion's wording does not outweigh it.

**T1** is 10:21, "Lord of heaven", without "Father" in the address and without "and of earth".

**T2** is 18:19, "No one is good except one, God the Father".

No motive is assigned to any reading (METHOD §2).

## Part B — Tests

*(Written after Part A was committed, `d91d7e9`.)* The candidate texts are SBLGNT, as in the reading copy. The witness evidence is quoted from `data/apparatus/`. Each candidate is translated in the same way as the certain text before it is tested.

### B-G1 — The preparing state

**Candidates at the position:**

| Passage | Class | Witness evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 3:2–20 (John's preaching, his imprisonment) | `A−` | Tertullian 4.7.1 joins the date of 3:1 to the descent to Capernaum in one sentence; Irenaeus, *Haer.* 1.27.2 and Hippolytus, *Haer.* 7.31 open with the descent. Roth: unattested. |
| 3:21–22 (truth baptized; the dove; the voice) | `A−` | Epiphanius, *Pan.* 42.11.4: the baptism was not in the text. |
| 3:23–38 (the genealogy) | `A−` | Epiphanius, *Pan.* 42.11.4: not in the text. |
| 4:1–13 (the testing in the wilderness) | `A−` | the junction (Tertullian 4.7.1); Roth, via *Marc.* 5.6.7 |
| 4:14–15 (return to Galilee) | `A−` | the junction; Roth: unattested |

**Translation of 3:2–20, by paragraph:**

- **3:2–6.** The source's word comes to the preparing state "in the wilderness", in a state of the soul empty of its structures [3:2]. The preparing state goes through the whole region around the Jordan proclaiming "a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins" [3:3]: a washing of the soul in its turning. This is the voice crying in the wilderness, "Prepare the way of the Lord" [3:4–6]. Every valley is filled and every mountain brought low, the crooked made straight and the rough smooth: the soul's structure is levelled so that truth can come in. "All flesh shall see the salvation of God": the outer degree as a whole will see it. 3:2a dates this to the high priests Annas and Caiaphas, the governing centre of worship at that time.
- **3:7–9.** The multitudes go out to be washed. They are told to bear fruit worthy of the turning, and not to rest on their descent from Abraham. The axe lies at the root of the trees.
- **3:10–14.** Each part asks what to do. The answers:
  - the one who has two tunics shares;
  - the parts serving the outer rule (tax collectors) collect no more than is set;
  - its soldiers take nothing by force.
- **3:15–17.** The soul wonders whether the preparing state is itself the Anointed One. It answers: "I baptize you with water; but one stronger than I is coming… He will baptize you with the holy spirit and fire." The one who comes gathers the wheat and burns the chaff.
- **3:18–20.** The partial ruler of the outer degree (Herod), rebuked by the preparing state, shuts it in prison.

**Test against R-G1:**

| Requirement | Supplied by |
|---|---|
| 1. Before truth comes | 3:4 ("prepare the way"); 3:16 ("one stronger than I is coming") |
| 2. In the wilderness | 3:2 |
| 3. A baptism | 3:3 |
| 4. The end of the law and the prophets | 3:4–6: the prophetic word fulfilled at the threshold |
| 5. Less than what comes | 3:16 ("not worthy to untie the strap of his sandals") |

Everything required is in 3:2b–6 and 3:15–17. The rest of the material is not required:
- 3:2a, the high priests;
- 3:7–14, the exhortations;
- 3:18–20, the imprisonment;
- 3:21–22, truth's own baptism and the voice;
- 3:23–38, the genealogy;
- 4:1–15, the testing and the return.

**New tensions.** 3:2b–6 and 3:15–17 open none.

Three points are recorded as observations, not as grounds:
- "one stronger" (3:16) is the word of the certain 11:22;
- the fire (3:16) is that of the certain 12:49;
- "repentance for the forgiveness of sins" (3:3) is the wording of the certain 24:47.

3:15 brings the name "the Anointed One" in early, but as a question about the preparing state, which that state denies. No part of the soul names truth's inner form before 4:34, so the sequence of names is unchanged.

The genealogy, by contrast, opens a tension: it derives truth from David, while the certain 20:41–44 denies that the Anointed One is David's son. It is not required, and it counts against itself.

**Counter-evidence.**
- Three witnesses agree that Marcion's text began with 3:1 joined to 4:31. That is strong evidence for Marcion's text.
- For the text before Marcion it is the doubt itself, not its answer.
- Marcion's own certain text keeps "the law and the prophets were until John" (16:16) and the baptism of John (20:4). It therefore points to a step that it does not contain.

**Result:** **supplied** by 3:2b–6 and 3:15–17. These are proposed for the text before Marcion, at this place.

**Doubt remains** for:
- 3:2a, 3:7–14 and 3:18–20 (not required);
- 3:21–22 and 4:1–15 (not required, attested absent in Marcion's text).

**Counts against** 3:23–38: not required, and it opens a tension with 20:41–44.

### B-G4 — Faith's trial

**Candidates at the positions:**

| Passage | Class | Witness evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 22:49 ("Lord, shall we strike with the sword?") | `U−` | depends on the absent 22:38 and 22:50–51 |
| 22:50–51 (the ear cut off and healed) | `A−` | Epiphanius sch. 67 |
| 22:52–53 ("as against a robber") | `U?` | no attested verse |
| 22:54–62 (the denial in the high priest's courtyard) | `U?` | no attested verse; Tertullian passes from the kiss (22:48) to the council (22:66; 4.41.2–3) |
| 24:12 (Peter runs to the tomb) | `U−` | both witnesses follow the chapter closely here; a Western non-interpolation |
| 24:33–35 ("appeared to Simon") | `U?` | tested at B-G5 |

**Translation of 22:54–62.** They seize truth and bring it into the house of the high priest, the governing centre [22:54]. Faith follows "at a distance". A fire is lit in the middle of the courtyard, and faith sits among those gathered round it [22:55]. Then come the three denials:
1. A servant girl, the least part of the centre's household, looks at faith in the light: "This man too was with him." "Woman, I do not know him" [22:56–57].
2. A little later another: "You are one of them too." "Man, I am not" [22:58].
3. About an hour later another insists: "He too was with him, for he is a Galilean", from the outer degree where truth began. "Man, I do not know what you are saying" [22:59–60].

While faith is still speaking, the cock crows, the herald of morning. "The Lord turned and looked at Peter": truth, in custody, turns toward faith. Faith remembers the word of truth: "Before the cock crows today, you will deny me three times" [22:61]. Faith goes out and weeps bitterly [22:62].

**Test against R-G4:**

| Requirement | Supplied by |
|---|---|
| 1. Three denials | 22:57, 58, 60 |
| 2. This day, before the herald of morning | 22:60–61 ("today", the cock) |
| 3. Denial of knowing truth | 22:57 ("I do not know him") |
| (lower, grey) Faith turning back | 22:62: grief only, no restoration |

**New tensions.** None.
- The night setting runs into the certain 22:63–64 (mocking in custody) and 22:66 ("when day came").
- Truth turning to look at faith from custody is consistent with the story.
- The paragraph cannot be cut. 22:54a (the seizure) is the ground of "followed at a distance".

**Counter-evidence.** No witness cites the passage. Neither the silence of Tertullian nor that of Epiphanius meets the `U−` test, because the passage would not obviously have served their arguments. Silence here is not evidence either way.

**Result:** **supplied** by 22:54–62, as a whole paragraph. It is proposed for the text before Marcion.

**Doubt remains** for 22:49–53: not required.

**Counts against** 24:12: not required, and `U−`.

### B-T2 — 18:19, "God the Father"

**Readings:**

| Form | Wording | Witnesses |
|---|---|---|
| Printed (Marcion's text) | εἷς ὁ θεὸς ὁ πατήρ | Epiphanius sch. 50: Marcion "added 'the Father'"; the Dialogue (the Marcionite speaker): "except one, the Father"; Origen and Hippolytus report the heretics' use |
| Canonical (NA27) | εἰ μὴ εἷς ὁ θεός ("except God alone") | Tertullian 4.36.3, the earliest witness to Marcion's text: "Sed quis optimus, nisi unus, inquit, deus?", without "the Father". Roth allows that the copies differed. |

**Test against R-T2:**
1. **The canonical form removes the tension.** "No one is good except God alone." Good is the source alone, and "Father" is not fixed as a title.
2. **It opens no new tension.** The certain text keeps "Father" relational throughout (`register.md` T2).
3. **The witness evidence does not outweigh it.**
   - Marcion's line is divided.
   - The earliest witness (Tertullian, c. 207) reads as the canonical text.
   - The later witnesses (Epiphanius, c. 375, and the Dialogue) have "the Father". Epiphanius' "added" is a comparison with his own copy of Luke: it reports a difference in content, not a motive.

**Result:** **supplied.** "Except God alone" is proposed for the text before Marcion. The printed form belongs to the later copies of Marcion's text.

### B-T1 — 10:21, "Lord of heaven"

**Readings:**

| Element | Printed (Marcion's text) | Canonical (NA27) | Witnesses for the printed form |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) address | no vocative πάτερ ("Yes, Father" follows in the verse) | πάτερ | Epiphanius sch. 22 (explicit); Tertullian 4.25.1 |
| (b) "and of earth" | absent | καὶ τῆς γῆς | Epiphanius (explicit); Tertullian; **𝔓45** |
| (c) hiding | "what was hidden… you revealed" (impersonal) | "you hid these things… and revealed them" | Tertullian 4.25.1, 6; his Latin cannot render NA27 |

**Test against R-T1.** The tension was (b): the source addressed as lord of the inner degree only.

1. **"And of earth" would remove it.** Inwardly, the canonical form is the more coherent one.
2. **It would open no new tension.**
3. **But the short form is not Marcion's alone.** 𝔓45, a manuscript of canonical Luke outside Marcion's line, also lacks "and of earth". The short form therefore circulated independently of Marcion's church. Against two independent lines, a weak-to-medium inner tension does not decide.

For (a) and (c) there is no tension in the inner story. Both forms run.

**Result:** **the doubt remains** for (a), (b) and (c). The tension at (b) stays registered.

### B-G2 — The arrival at the centre

**Candidates at the position:**

| Passage | Class | Witness evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 19:27 (slaughter of the master's enemies) | `U−` | Tertullian would have used it (4.37.4) |
| 19:28 ("he went on ahead, going up to Jerusalem") | `U?` | — |
| 19:29–46 (the colt and the acclaim; the weeping over the city; the cleansing of the temple) | `A−` | Epiphanius sch. 53 names all three sections; Tertullian passes from 19:26 (4.37.4) to 20:1 (4.38.1) |
| 19:47–48 (teaching daily in the temple; the leaders seek to destroy truth) | `U?` | — |

**Translation of 19:28–48:**
- **19:28.** Truth goes on ahead, up toward the centre.
- **19:29–40.** At the mount, a colt no one has ridden is untied and brought. The whole multitude of the following powers praises the source: "Blessed is the king who comes in the name of the Lord". The self-righteous keeping asks truth to silence them; "if these were silent, the stones would cry out".
- **19:41–44.** Seeing the centre, truth weeps over it: "If you had known… the things that make for peace". Its enemies will surround it and leave no stone on another, "because you did not know the time of your visitation".
- **19:45–46.** Truth enters the temple, the soul's centre of worship, and drives out those who sell there: "My house shall be a house of prayer, but you have made it a den of robbers." What the soul holds as its own is driven out of the place of worship.
- **19:47–48.** Truth teaches daily in the temple. The governing centre seeks to destroy it, and cannot, because the people hang on its words.

**Test against R-G2:**
1. **Between 19:11 and 20:1:** all of the candidates.
2. **Entry into the centre, and the centre's response:**
   - The entry is in **19:45–46**, and nowhere else.
   - The centre's response: 19:44 has truth naming the centre's failure to know its visitation. 19:47 has the governing centre seeking to destroy truth, but the certain 20:19 already has that.

   19:45–46 stands as a sentence unit on its own ("And entering the temple…"), and 20:1 ("One day, as he was teaching the people in the temple") follows it without strain.

**Not required:**
- 19:27 and 19:28;
- 19:29–40: the acclaim is the following powers' response, not the centre's;
- 19:47–48.

**19:41–44 is partly relevant.** Its relevance lies in a statement by truth, not in the centre's own response, and it cannot be cut down without dividing the lament.

**New tensions.** 19:45–46 opens none. The house of prayer quotes the prophets the certain text honours (16:29–31), and driving out trade agrees with the certain 16:13 ("God and mammon").

**Counter-evidence.**
- The absence of 19:45–46 from Marcion's text is explicit: Epiphanius names the temple section. Tertullian's sequence confirms it.
- The gap itself is only medium (`register.md` G2).

**Result:** **supplied** by 19:45–46, with **medium** confidence. It is proposed for the text before Marcion, with the explicit attested absence in Marcion's text stated beside it.

**Doubt remains** for 19:28–44 and 19:47–48.

**Counts against** 19:27: `U−`, and not required.

### B-G5 — From two powers to all

**Candidates at the position:**

| Passage | Class | Witness evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 24:32 ("did not our hearts burn… while he opened to us the scriptures?") | `U−` | the opening of the scriptures was Tertullian's thesis and Epiphanius' theme; neither cites it |
| 24:33–35 (the return to Jerusalem; "appeared to Simon"; the report of the breaking of bread) | `U?` | no attested verse |

**Translation of 24:33–35.** In that same hour the two powers rise and return to the centre. They find the eleven gathered, with those who are with them, saying: "The Lord has truly been raised, and has appeared to Simon." Faith has seen. The two tell what happened on the way, "and how he was made known to them in the breaking of the bread".

**Test against R-G5:**
1. **Between 24:31 and 24:37:** yes.
2. **"They" at 24:37:** the two and the eleven, gathered at the centre. The recognition of the two is carried to all the powers.

**New tensions.** One mild: the powers who say "truly raised" (24:34) are frightened when truth stands among them (24:37). Inwardly, report and presence differ: "they still disbelieved for joy" (24:41).

**Observation, not grounds.** 24:34 also meets the lower, grey requirement of G4: faith is restored, since the Living One appears to it.

**Counter-evidence.** No witness cites the paragraph. 24:32, classed `U−`, is not required.

**Result:** **supplied** by 24:33–35. It is proposed for the text before Marcion.

**Counts against** 24:32: `U−`, and not required.

### B-G3 — The occasion of "these things"

**Candidates:**

| Passage | Class | What it announces |
|---|---|---|
| 21:5–6 | `U?` | "The days will come when not one stone will be left upon another" (of the temple) |
| 19:41–44 | `A−` | the city surrounded, "not leave one stone upon another" |
| 20:45–47, 21:1–4 | `U?` | nothing about the centre's end |

**Test against R-G3.** Two candidates meet the requirement:
- **21:5–6** stands directly before the question (21:7) and is its literal referent ("these things that you see").
- **19:41–44** stands at a distance, with 20:1–44 between, and is attested absent in Marcion's text.

The requirement fixed in Part A does not choose between them. Adjacency and the witness evidence favour 21:5–6, but neither is a requirement.

**Result:** **more than one candidate fits.** The doubt remains, with 21:5–6 recorded as the nearer and less contradicted candidate.

### G6 — Not tested

The ending lies beyond the last attested verse (METHOD §10). The gap is in the evidence, not in a text that could be tested against the inner story.

## Part C — Summary of proposals for the text before Marcion

| Doubt | Proposal | Confidence | Against it |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | 3:2b–6; 3:15–17 | high | Marcion's text began at 3:1 + 4:31 (three witnesses) |
| G4 | 22:54–62 | high | unattested (`U?`) |
| T2 | 18:19 "except God alone" (NA27) | high | Epiphanius and the Dialogue have "the Father" |
| G5 | 24:33–35 | medium to high | unattested (`U?`) |
| G2 | 19:45–46 | medium | explicitly absent in Marcion's text (Epiphanius sch. 53) |
| G3 | none (21:5–6 or 19:41–44) | — | — |
| T1 | none | — | 𝔓45 shares the short form |
| G6 | not tested | — | — |

**Counts against:**
- 3:23–38: opens a tension with 20:41–44;
- 19:27, 22:49, 24:12, 24:32: `U−`, and not required.

**Everything else at these positions remains unknown.** Not being required here is not evidence that a passage was absent.

**Consequences for the reading, if the proposals are accepted:**
- **G1.** The story would open with the source's word coming to the preparing state in the soul's wilderness. The preparing state announces "one stronger" who will wash the soul "with the holy spirit and fire", and then truth comes down (4:31).
- **G4 and G5.** Faith's course would be completed. It denies before the least parts of the centre, is looked at by truth, weeps, and is the one to whom the Living One appears.
- **G2.** At the centre, truth's first act would be to clear the place of worship of trade.
- **T2.** Good would be the source alone, with no title.
- **The sequence of names (story §3).** Unchanged except for one addition: "the Lord" in the report to the eleven (24:34).
