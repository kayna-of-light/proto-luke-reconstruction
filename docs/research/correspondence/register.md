# Register: where the inner story does not run

Step 3 of the reading (README §2). This file registers the places where the inner story of the certain text does not run. It also lists the places that look like gaps but hold. Nothing here decides anything about the text.

- For each gap, the requirement is stated from the certain text alone (README §1).
- The unprinted passages at the same place are listed as positions only. Their content was not read for this register.
- Whether the earlier text had more at these places is a later question, not answered here. The tests and proposals are in `decisions.md`.

Strength:
- **strong**: the certain text itself establishes what is missing;
- **medium**: the inner story runs only with a step it does not contain;
- **weak**: the seam is in the letter, and the inner story passes it with little strain.

## 1. Gaps in the inner story

Numbered in text order and listed by strength.

### G1 — The preparing state never enters (strong)

- **Where:** the opening, 3:1 ◊ → 4:31 ◊.
- **Kind:** gap. A state is entered without its preparation.
- **Before:** the soul ruled from outside (3:1).
- **After:** truth comes down into the outer degree and teaches (4:31).
- **Requirement from the certain text:** the text states, in its own words, that a preparing state goes before truth.
  - "Behold, I send my messenger before your face, who will prepare your way before you" (7:27).
  - "The law and the prophets were until John; since then the kingdom of God is proclaimed" (16:16).

  It treats that state as known throughout:
  - John's disciples fast (5:33);
  - John has taught his disciples to pray (11:1);
  - John sends from where he is (7:18–20);
  - John is said to have been raised from the dead (9:7);
  - "the baptism of John" is a question everyone at the centre can be asked (20:4).

  In the inner story this is the soul's turning, in a desolate state, before truth itself is present. In the printed text it never takes place. Its beginning is not told, and nor is its end: 9:7 presupposes John's death, and the beheading is grey (9:9 g).
- **Why it fails inwardly:** truth comes down into a soul that the text itself says must first be prepared. Every later reference to the preparing state points back to a step the story does not contain.
- **Positions at this place:** 3:2–20, 3:21–22, 3:23–38, 4:1–13, 4:14–15 (`A−` in Marcion's text).
- **Certainty:**
  - the requirement rests on certain verses (7:27; 16:16; 20:4; 5:33; 11:1; 7:18–20; 9:7);
  - the junction 3:1 → 4:31 is itself a doubt (◊), because it is the opening of Marcion's text;
  - for the text before Marcion, both the gap and its place are open.

### G4 — Faith's trial is foretold and never told (strong)

- **Where:** 22:33–34 → the end of the text.
- **Kind:** gap. Begun and never completed.
- **Before:**
  - Faith has named the inner form (9:20) and been told to keep silent.
  - On the mountain it would house truth with the law and the prophetic voice (9:33).
  - It declares itself ready "to go with you both to prison and to death" (22:33).
  - Truth answers: "the cock will not crow this day until you deny three times that you know me" (22:34).
  - Grey: "when you have turned again, strengthen your brothers" (22:32 g).
- **After:** faith does not appear again in the printed text. The handing over (22:47–48) goes straight to truth held and mocked (22:63). After the death, the powers as a whole do not believe (24:11). The recognition is given to two other powers (24:31).
- **Requirement from the certain text:** faith is the power that acknowledges, and the story turns on acknowledgement (story §3).
  - The certain text announces faith's failure precisely: three times, this day, before the morning's herald.
  - It names no other prediction about faith.
  - Neither the failure nor any turning back is shown.
  - The faith that first named the inner form has no state at the end.
- **Why it fails inwardly:** a state that the text foretells in exact terms is begun and never completed. The soul's acknowledgement is left without its trial and without its restoration, at the point where the story most needs it.
- **Positions at this place:** 22:49 (`U−`), 22:50–51 (`A−`), 22:52–53 (`U?`), 22:54–62 (`U?`); 24:12 (`U−`); 24:33–35 (`U?`).
- **Certainty:** the foretelling (22:33–34) is certain, and the turning (22:32) is grey.

### G2 — The arrival at the centre is not shown (medium)

- **Where:** 19:11–26 → 20:1.
- **Kind:** gap. A state is entered without the step into it.
- **Before:** the way toward the centre:
  - "on the way to Jerusalem" (17:11);
  - near Jericho (18:35);
  - "he was near Jerusalem" (19:11).
- **After:** "as he was teaching the people in the temple" (20:1).
- **Requirement from the certain text:** at every other arrival the text shows what the place does with truth when truth enters.
  - Capernaum is struck (4:31–32).
  - Nazara claims truth and expels it (4:16–30).
  - The Samaritan village does not receive it (9:52–53 g).
  - Zacchaeus receives it with joy (19:5–6).

  The soul's centre of worship is the place the whole movement goes toward. Its response to truth's entering is not shown: the first thing shown at the centre is the challenge to truth's authority (20:1–2).
- **Why it fails inwardly:** the movement from periphery to centre is the longest movement in the story. It reaches its goal without the meeting of truth and the centre that each earlier arrival has.

  Against this:
  - the challenge (20:1–8) can be read as the centre's first response;
  - 19:11 marks the nearness explicitly.

  The inner story strains here, but does not break outright.
- **Positions at this place:** 19:27 (`U−`), 19:28 (`U?`), 19:29–46 (`A−`), 19:47–48 (`U?`).
- **Certainty:** the before and after are certain.

### G3 — "These things" without their occasion (medium to weak)

- **Where:** 20:44 → 21:7.
- **Kind:** gap. A question is asked whose occasion is not in the story.
- **Before:** the inner form is the lord, not the son, of the soul's past kingship (20:41–44).
- **After:** "Teacher, when will these things be, and what will be the sign when these things are about to take place?" (21:7).
- **Requirement from the certain text:** the question presupposes that something has been announced. The answer then describes the desolation of the soul's centre (21:20) and its shaking (21:25–26). The announcement that would make the soul ask is not in the certain text.
- **Why it fails inwardly:** the soul asks when its centre will be undone, but nothing in the story at this point has told it that it will be. The answer supplies its own content, so the inner story runs, but without its trigger.
- **Positions at this place:** 20:45–47 (`U?`), 21:1–4 (`U?`), 21:5–6 (`U?`).
- **Certainty:** certain.

### G5 — From recognition to fright (medium)

- **Where:** 24:31 → 24:37.
- **Kind:** gap. A state is entered without its preparation. If read as the same powers, it is an inversion.
- **Before:** two powers recognise truth in the breaking of bread, and it vanishes from their sight (24:31).
- **After:** grey, "he himself stood among them" (24:36 g). Certain: "they were startled and frightened and thought they saw a spirit" (24:37), and "they still disbelieved for joy" (24:41).
- **Requirement from the certain text:** the certain text does not say who "they" are at 24:37. There are two readings:
  - **The same two powers.** Then the recognition reached at 24:31 is undone with no movement between: an inversion of the order the story has just established.
  - **The eleven,** who did not believe (24:11). Then the recognition never passes from the two to the whole set of powers, and the scene changes without transition.

  Either way, the step that carries the recognition of the two to the powers as a whole is missing.
- **Why it fails inwardly:** the story's end depends on the soul as a whole recognising the Living One. The certain text has one partial recognition (24:31) and one general disbelief (24:11; 24:41), with no step joining them.
- **Positions at this place:** 24:32 (`U−`), 24:33–35 (`U?`).
- **Certainty:** 24:36 is grey; 24:31, 24:37–39 and 24:41 are certain.

### G6 — The end (weak)

- **Where:** 24:41 / 24:43 g → 24:47 → end.
- **Kind:** gap. A state begun and not closed.
- **Before:** truth among the powers asks for food (24:41); grey, it eats (24:42–43 g).
- **After:** "… and that repentance for the forgiveness of sins should be proclaimed in his name to all the nations" (24:47). The sentence has no main clause in the printed text.
- **Requirement from the certain text:** inwardly, the commission is complete: every part of the soul is to be called to turn and be released. It completes the sending begun at 4:43 ("to the other towns also, because for this I was sent"). What is not closed is truth's presence among the powers. The story stops in the middle of truth speaking.
- **Why it fails inwardly:** only slightly. The content holds. The letter is broken off, and the state of truth's presence is left open.
- **Positions at this place:** 24:44–46 (`U−`), 24:47b, 24:48–53 (`U?`).
- **Certainty:** 24:47 is certain only as far as it is attested; the end of the attestation is not the end of a text (METHOD §10).

## 3. Doubtful wordings and orders against the inner story

Where Marcion's text differs from canonical Luke (◊), the reading followed the printed form. Here is how each form stands against the inner story of the certain text. Coherence is recorded, but it is not evidence. "Fits" decides nothing (README §2).

### Tensions

**T1 — 10:21 ◊, "Lord of heaven" (without "Father" in the address and without "and of earth").** Possible inversion (weak to medium).
- In the certain text the source's working reaches the outer degree:
  - "authority on earth" (5:24);
  - "fire on the earth" (12:49);
  - heaven and earth as a pair (16:17 ◊; 21:33);
  - grey: "faith on earth" (18:8 g).
- The story is the source shaping the soul down into its outer form. Addressing the source as lord of the inner degree alone sets the outer degree outside its lordship.
- The printed verse keeps "Yes, Father" (NA27).
- Against the tension: "Lord of heaven" can be read as the source named from where all comes, without excluding the outer degree.

**T2 — 18:19 ◊, "No one is good except one, God the Father."** Literalization (medium).
- Everywhere else in the certain text "Father" is relational:
  - in address: 10:21; 11:2; 23:46; 22:42 g;
  - with a person: "your Father" (6:36; 12:30), "my Father" (10:22);
  - as the one who gives: 11:13; 12:32.
- Only here does it stand as a title in apposition to "God", fixing the relation as the name of a being.
- The canonical form is "except God alone" (NA27).

Here a step of the inner story is carried by grey verses alone. If the grey verses were not in the earlier text, the step would become a gap. Each case is stated from the certain text.

## 5. Elements whose function is not derived (?)

They are read no further than the text allows and carry no weight here:
- the lake and the boat (5:1–3; 8:22–23);
- the fish and the nets;
- the individual names of the twelve, except Simon/Peter and Judas Iscariot;
- seven (8:2; 17:4);
- eighteen (13:11, 16);
- the twelve years shared by 8:42 and 8:43;
- eight days (9:28);
- seventy, and two by two (10:1);
- snakes and scorpions (10:19);
- Gehenna and who "has authority to cast" there (12:5);
- the man and the woman as a pair (13:19–21; 15:4–10);
- divorce and adultery (16:18);
- Jericho (18:35);
- The Skull (23:33);
- the new tomb (23:53);
- sixty stadia (24:13).
