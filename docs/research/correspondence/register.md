# Register: where the inner story does not run

Step 3 of the reading (README §2).

## Standard

A gap is registered only where the **inner mechanics** of the story fail. That means one of two things:
- the story's own movement requires a state of the soul that is not there;
- a step inverts the order the story has set.

**Letter logic is not a gap.** The following are seams in the letter, not failures of the inner story:
- a person or event referred to but not introduced;
- a prediction whose fulfilment is not narrated;
- an arrival that is not narrated;
- a pattern seen elsewhere that is not repeated;
- a question whose referent is not in the verse before it.

**Witness rules.**
- `A−` is absent.
- `U−` is probably absent.
- `U?` outside an attested pericope is unknown, and comes into question only if the inner mechanics of the certain text require it.

**Result: no gap in the certain text meets this standard.** Six seams were registered in the first version of this file (`dbc6ef5`). All six hold inwardly (§1). What remains are two tensions at Marcion-only wordings (§3).

## 1. Seams tested and found to hold inwardly

### The opening (3:1 → 4:31)

- **Seam in the letter:** the preparing state (John) is referred to throughout:
  - his disciples fast (5:33);
  - he sends to truth (7:18–20);
  - he is the messenger "before your face" (7:27);
  - he taught his disciples to pray (11:1);
  - "the baptism of John" (20:4).

  But he never enters the story.
- **Inner mechanics:** the certain text places the preparing state before its own beginning: "The law and the prophets were until John; since then the kingdom of God is proclaimed" (16:16). The story opens at that "since then": the soul as it stands after its preparation, ruled from outside (3:1), into which truth comes down (4:31). The later mentions of John are the preparing state still at work in parts of the soul beside truth (5:33; 7:18–20; 11:1), or remembered (9:7; 20:4). Nothing in the inner story is missing.
- **Witnesses:** three witnesses attest the opening (Tertullian 4.7.1; Irenaeus; Hippolytus). 3:2–4:15 is attested absent.

### The arrival at the centre (19:11–26 → 20:1)

- **Seam in the letter:** truth is "near Jerusalem" (19:11), and next it is "teaching the people in the temple" (20:1). The entry is not narrated.
- **Inner mechanics:**
  - On the approach the soul expects the rule from within to appear at once (19:11).
  - The parable answers that expectation. The lord goes away to receive the rule, and returns to see what each power did with what it was given (19:12–26).
  - Then truth is at the centre, teaching (20:1).
  - The centre's response follows at once: the challenge to truth's authority (20:1–8) and the attempt to seize it (20:19).

  The entry as an event was expected from the pattern of other arrivals. That is a template, not mechanics.
- **Witnesses:** 19:29–46 is attested absent (Epiphanius sch. 53); 19:27 is `U−`.

### "These things" (21:7)

- **Seam in the letter:** "when will these things be?" has no antecedent in the verse before.
- **Inner mechanics:** the matter of the question is in the certain text:
  - the soul's expectation that the kingdom will appear at once (19:11);
  - the lord's return and his reckoning (19:12–26);
  - the days of the son of man and the coming of the kingdom (17:20–37).

  The question asks when, and by what sign. The answer gives the son of man coming (21:27) and the kingdom near (21:31): the same matter. Between the two, 20:1–44 has emptied every claim of the centre (authority, tribute, the risen state, lineage), so the soul asks when its centre's order will end.
- **Witnesses:** 21:5–6 and the paragraphs before it are `U?`, not required.

### Faith's denial (22:34)

- **Seam in the letter:** the denial is foretold in exact terms, and its fulfilment is not narrated.
- **Inner mechanics:** the foretelling carries the inner content. Faith's readiness, relying on itself (22:33), will fail in the dark, before the new state, and faith will not know truth. The certain text shows that state in the soul's powers as a whole:
  - none of the following powers stands with truth from the handing over to the tomb; only the affections that came from Galilee are there (23:55);
  - after the dawn, the eleven, faith among them, do not believe (24:9–11);
  - two of them do not recognise truth walking beside them (24:16);
  - all take it for a spirit (24:37).

  Faith's not-knowing is fulfilled inwardly.
- **Witnesses:** 22:54–62 is `U?`, not required.

### From recognition to fright (24:31 → 24:37)

- **Seam in the letter:** truth is recognised and vanishes (24:31). Then "they" are frightened and think they see a spirit (24:37). Who and where "they" are is not said.
- **Inner mechanics:** this is not an inversion but the next step.
  1. At 24:31 truth is recognised inwardly, in the giving of the bread, and the outer form vanishes.
  2. At 24:37 the outer form stands among the powers and is taken for a spirit. The soul has recognised truth within, but not yet as its outer form.
  3. Truth then shows its hands and feet: "it is I myself", with bones. It asks to eat (24:38–41). What is recognised within becomes one with the outer form.

  This is the inner form becoming recognised in the outer: the movement the whole sequence of names shows (story §3).
- **Witnesses:** 24:32 is `U−`; 24:33–35 is `U?`, not required.

### The end (24:47)

- **The attestation ends here** (METHOD §10). This is the boundary of the evidence, not a seam in the text.

### The attested orders

These are certain, and the inner story runs in them:
- **Capernaum before Nazara.** The claim of 4:23 ("what we heard was done at Capernaum") has its ground.
- **4:27 inside 17:12–14.** The saying about the one cleansed foreigner is fulfilled in front of the reader by the Samaritan (17:15–18).

## 3. Tensions at Marcion-only wordings

These are the doubts about the text before Marcion. Decisions are in `decisions.md`.

**T1 — 10:21 ◊, "Lord of heaven" (without "Father" in the address and without "and of earth").** Possible inversion, weak to medium.
- In the certain text the source's working reaches the outer degree:
  - "authority on earth" (5:24);
  - "fire on the earth" (12:49);
  - heaven and earth as a pair (21:33).
- Addressing the source as lord of the inner degree alone sets the outer degree outside its lordship.
- The verse keeps "Yes, Father".

**T2 — 18:19 ◊, "No one is good except one, God the Father."** Literalization, medium.
- Everywhere else in the certain text "Father" is relational:
  - in address: 10:21; 11:2; 23:46;
  - with a person: "your Father" (6:36; 12:30), "my Father" (10:22);
  - as the one who gives: 11:13; 12:32.
- Only here does it stand as a title in apposition to "God", fixing the relation as the name of a being.

**Both forms run** (no inner reason to prefer either):

## 4. Elements whose function is not derived (?)

They are read no further than the text allows, and carry no weight here:
- the lake and the boat;
- the fish and the nets;
- the individual names of the twelve, except Simon/Peter and Judas Iscariot;
- seven, eighteen, eight days, seventy, sixty stadia;
- the twelve years shared by 8:42 and 8:43;
- two by two;
- snakes and scorpions;
- Gehenna, and who "has authority to cast" there (12:5);
- the man and the woman as a pair (13:19–21; 15:4–10);
- divorce and adultery (16:18);
- Jericho;
- The Skull;
- the new tomb.
