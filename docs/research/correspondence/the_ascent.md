# What ascends, how, and through which states

*The answer to the question asked of the reading, drawn from the word-by-word reading (`word_by_word.md`) and nothing else. Every claim gives the place in the text where the reading found it.*

*Passages that are not printed are marked (test). The reading tested them at their places by their words. The answer says when it leans on one of them, so that what rests on the printed text alone can be told apart from what does not.*

*No structure was set beforehand. The states below are the ones the text marks with its own returning words. The reading followed those words and they mark the stages; where the text does not mark one, none is drawn.*

---

## 1. The movement in the text's own words

The text has a small number of words for the movement, and it uses them sparingly. They are the first thing to read.

**It begins with a descent.**
- "He came down (κατῆλθεν) to Capernaum" (4:31). This is the first act after the rulers of 3:1. It is the first of two places the verb occurs; the second is the coming down from the mountain of glory (9:37).
- The road (πορεύομαι) is first named after the descent, at the point where the own rejects him: "passing through their midst, he went on (ἐπορεύετο)" (4:30).

**It names its own ascent once, and in the passive.** "When the days of his taking up (ἀνάλημψις) were being filled, he set his face to go to Jerusalem" (9:51).
- The ascent is a being taken up (ἀνα-λαμβάνω), not a climbing.
- The days are "filled" with the verb of the boat that filled in the storm (8:23).
- The going is the infinitive the crowds held him back from at the start (4:42).

**The printed text never says "go up to Jerusalem".** The two places NA27 joins that verb to Jerusalem are not printed: 18:31, "we go up to Jerusalem" (test), and 19:28, "going up to Jerusalem" (test). The printed text says "go to" Jerusalem (9:51, 9:53; 17:11) and "near" it (19:11).

**Where the printed text does say "go up" (ἀναβαίνω), there are four places, and each one tells something.**
1. He "went up into the mountain to pray" (9:28).
2. "Two men went up into the temple to pray" (18:10).
3. Zacchaeus, small in stature, "went up onto a sycamore-fig, to see him" (19:4).
4. "Why do reasonings go up in your heart?" (24:38).

So going up is for prayer (1–2) and to see (3). The last going up in the printed text is the own's doubt rising (4), and it is answered with hands and feet.

**The end is named in the passive, or as entering.**
- "From now on the Son of Man will be seated at the right hand of the power of God" (22:69). "Sit at my right hand" (20:42).
- "Was it not necessary that the Anointed One should suffer these things and enter into his glory?" (24:26).
- "Carried up (ἀνεφέρετο) into heaven" (24:51, test). "Clothed with power from on high (ἐξ ὕψους)" (24:49, test).

**The law of it is stated once in the printed text.** "Whoever lifts himself up will be brought low, and whoever brings himself low will be lifted up" (18:14).
- The same words are in the unprinted 14:11. They follow "go and recline in the last place, so that… he will say to you, Friend, go up higher (προσανάβηθι ἀνώτερον)" (14:10, test).
- Against it the printed text sets: "what is high (ὑψηλόν) among men is an abomination before God" (16:15). And: "who by being anxious can add a cubit to his stature (ἡλικίαν)?" (12:25). Its only other use of that word is Zacchaeus, "small in stature", who goes up a tree to see and is called down (19:3–6).

**In the text's own words, then:**
- what ascends is first of all he;
- the ascent is a being taken up, raised, seated, carried up;
- the one who lifts himself is brought low;
- the one brought low is lifted.

## 2. What ascends

### He ascends

The one who "came down" (4:31) is taken up (9:51). Between the two, the reading found one movement, followed through the Greek.

**He is handed over, and then sets himself into the Father's hands.**
- He is "handed over into the hands of men" (9:44; 24:7).
- At the end he sets (παρατίθεμαι) his spirit "into your hands, Father" (23:46).
- παρατίθημι is the verb of the bread "set before" the crowd (9:16) and of what is "entrusted" (12:48).
- What was handed down into men's hands is set up into the Father's.

**His will is yielded.**
- "Not my will, but yours be done" (22:42), on his knees, on the mountain where he went to pray (22:39–41).
- He is then handed over "to their will" (23:25).
- "Let him deny himself" (9:23) is done by him.

**His face.**
- It "changed" in prayer on the mountain (9:29).
- It was "set firm" (στηρίζω) toward Jerusalem (9:51).
- It was "covered" by those who mocked him (22:64).
- Through the whole text, the face is the inner made visible.

**He is raised, and the end of the movement is named at its lowest point.**
- "He has been raised (ἠγέρθη)" (24:6). That is the form the people used of a prophet at Nain (7:16) and the rumour used of John (9:7).
- At the lowest point — held fast, beaten, covered, led away — he says "from now on… at the right hand" (22:63–69).
- *In this text the lifting up is not after the lowering. It is in it.*

### Of the person, what ascends is what is received

The reading kept finding the same division.

**The own (proprium) does not ascend.**
- When the own rises, it rises against him:
  - Nazareth "rising up" casts him out (4:29);
  - the multitude "rising up" brings him to Pilate (23:1);
  - the lawyer "stood up to test him" (10:25).
- The height the own gives itself is rejected (16:15; 18:14). The city that would be lifted up goes down (10:15, test).
- When it holds, it is emptied:
  - the strong man guarding his court and his possessions "in peace" is disarmed, and his spoils given out (11:21–22);
  - the rich man's "my barns, my grain, my goods, my soul" ends with "this night they demand back your soul" (12:16–20).
- What goes up in it at the end is "reasonings" (24:38).

**What rises in a person is always given.** In the printed text, rising (ἀνίστημι, ἐγείρω) comes after something is let go, and it goes toward something done:
- after the sins are let go, "rise, take up your bed, and go" (5:23–25);
- "leaving everything, rising, he followed" (5:28);
- "rise and stand into the midst" (6:8);
- "young man, rise" (7:14); "child, rise" (8:54);
- the friend inside, "being raised, he will give" (11:8);
- the bent woman, loosed and "made straight" (13:13);
- the cleansed foreigner who turned back, "rising, go" (17:19);
- "rise and pray" (22:46).

*In the test passages:*
- Simon's mother-in-law, "rising, she served" (4:39);
- the younger son, "rising, I will go to my father" (15:18);
- the two from Emmaus, "rising, they returned to Jerusalem" (24:33).

**Those who are lowest are carried.**
- The lost sheep is carried on the shoulders (15:5).
- The poor man is "carried away by the angels" into the bosom (16:22). The bosom is the word of the measure "given into your bosom" (6:38).
- *The verb of the poor man's carrying (ἀποφέρω) is the verb of his own carrying up at the end (ἀναφέρω, 24:51, test).*

**Those taken with him.**
- "Taking along (παραλαβών) Peter and John and James, he went up into the mountain" (9:28).
- "One will be taken along (παραλημφθήσεται), the other left" (17:34–35).
- "Taking along the twelve: we go up to Jerusalem" (18:31, test).
- *The verb of the ascent of the mountain is the verb of the one taken.*

**In correspondence:**
- What ascends in a person is the states that have received:
  - the affections freed (8:2–3), who serve, follow to the cross (23:55), and are first at the tomb (24:10);
  - faith, which fails and returns (22:31–34; 22:54–62, test; 24:34, test);
  - the lost, found;
  - the poor, carried.
- What does not ascend is the own that claims. The text says so in four words: "what is high among men" (16:15).

## 3. How it ascends

The reading found eight mechanics. Each is a thread the text carries through the Greek from beginning to end.

### 3.1 Down before up

- The first ascent in the text is his own, and it is for prayer. He comes down from it with the twelve and stands on the level ground, and power goes out from him (6:12–19).
- The mountain of glory (9:28–36) is followed by "coming down (κατελθόντων)" (9:37), the verb of 4:31, to a boy torn by a spirit.
- The catch came from "the deep" (5:4). The house stands because it was dug "deep" to the rock (6:48). The women come to the rock tomb at "deep" dawn (24:1). These are the only places the word occurs.
- Zacchaeus goes up to see, and is told "hurry, come down" to receive (19:4–6). The tax collector who did not lift his eyes "went down to his house justified" (18:14).
- (Test: the arrival at Jerusalem is "at the descent of the Mount of Olives", with "glory in the highest", 19:37–38.)

### 3.2 Brought low, lifted up (passive)

- "Whoever brings himself low will be lifted up" (18:14).
- "Friend, go up higher", said by the host to the one in the last place (14:10, test).
- The stone the builders rejected (the verb of 9:22 and 17:25) becomes the head (20:17, test).
- *The lifting is always done to the one lifted.* "Raised", "taken up", "seated", "carried up", "clothed from on high".

### 3.3 What one holds is given out

- "Take nothing for the way" (9:3; 10:4).
- The strong man disarmed and his spoils distributed (11:22).
- "Give as alms the things within, and all is clean" (11:41).
- Let go of what is owed (16:5–7).
- "Sell all that you have and give to the poor… and follow me" (18:22). The ruler does not. Zacchaeus, standing, gives half and gives back fourfold (19:8), and "today salvation has come to this house" (19:9).
- On the roof, "do not go down to take" your vessels (17:31).
- *The giving thread ends with the giver: "my body, given for you" (22:19); "my blood, poured out for you" (22:20). Pouring was:*
  - the new wine that cannot be held in old skins (5:37);
  - the measure "poured over" into the bosom (6:38);
  - the blood of the prophets (11:50, test).

### 3.4 Receiving

- What the own will not receive (4:24), others receive (5:29).
- Reception becomes mutual: the crowd receives him (8:40); he receives them (9:11).
- Whoever receives the child receives him and the one who sent him (9:48).
- "Whoever does not receive the kingdom as a child will not enter it" (18:17).
- The watchful wait for (προσδέχομαι) their lord, and he serves them (12:36–37). He receives (προσδέχεται) sinners (15:2). Joseph was "waiting for (προσεδέχετο) the kingdom" (23:51).
- The eternal tents are where one is received (16:9). The dwellings that faith wanted to build on the mountain (9:33) are given at the end of letting go.
- At the end, he receives what they give: the fish (24:42–43).

### 3.5 The will yielded

- "Let him deny himself" (9:23).
- The elder son (15:29, test) and the Pharisee (18:11–12) claim; the servant "of no need" claims nothing (17:10, test).
- "Not my will but yours" (22:42), then handed over to theirs (23:25).

### 3.6 The veil, lifted in stages

The reading followed one law through the text: what cannot yet be received is covered, and is uncovered when it can be. (Chapter 7 revised the reading of 8:9–10 on this point.)
- Stated: "nothing hidden that will not become manifest" (8:17; 12:2).
- Done: "it was veiled from them, so that they should not perceive it" (9:45).
- Thanked for: "you revealed them to infants" (10:21).
- *Put over him:* those holding him "covered" him and asked "who is it?" (22:64).
- *Torn:* "the veil of the sanctuary was torn in the middle" (23:45). The tearing is the verb of the new garment (5:36).
- Lifted by remembering: "they remembered his sayings" (24:8). That is the saying veiled in 9:45.
- Lifted at the bread: "their eyes were opened, and they recognized him" (24:31), after "their eyes were held" (24:16).
- *Its last stage is in the unprinted 24:45: "he opened their mind to understand the scriptures".*

### 3.7 The midst

The text's longest thread, from the first step to the last:
- "passing through their midst" (4:30);
- thrown into the midst by a falsity (4:35);
- let down into the midst by friends (5:19);
- "rise and stand into the midst… and he stood" (6:8);
- thorns in the midst (8:7);
- lambs in the midst of wolves (10:3);
- "through the midst" of Samaria and Galilee (17:11);
- (test) "I am in your midst as the one who serves" (22:27);
- (test) Peter in the midst of the strong man's courtyard (22:55);
- the veil torn "in the middle" (23:45);
- *"he himself stood in their midst" (24:36).*

The midst is the inmost. A person is first thrown into it, then let down into it, then made to stand in it. The Divine passes through it at the start and stands in it at the end. His word there is the first word the seventy were to say at every house: "Peace" (10:5; 24:36).

### 3.8 Rising toward giving

See §2. Every rising the text gives a person goes toward serving, following, standing, giving, going, praying or returning. None of them is rising to take.

## 4. Through which states

The text marks its stages with its own returning words. The reading found five sequences that move together. Read side by side, they give the states and how each develops into the next.

### The five sequences

**The road (πορεύομαι):**
1. begins at rejection (4:30);
2. is held back by the crowds (4:42);
3. is set toward its end (9:51);
4. is given its reason — "a prophet cannot perish outside Jerusalem… on the third day I am perfected" (13:32–33, test);
5. is marked again with the sentence of 4:30 (17:11);
6. is near (19:11);
7. (test) arrives (19:28–46);
8. becomes his passion: "the Son of Man goes (πορεύεται)" (22:22);
9. goes away from Jerusalem with the two (24:13);
10. is walked by him with them (24:15);
11. goes "further" (24:28);
12. (test) returns: "rising, they returned to Jerusalem" (24:33).

**The mountain:**
1. Nazareth's hill, from which the own would throw him down (4:29);
2. the night of prayer (6:12);
3. the swine's pasture (8:32);
4. the mountain of glory, gone up to pray, where his face changes (9:28–36);
5. the Mount of Olives, by night (21:37);
6. its prayer on the knees, where his will is yielded (22:39–46);
7. (test) Bethany at the Mount of Olives, where he is carried up while blessing (24:50–51; 19:29).

**The bed, the declining day, reclining (κλίνω):**
1. the paralysed man's bed (5:18–24);
2. "the day began to decline", and the crowd made to recline (9:12–15);
3. "nowhere to lay his head" (9:58);
4. the lord who makes the watchful recline (12:37);
5. "two on one bed" (17:34);
6. the women's faces bowed to the earth (24:5);
7. "the day has declined", "and when he had reclined with them" (24:29–30).

**Rising:**
1. the own against him (4:29);
2. the one who cannot move, the one who sits, the one without power (5–6);
3. the dead (7:14–16; 8:54);
4. rising to give (11:7–8);
5. the bent made straight (13:13);
6. (test) rising to go to the father (15:18);
7. "rising, go" (17:19);
8. rising from prayer (22:45);
9. "he has been raised" (24:6);
10. (test) rising to return to Jerusalem (24:33).

**The veil** (§3.6) and **the midst** (§3.7).

### The states, as the sequences mark them

**1. The Divine comes down into the outward, and the hidden falsity shows itself (3:1–4:44).**
- A land governed from outside: rulers, no priest, no word (3:1).
- The word comes down. A falsity in the assembly knows him and is cast out (4:31–37).
- The own, in the place that fed it, claims him as its own and rises to throw him down.
- He passes through its midst, and the road begins (4:16–30).
- *Unprinted:* he rose to read the Word, unrolled it, read "to proclaim release", and said "today this scripture is fulfilled" (4:17–21, test). The reading finds the printed text needs it (§5).

**2. Letting go, and the first rising (5:1–6:11).**
- From the deep, nets filled. Faith says "go out from me" and is called.
- Sins are let go, and the paralysed man rises. Everything is left, and Levi rises and follows.
- The new will not go into the old.
- The dry hand is made to stand in the midst.
- *Each rising comes after a letting go, at his word.*

**3. The first going up is his, for prayer, and the law of the vessel (6:12–49).**
- He goes up the mountain to pray, chooses, comes down, and stands on the level ground.
- The height is named as sonship, reached by giving without return (6:35).
- Empty now, filled later. The measure poured over into the bosom.
- The house dug deep to the rock is not shaken.
- Casting out turns inward, on one's own eye.

**4. Reception: the unworthy receive, and the dead are raised (7:1–8:56).**
- The centurion, "not worthy". The widow's son, "be raised". "A great prophet has been raised."
- The woman, loving in the measure of what is let go.
- The four soils: the way, the rock, the midst, the good earth.
- What only seems one's own is taken.
- In the storm, he is raised from sleep. Legion is clothed and seated.
- Two daughters: what runs out stands, and "child, rise".

**5. Giving out what was received, the naming, and the mountain of glory (9:1–62).**
- His power is given to the twelve. The feeding, at the declining of the day: he looks up into heaven, breaks, gives.
- Faith names him and is silenced. "He must suffer, be rejected, be killed, and be raised."
- "Deny himself, take up the cross daily, follow".
- He goes up the mountain to pray. His face changes, two men in glory, the cloud, "hear him". He comes down.
- *The taking up is named, and the face set to Jerusalem (9:51).*

**6. The road (10:1–19:27).** This is the longest state. The text develops it by stages that follow its own returns.

- **a. Sent ahead; the kingdom has come near (10).**
  - Lambs among wolves, with nothing. Peace given first.
  - Names written in heaven.
  - The veil, thanked for. All things handed over to him.
  - Love with the whole; "do this and you will live".

- **b. Prayer, and the own disarmed (11).**
  - Asking. The friend inside rises to give.
  - The strong man disarmed and his spoils distributed.
  - The inside cleansed by giving it out.
  - The outside and the inside have one maker.

- **c. The own's possessions and its anxiety (12).**
  - The rich man's barns, and his soul demanded back.
  - God feeds the ravens; no one adds to his height.
  - (12:28a: God clothes the lilies.)
  - The lord who serves those who watch.
  - Division in the one house.

- **d. Straightened and loosed; the door; called higher (13–14).**
  - The bent woman straightened and loosed.
  - The narrow door, shut by the master who rises.
  - *Unprinted, and the printed text needs it:* "go up higher", said to the one in the last place (14:7–11).
  - The poor brought into the dinner.

- **e. Sought and found (15).**
  - The shepherd goes after the lost and carries it home.
  - *Unprinted:* the turn from inside — coming into oneself, rising, going to the father, living again (15:11–32, test).

- **f. What is owed, let go; the height of men refused (16).**
  - The steward lets go what is owed.
  - "What is high among men is abomination before God."
  - The poor man is carried up by angels.
  - Moses and the prophets must be heard.

- **g. Cleansed on the way, turning back, rising (17).**
  - "Through the midst", the sentence of 4:30, again.
  - Cleansed as they went. The foreigner turns back. "Rising, go."
  - The kingdom within you.
  - On the roof, do not go down to take; in the field, do not turn back.
  - Taken along, or left.

- **h. Brought low and lifted; looking up (18).**
  - Two go up to pray. The one who did not lift his eyes goes down justified.
  - Received as a child.
  - The rich ruler lacks one thing.
  - The blind man beside the way looks up and follows.

- **i. Up to see, down to receive (19:1–27).**
  - Zacchaeus goes up to see, is called down, and gives.
  - "Today salvation has come to this house."
  - The kingdom is received by going away and returning.

*Across the road, the development is the same each time:*
- the own claims, and is emptied (the strong man, the rich man, the ruler, the elder son, test);
- the one brought low is received, straightened, carried, or called up (the bent woman, the lost, Lazarus, the tax collector, Zacchaeus);
- the rising given goes toward giving.

The road moves inward:
- the kingdom "has come near" (10:9);
- it "has arrived" (11:20);
- "it is within you" (17:21).

**7. Arrival, rejection, the outward laid waste (19:28–21:38).**
- *Unprinted, and the printed text needs it:* the arrival — going up, the descent of the Mount of Olives with "peace in heaven, and glory in the highest", the weeping over the city whose peace is hidden from its eyes, the house of prayer (19:28–46).
- The heads of the church named in 9:22 reject him.
- "Give back to God what is God's." The resurrection named: sons of God, equal to angels.
- The right hand.
- *Unprinted, and the printed text needs it:* the adorned house thrown down, stone from stone (21:5–6).
- Jerusalem surrounded, its desolation near, trodden like the seed beside the way.
- *While the outside falls, "straighten up and lift up your heads, because your redemption draws near" (21:28). It is the bent woman's word (13:11), given to all.* Stay awake, pray, "stand before the Son of Man" (21:36).

**8. The upper room, the will, the lowest point (22).**
- The Passover in a room "above the ground" (ἀνάγαιον).
- The body given, the blood poured out.
- On the mountain of prayer, on his knees: "not my will".
- *Unprinted, and the printed text needs it:* faith's failure in the strong man's courtyard, and its return by remembering (22:54–62).
- Held fast, covered, mocked.
- *At the lowest point: "from now on, at the right hand of the power of God".*

**9. The cross at the head, the veil torn, the spirit given back (23).**
- The rulers of the first verse receive him and send him "up" between them.
- The loosing is exchanged: the bound murderer loosed, the Loosener bound.
- The cross at the Skull, the place of the head.
- *Unprinted, and the printed text leans on it:* of the two beside him, one is taken: "today, with me" (23:39–43).
- The veil torn in the middle.
- His loud voice, against the falsity's loud voice of 4:33: "Father, into your hands".
- Laid where no one had been laid.

**10. Dawn at the deep: the Living One, recognized, in the midst (24).**
- At deep dawn, at the rock: "why do you seek the Living One among the dead?"
- Remembering lifts the veil of 9:45.
- The road away from Jerusalem, walked with him. Eyes held.
- "Enter into his glory." "Remain with us": the day declined.
- He reclines, breaks, gives; eyes opened; no longer seen.
- *Unprinted, and the printed text needs it:* "rising, they returned to Jerusalem… he has appeared to Simon" (24:33–35).
- He stands in their midst: "Peace to you". Hands and feet: "it is I myself".
- *Unprinted, and the printed text needs it:* the mind opened to the scriptures — the last stage of the veil, and the meeting of 16:31 (24:44–46).
- *Unprinted, and the printed text needs it:* clothed with power "from the height"; hands lifted, blessing; carried up into heaven — the taking up named at 9:51; the return to Jerusalem, to the house of prayer, with joy (24:48–53).

### How the states develop

The reading does not find a ladder of separate levels. It finds one movement repeated at a greater depth each time, in three places.

**On the mountain.**
- *He prays through the night* (6:12).
- *His face changes in glory* (9:29).
- *He kneels and yields his will* (22:41–42).
- *(test) He is carried up while blessing* (24:51).
- After each, he comes down to the sick, or is handed over, or is gone from sight. And each time something more of the own is laid down: the night, the face, the will, the body.

**In the midst.**
- The person is thrown in, let down, and made to stand there.
- The Divine passes through and, at the end, stands there.

**Under the veil.**
- What cannot be received is covered.
- The covering is put over him and torn.
- Remembering, the bread, and (test) the opening of the mind uncover what was covered.

Each stage develops into the next the same way: by a going down — of the Divine into what does not receive him, and of the person into the last place — and by a raising that is given and goes toward giving. The own's height is refused at every stage, and what is raised is what has received.

## 5. What this answer rests on that is not printed

The answer leans on unprinted passages at the points the reading marked as "needed". There the printed words point to something missing, and the inner mechanics break without it:
- **4:17–21.** The reading of the Word: "proclaim release", "today fulfilled".
- **14:1–11.** "Go up higher."
- **19:28–46.** The arrival.
- **21:5–6.** The adorned house thrown down.
- **22:54–62.** Faith's denial and return.
- **24:33–35.** The return to Jerusalem; the appearance to Simon.
- **24:44–46.** The mind opened to the scriptures.
- **24:48–53.** The taking up told.

Without them the printed text still carries the whole movement in its own words:
- the descent (4:31);
- the taking up named (9:51);
- the mountain (9:28);
- the law of 18:14;
- the right hand (22:69);
- the veil torn (23:45);
- the eyes opened (24:31);
- the standing in the midst (24:36).

But it does not complete it:
- it names the taking up and does not tell it;
- it goes toward Jerusalem and does not arrive;
- it announces faith's failure and return and does not show them;
- it veils the understanding and does not open it.
