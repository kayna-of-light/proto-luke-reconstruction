# Third pass: every remaining unprinted passage, by the inner test alone

*Run on the text as printed after the second pass (commit 6562c68). The witnesses come first: what they attest is printed (METHOD §4–5). The correspondential reading is the last pass. It weighs the parts the witnesses leave unattested or report absent. This pass finishes it.*

## The test

The first pass often closed a passage with "X → Y runs, so it does not close a gap". That is a test of the letter. Here every remaining unprinted passage is re-read at its place in the final text, and only the inner test decides. A passage is needed if either:

- **(a)** printed words have nothing to refer to or to answer without it; or
- **(b)** a movement the text sets going does not complete without it: two parties set up and only one met, a promise or a question left without its fulfilment.

A passage that continues the line but completes nothing is not printed. Under the premise it is consistent with the original. But the witnesses come first, and nothing in the text requires it.

## Needed: 15:11–32, the two sons (attested absent, Epiphanius sch. 42)

- **(a)** The printed 15:2 gives the grumbling two objects: "this man welcomes sinners *and eats with them*".
  - The sheep and the coin answer the welcoming.
  - Nothing printed answers the eating except "let us eat and celebrate" (15:23).
- **(a)** The printed 15:7 and 15:10 name the cause of joy: "one sinner who *repents*".
  - The sheep and the coin are found. They do not repent.
  - The turn the printed text names is shown only in "coming to himself… rising, I will go to my father" (15:17–20).
- **(b)** 15:1–2 sets two parties going: the sinners who draw near, and the Pharisees and scribes who grumble.
  - The printed parables meet the first.
  - The second is met only by the elder son, who "would not go in" and to whom the father comes out and pleads (15:28–32).
  - This is the same pattern that made 19:27 needed.
- **Mechanics.** It is the text's fullest statement of the ascent the reading traced:
  - the own takes its share and scatters it;
  - it comes to itself, rises (ἀναστάς, twice) and goes to the father;
  - it is seen while still far off;
  - it is clothed by the father (ἐνδύσατε: the thread of 12:22, 12:28a and 24:49);
  - "was dead and is alive again" answers "the Living One" (24:5).

**Needed.** Printed against Marcion's text.

## Result

The reading is finished.
- The text prints what the witnesses attest.
- Where they are silent or report absence, it prints what the inner narrative needs, and no more.
- The third pass adds 15:11–32.
- Every other unprinted passage continues the line but completes nothing, and is not printed.
