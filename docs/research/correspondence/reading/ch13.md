# Chapter 13 — Luke 15:1–10

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey (README §1).

## Inner sense

### The occasion [15:1–2 g; 15:3]

Grey: the parts in the outer rule's service and the strayed parts come near to hear truth [15:1–2 g]. The self-righteous keeping and its interpreting grumble: "This man welcomes sinners and eats with them." Truth answers with a parable [15:3].

### The lost sheep [15:4–7]

Which man with a hundred sheep, having lost one, does not leave the ninety-nine in the wilderness and go after the lost one until he finds it? [15:4]. When he finds it he lays it on his shoulders, rejoicing [15:5]. At home he calls his friends and neighbours: "Rejoice with me, for I have found my sheep that was lost" [15:6].

The seeker leaves the many in the open and goes after the one part of the soul that has strayed. He carries it home himself. "There will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous people who need no repentance" [15:7]. The inner degree rejoices more over one part that turns back than over all the parts that never left.

### The lost coin [15:8–10]

Which woman with ten silver coins, having lost one, does not light a lamp, sweep the house and search carefully until she finds it? [15:8]. When she finds it she calls her friends and neighbours to rejoice [15:9]. "There is joy before the angels of God over one sinner who repents" [15:10]. Inside its own house, the soul lights its lamp (cf. 8:16; 11:33) and sweeps its own state to find the one lost part of what it values. The source's messengers rejoice when it is found.

The two parables are a pair:
- a man outside in the wilderness, a woman inside the house;
- a living thing that has wandered off, a thing of value lost within (?).
