# Chapter 10 — Luke 12:1–5, 8–32, 35–59

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey · `[v ◊]` doubtful wording (README §1).

## Inner sense

### The leaven and what is hidden [12:1–3]

The multitude gathers in thousands, trampling one another, and truth speaks first to its following powers: "Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy" [12:1]. Leaven spreads hidden through the whole dough. The pretence of the self-righteous keeping spreads hidden through the whole soul.

Nothing covered will not be revealed [12:2]. What was said in the dark will be heard in the light, and what was whispered in private rooms will be proclaimed on the housetops [12:3]. What the soul hides in itself comes into the open.

### Whom to fear [12:4; 12:5 ◊; 12:8–9 ◊; 12:10–12]

Do not fear what kills the body and can do nothing more [12:4]: what can end only the outer form. Fear the one who, after killing, "has authority to cast into Gehenna" [12:5 ◊]. That is the power that decides the soul's whole state. The text does not say who that power is, and Gehenna is read here only as the place of destruction (?).

Everyone who acknowledges truth before people, the son of man will acknowledge "before God" [12:8 ◊]. Whoever denies it before people will be denied before God [12:9 ◊]. What the soul owns of truth in its outer parts, the inner form owns before the source.

A word against the son of man will be forgiven, but blasphemy against the holy spirit will not [12:10]. The soul may fail to recognise the inner form and still be released. Speaking against the source's own working in it refuses the very working that releases.

When the soul's assemblies, rulers and authorities bring the following powers to account, they need not worry about what to say [12:11]. The holy spirit will teach them in that hour [12:12].

### The inheritance and the rich fool [12:13–14; 12:15 g; 12:16; 12:17–18 g; 12:19–20; 12:21 g]

Someone asks truth to make his brother divide the inheritance [12:13]. "Man, who appointed me a judge or arbitrator over you?" [12:14]. Truth does not divide what the soul's natural parts hold between them. Grey: guard against all greed [12:15 g].

A rich man's land produces plentifully [12:16]. Grey: he plans to pull down his barns and build larger ones [12:17–18 g]. Then he says "to my soul: Soul, you have ample goods laid up for many years; relax, eat, drink, be merry" [12:19]. The text itself speaks to the soul. A part that has much tells the soul to settle in what it has stored.

"But God said to him, 'Fool! This night your soul is demanded of you; and the things you have prepared, whose will they be?'" [12:20]. In that night the soul is required of it, and what it stored is no longer its own. Grey: so it is with what stores up for itself and is not rich toward the source [12:21 g].

### Ravens and lilies [12:22–24; 12:25–26 g; 12:27 ◊; 12:28 ◊; 12:29 g; 12:30; 12:31–32 ◊]

Do not worry about life, what to eat, nor about the body, what to wear: life is more than food and the body more than clothing [12:22–23]. The ravens neither sow nor reap nor store, "and God feeds them" [12:24]. Grey: worry adds nothing [12:25–26 g]. The lilies neither spin nor weave, yet Solomon in all his glory was not clothed like one of them [12:27 ◊]. "How much more you, you of little faith!" [12:28 ◊].

The soul's anxious care is for its sustenance and its outer form. The source sustains what does not store, and clothes what does not make its own clothing. Grey: do not seek what to eat and drink [12:29 g]. The nations of the world seek these things, and "your Father knows that you need them" [12:30]. Seek the kingdom of God, and these things will be added [12:31 ◊]. "Do not be afraid, little flock, for it has pleased the Father to give you the kingdom" [12:32 ◊]. The rule from within is the source's gift.

### Waiting with lamps [12:35–37; 12:38 ◊; 12:39–40]

Let loins be girded and lamps burning [12:35]. Be like servants waiting for their master to return from the wedding feast, ready to open at once when he knocks [12:36]. The soul stays ready, its light burning, for the coming of its lord from the union.

Blessed are the servants whom the master finds awake [12:37]: "he will gird himself and have them recline at table, and he will come and serve them". The lord, finding the soul awake, serves it. If he comes "in the evening watch" and finds them so, they are blessed [12:38 ◊]. If the householder knew the thief's hour, he would not let his house be broken into [12:39]. "You also must be ready, for the son of man is coming at an hour you do not expect" [12:40].

### The steward of the household [12:41–48]

Faith asks whether the parable is for the following powers or for all [12:41]. "The Lord" answers with the faithful and wise steward, set over the household "to give them their portion of food at the proper time" [12:42]. This is the power set over the soul's household, giving each part its good when it is due. Blessed is that servant, and it will be set over all its master's possessions [12:43–44].

The servant that says "my master is delayed" beats the menservants and maidservants, eats, drinks and gets drunk [12:45]. The master comes on a day it does not expect, cuts it in pieces, and puts it with the unfaithful [12:46]. The power that thinks its lord is delayed abuses the soul's other parts and feeds itself, and it is divided when the lord comes.

- The servant that knew and did not act receives a severe beating [12:47].
- The servant that did not know receives a light one [12:48].
- From the one to whom much was given, much is required.

### Fire and division [12:49; 12:50 g; 12:51; 12:52 g; 12:53 ◊]

"I came to cast fire on the earth, and how I wish it were already kindled!" [12:49]. Truth casts fire into the outer degree, a kindling. Grey: truth has a baptism to be baptized with, and is constrained until it is accomplished [12:50 g].

Truth has not come to give peace on earth, "but rather division" [12:51]. Grey: five in one house are divided [12:52 g]. Father against son and son against father, mother against daughter and daughter against mother, mother-in-law against daughter-in-law [12:53 ◊]. The kindling divides the soul's household: what it inherited is set against what it brings forth, and what it brings forth against what it inherited.

### Reading the time; settling on the way [12:54–55 g; 12:56 ◊; 12:57; 12:58 ◊; 12:59]

Grey: the multitude reads the cloud in the west and the south wind [12:54–55 g]. "Hypocrites! You know how to interpret the appearance of heaven and earth, but why do you not know how to interpret the present time?" [12:56 ◊]. The soul can read signs in its inner and outer degrees, and cannot read the state it is in now. "Why do you not judge for yourselves what is right?" [12:57].

Going with your accuser before a magistrate, make an effort on the way to settle with him [12:58 ◊]. Otherwise he drags you to the judge, the judge hands you to the officer, and the officer throws you into prison. You will not get out until you have paid the last copper [12:59]. Settle with what accuses the soul while it is still on the way. Otherwise the account is fixed, and the soul is shut in until the whole debt is paid.
