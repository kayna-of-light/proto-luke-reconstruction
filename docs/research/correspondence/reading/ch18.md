# Chapter 18 — Luke 20:1–8, 19–36, 39–44

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey · `[v ◊]` doubtful wording (README §1).

## Inner sense

### By what authority [20:1; 20:2–3 g; 20:4–8]

Truth is teaching the people in the temple and proclaiming the good news [20:1]. Truth is now at the soul's centre of worship. The chief priests, the scribes and the elders come up: the soul's governing centre and its interpreting. Grey: they ask by what authority truth does these things, and truth answers with a question [20:2–3 g].

"Was the baptism of John from heaven or from men?" [20:4]. Did the preparing state come from the inner degree, or from the soul's natural parts?

They reason among themselves [20:5–7]:
- if they say "from heaven", truth will ask why they did not believe;
- if they say "from men", the people will stone them, "for they are convinced that John was a prophet";
- so they answer that they do not know.

The governing centre reasons from what follows for itself, and from fear of its own multitude, not from what is true. "Neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things" [20:8]. Truth gives no account of its authority to a question that does not seek truth.

### Seeking to seize [20:19]

The scribes and chief priests seek to lay hands on truth "at that very hour", but they fear the people [20:19]. The governing centre wants truth in its power and is held back by fear of its own multitude.

### Caesar's coin [20:20–23 g; 20:24–25; 20:26 g]

Grey: they watch truth and send spies who pretend to be sincere, to catch truth in its words and hand it over "to the authority and jurisdiction of the governor" [20:20–23 g]. The governing centre seeks to hand truth over to the outer rule. They ask whether it is lawful to give tribute to Caesar, and truth perceives their craftiness.

"Show me a denarius. Whose image and inscription does it have?" "Caesar's" [20:24]. "Then give back to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's" [20:25]. What bears the stamp of the outer rule belongs to it, and is given back to it. What belongs to the source is given to the source. The text does not say what that is. Grey: they cannot catch truth, and fall silent [20:26 g].

### The Sadducees and the risen state [20:27–31; 20:32 g; 20:33–34; 20:35 ◊; 20:36]

The Sadducees come, who deny that there is a resurrection [20:27]: the part of the soul that denies any life beyond the present state. They use the received order to make the risen state absurd [20:28–31; 20:32 g; 20:33]:
- Moses wrote that a man must take his dead brother's widow and raise up offspring;
- seven brothers in turn took one wife, and all died childless;
- "In the resurrection, therefore, whose wife will the woman be?"

A bearing part was joined in turn to a whole series of powers, and none of the unions brought anything forth.

"The sons of this age marry and are given in marriage" [20:34]. But those "whom God has deemed worthy" to attain that age and the resurrection from the dead neither marry nor are given in marriage [20:35 ◊]. "They cannot die any more, because they are equal to angels and are sons of God, being sons of the resurrection" [20:36]. Union by marriage belongs to the present state. Those in the risen state do not unite in that way, cannot die, are like the source's messengers, and are the source's offspring.

### David's son [20:39; 20:40 g; 20:41; 20:42–43 g; 20:44]

Some of the scribes say: "Teacher, you have spoken well" [20:39]. Grey: they no longer dare to ask anything [20:40 g].

Truth asks: "How can they say that the Anointed One is David's son?" [20:41]. Grey: David himself says in the Psalms, "The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies your footstool" [20:42–43 g]. "David thus calls him Lord, so how is he his son?" [20:44]. The soul's past kingship calls the inner form its lord. So the inner form is not that kingship's offspring.

At 18:38–39 the blind part called truth "son of David" and was given sight. Here truth itself raises the question about that name. The inner form does not come from the soul's inheritance, but stands over it.
