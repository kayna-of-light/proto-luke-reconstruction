# Chapter 1 — Luke 3:1; 4:31–37; 4:16, 23–24, 28–30; 4:40–44

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey · `[v ◊]` doubtful wording or order (README §1).

## Inner sense

### The soul as it is [3:1 ◊ junction]

The soul's time is counted from Caesar. Its states are measured by a rule that comes from outside it. Its centre (Judea) is governed by an appointee of that outside rule (Pilate), so the will is held by a principle that is not its own. Its outer degree (Galilee) and the regions around it are divided among three tetrarchs (Herod, Philip, Lysanias): the outer form is split into parts, each ruled by a partial power. Nothing in this soul is shaped from within. The source is not mentioned.

### Truth enters the outer degree [4:31 ◊ order, 4:32]

Truth "came down" into Capernaum, a settled structure of the outer degree. The direction is from above to below: the direction in which the source shapes. Truth teaches there "on the sabbaths", in the states in which the soul's own activity stops. The soul's thoughts are struck, because truth's word has authority: it carries its own power, unlike the teaching the soul has learned and repeats.

### The foreign part names the inner form [4:33–35; 4:36 g, 4:37 g]

In the assembly, where the soul keeps and repeats its learned teaching, lives a part occupied by something not its own: "the spirit of an unclean daimon". It cries with a loud voice and speaks as "we" [4:33–34]. It names truth twice. First by its outer name, "Jesus the Nazarene": truth as it appears, from its place of origin. Then by its inner form, "the holy one of God". The first thing in this soul to recognise the inner form is the foreign part, and it recognises it as its own end: "Have you come to destroy us?"

Truth rebukes it: "Be silent, and come out of him" [4:35]. Recognition without reception may not speak, and what is not the soul's own is put out. The occupant throws the man down "in their midst", in front of the whole assembly, and leaves him unharmed. Losing what was never the soul's own costs the soul nothing. The soul's thoughts see the word's power over what is unclean [4:36 g]. The knowledge of it spreads through the whole surrounding region of the outer degree [4:37 g].

### The inherited formation claims truth and expels it [4:16 ◊ order; 4:23; 4:24 g; 4:28 g; 4:29–30]

Truth comes to Nazara, "where he had been brought up": the soul's inherited formation. "As was his custom" it enters the assembly on the sabbath and stands up to read. It rises in the place of learned teaching, in the state of rest, to bring forward what is written there [4:16].

Truth itself speaks what the inherited formation will say: "Physician, heal yourself", and "whatever we heard was done at Capernaum, do here also in your home town" [4:23]. The inherited formation knows truth only as a healer, by its effects. It has heard what truth did in the outer structure and wants the same done in itself, as its due, without being changed. Truth says that no prophet is accepted in his home town [4:24 g]: the inherited formation cannot accept the voice that calls it back.

The assembly is filled with rage [4:28 g]. With the same movement in which truth rose to read, the inherited formation rises against it [4:29]. It drives truth out of its town, its settled thinking, and leads it to the brow of the hill on which that town is built, the foundation of its own structure, to throw it down. Truth passes through the midst of them and goes on its way [4:30]. The rejection does not touch it. The road begins from this rejection.

### Evening and day [4:40–43; 4:44 g]

"As the sun was setting": as a state closes, everything in the soul that is sick is brought to truth. Truth lays its hands on each one and heals them [4:40]. Its power to act reaches every infirmity singly.

Foreign occupants come out of many, shouting "You are the son of God" [4:41]. Truth rebukes them and does not allow them to speak, "because they knew that he was the Anointed One": truth on which love has been poured. The second time, as the first, the inner form is named only by the foreign parts, and they are silenced.

"When day came", in the new state, truth goes out to a deserted place, a state empty of the soul's structures [4:42]. The parts that were healed search for it and try to keep it from leaving: a gentler form of possession. Truth answers that it must bring the good news of the kingdom to the other towns as well, "because for this I was sent" [4:43]. Its task is every settled structure of the soul. It does not stay in the one that holds on to it. "I was sent" is the first time the text points to a source behind truth. Truth proclaims in the assemblies of Judea [4:44 g], so its word reaches the places where the centre keeps its teaching.
