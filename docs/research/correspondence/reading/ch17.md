# Chapter 17 — Luke 19:1–26

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey (README §1).

## Inner sense

### Zacchaeus [19:1 g; 19:2; 19:3–5 g; 19:6; 19:7 g; 19:8–10]

Grey: truth enters Jericho and is passing through [19:1 g]. There is a man named Zacchaeus, a chief tax collector, and rich [19:2]: the chief of the parts that serve the outer rule, holding much.

Grey [19:3–5 g]:
- he tries to see who truth is, and cannot because of the multitude, "because he was small in stature";
- he runs ahead and climbs a sycamore;
- truth looks up: "Zacchaeus, hurry and come down, for I must stay at your house today".

He hurries down and receives truth with joy [19:6]. Grey: all grumble that truth has gone to the house of a sinner [19:7 g]. He stands and says to "the Lord": "Look, Lord, half of my possessions I give to the poor, and if I have defrauded anyone of anything, I restore it fourfold" [19:8]. The rich part receives truth into its state and lets go of what it holds. It restores more than it took.

"Today salvation has come to this house, since he also is a son of Abraham" [19:9]. Its state is saved, and it belongs to the origin. "For the son of man came to seek and to save the lost" [19:10].

The rich ruler (18:18–23) and the rich chief tax collector stand side by side in the text:

| The rich ruler | The rich chief tax collector |
|---|---|
| a ruling part that has kept the commandments | a part in the outer rule's service, called a sinner |
| cannot let go | lets go with joy |

### The minas [19:11; 19:12 g; 19:13; 19:14–21 g; 19:22–23; 19:24–25 g; 19:26]

Because truth is near Jerusalem, and because they think the kingdom of God is about to appear at once, truth tells a parable [19:11]. Near the centre, the soul expects the rule from within to appear immediately.

Grey: a nobleman goes to a far country to receive a kingdom and then return [19:12 g]. He calls ten servants, gives them ten minas, and says, "Engage in business until I come" [19:13]. Each servant power is given something to work with until its lord returns. Grey [19:14–21 g]:
- his citizens hate him and send word that they do not want him to reign;
- on his return, the first servant has made ten minas, the second five, and they are set over ten cities and five;
- another has kept his mina laid away in a handkerchief, "for I was afraid of you, because you are a severe man".

"I will condemn you with your own words, you wicked servant! You knew that I was a severe man…?" [19:22]. "Why then did you not put my money in the bank…?" [19:23]. The power that kept what it was given unused, out of fear and an image of its lord as severe, is judged by that image. By its own picture of the lord, it should at least have put what it was given to work. Grey: its mina is given to the one who has ten [19:24–25 g]. "To everyone who has, more will be given; but from the one who has not, even what he has will be taken away" [19:26] (cf. 8:18).
