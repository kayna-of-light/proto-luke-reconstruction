# Chapter 21 — Luke 23:1–12, 18–25, 32–33, 44–46, 50–56

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey · `[v ◊]` doubtful wording (README §1).

## Inner sense

### Before the outer rule [23:1; 23:2 ◊; 23:3]

The whole company rises and brings truth before Pilate [23:1]. The soul's governing centre hands truth over to the outer rule that governs it from outside (3:1).

They accuse it [23:2 ◊] of:
- misleading the nation;
- "destroying the law and the prophets" (◊);
- forbidding tribute to Caesar;
- "turning away the women and the children" (◊);
- "saying that he himself is the Anointed One, a king".

Measured against the story, these charges are false:
- truth sent the cleansed parts to the order the soul received (5:14; 17:14), and pointed to "Moses and the prophets" (16:29–31);
- it told the soul to give back to the outer rule what bears its stamp (20:25);
- it did not call itself the Anointed One; faith did (9:20), and truth ordered silence.

The governing centre turns everything truth did into an offence against the outer rule.

Pilate asks: "Are you the King of the Jews?" "You say so" [23:3]. The outer rule asks with a name of rule in the outer degree. As with the council (22:70), truth gives the name back to the one who speaks it.

### Herod [23:4–6 g; 23:7–9; 23:10–12 g]

Grey: Pilate finds no guilt [23:4–6 g]. They insist that truth stirs up the people "from Galilee where he began even to this place". Learning that truth is a Galilean, Pilate asks.

Learning that truth belongs to Herod's jurisdiction, Pilate sends it to Herod, who is in Jerusalem at that time [23:7]. The outer rule over the centre passes truth to the partial ruler of the outer degree, where truth began. Herod is very glad: he has long wanted to see truth, and hopes to see some sign done by it [23:8]. The partial ruler wants a sign. He questions truth at length, and truth answers nothing [23:9]. Truth gives no sign to the outer rule's wish for a sign (cf. 11:29).

Grey [23:10–12 g]:
- the chief priests and scribes accuse truth vehemently;
- Herod and his soldiers mock it, dress it in splendid clothing, and send it back;
- Herod and Pilate become friends that day, though they had been enemies.

The outer rulers are reconciled in rejecting truth.

### Barabbas [23:18–19; 23:20–21 g; 23:22–23; 23:24 g; 23:25]

They all cry out together: "Away with this man, and release to us Barabbas" [23:18]: a man thrown into prison for an insurrection in the city, and for murder [23:19]. The soul's multitude chooses to free the part that rose against the order of its centre and killed. It chooses to be rid of truth. Grey: Pilate wants to release truth, and they shout "Crucify, crucify him!" [23:20–21 g].

A third time Pilate asks: "Why? What evil has he done? I have found in him no guilt deserving death" [23:22]. They press with loud cries that truth be crucified, "and their voices prevailed" [23:23]. Grey: Pilate decides to grant their demand [23:24 g]. He releases the one imprisoned for insurrection and murder, "but he delivered Jesus over to their will" [23:25]. The outer rule finds no evil in truth three times, and yields to the soul's loud demand. Truth is handed over to the soul's will.

### The Skull [23:32–33]

Two others, criminals, are led away with truth to be put to death [23:32]. At the place called The Skull, they crucify truth with the criminals, one on its right and one on its left [23:33]. Truth is led out with two parts that broke the order. At the place named for the bare head of the dead (?), it is fixed between them.

### Darkness, the curtain, the spirit given back [23:44–46]

It is about the sixth hour, and darkness covers the whole land until the ninth [23:44]. In the middle of the day, darkness covers the whole soul. "The sun's light failed. And the curtain of the temple was torn in two" [23:45]. The soul's light fails. The curtain at its centre of worship, which closed off the inmost room from the rest, is torn in two: the inmost lies open.

Truth calls with a loud voice: "Father, into your hands I commit my spirit!" Having said this, it breathes its last [23:46]. Truth gives its living spirit back into the hands of the source, and its outer life ends.

### Joseph and the tomb [23:50–53; 23:54 g; 23:55–56]

There is a man named Joseph, a member of the council, good and righteous, who had not consented to their decision and action [23:50–51]. He is from Arimathea, and is "looking for the kingdom of God". Within the governing centre itself there is a part that did not consent, and that waits for the rule from within.

It goes to Pilate and asks for the body [23:52]. It takes the body down, wraps it in linen, and lays it in a tomb cut in stone "where no one had ever yet been laid" [23:53]. That part receives truth's outer form from the outer rule and lays it in a state of the soul never used before (?). Grey: it is the day of Preparation, and the sabbath is beginning [23:54 g].

The women who had come with truth from Galilee follow, and see the tomb and how the body is laid [23:55]. The affections that came with truth from the outer degree see where it is laid. They return and prepare spices and ointments, love made ready to be applied. On the sabbath they rest according to the commandment [23:56]: they keep the state of rest as the order commands.
