# Chapter 9 — Luke 11:1–23, 27–29, 33, 37–48, 52

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey · `[v ◊]` doubtful wording (README §1).

## Inner sense

### Teach us to pray [11:1; 11:2–3 ◊; 11:4]

Truth is praying. When it has finished, one of its following powers says: "Lord, teach us to pray, as John also taught his disciples" [11:1]. The soul's learning powers ask to be taught how to turn to the source, as the preparing state taught its own.

"When you pray, say: Father" [11:2 ◊]. The soul turns to the source as Father, as Divine Love. It asks:
- "may your holy spirit come": that the living working of the source come into it;
- "may your kingdom come": that the source's rule from within come;
- "give us each day your bread for the coming day" [11:3 ◊]: the source's own good, day by day;
- "forgive us our sins, for we ourselves forgive everyone indebted to us" [11:4]: release, as the soul itself releases;
- "do not bring us into temptation": that it not be led into trial.

### The friend at midnight; the father's gifts [11:5; 11:6 g; 11:7–9; 11:10 g; 11:11–13]

At midnight, the soul's darkest state, a part goes to a friend and asks for three loaves [11:5]. Grey: a friend has come to it from a journey and it has nothing to set before him [11:6 g]. From inside comes the answer that the door is shut and the children are in bed [11:7]. Yet because of the asking part's persistence, the friend gets up and gives whatever is needed [11:8].

Ask, seek, knock, and it will be given, found, opened [11:9; 11:10 g]. No father gives a snake for a fish or a scorpion for an egg [11:11–12]. "If you then, who are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will the Father from heaven give the holy spirit to those who ask him!" [11:13]. The source gives the soul its own living working when the soul asks. The prayer's first petition (11:2 ◊) is answered here.

### The stronger one [11:14–15; 11:16–17 g; 11:18–22; 11:23 g]

Truth casts out a foreign occupant that is mute. When it is gone, the mute speaks, and the multitude is amazed [11:14]. A part of the soul that could not speak is freed into speech. Some say truth casts out the occupants "by Beelzebul, the ruler of the daimons" [11:15]: they attribute truth's power to the ruler of the foreign occupation. Grey: others demand a sign from heaven, and truth answers that every kingdom divided against itself is laid waste [11:16–17 g].

"If Satan also is divided against himself, how will his kingdom stand?" [11:18]. The foreign rule is one, and it does not drive itself out. By whom do the asking parts' own sons cast them out? [11:19]. "But if I cast out the daimons by the finger of God, then the kingdom of God has come upon you" [11:20]. The finger is the least extension of the source's power. Where even that drives out the foreign rule, the source's rule has reached that part of the soul.

A strong man, fully armed, guards his own court, and his goods are safe [11:21]. When one stronger attacks and overcomes him, he takes away the armour he trusted in and divides his plunder [11:22]. The foreign rule holds its court in the soul as its own. Truth is the stronger, which takes from it what it relied on. Grey: whoever is not with truth is against it [11:23 g].

### The womb and the word [11:27–28]

A woman in the multitude calls out: "Blessed is the womb that bore you and the breasts that you sucked!" [11:27]. A part of the soul blesses truth's natural origin. "Blessed rather are those who hear the word of God and keep it!" [11:28]. As at 8:21, truth turns the blessing from natural origin to hearing and keeping.

### No sign [11:29 ◊]

As the multitude grows, truth says: "This generation is an evil generation; it seeks a sign, and no sign will be given to it" [11:29 ◊]. A soul that asks for a sign before it will receive is given none.

### The lamp [11:33]

No one puts a lamp in a cellar or under a basket, but on a lampstand, so that those who come in may see the light [11:33]. This repeats 8:16: what truth lights is there to light the whole state.

### Inside and outside [11:37; 11:38 ◊; 11:39–41; 11:42 ◊; 11:43; 11:44–45 g; 11:46–48, 52]

A part of the self-righteous keeping asks truth to dine, and truth goes in and reclines [11:37]. The host wonders why truth did not wash first [11:38 ◊]. "The Lord" says [11:39–41]:
- the keeping cleans the outside of the cup and dish, while inside it is full of greed and wickedness;
- "Did not the one who made the outside make the inside also?";
- "give as alms what is within, and behold, everything is clean for you".

The text names the two degrees itself. The self-righteous keeping cleans the outer and leaves the inner full of grasping. Truth says the source made both. Let what is within be given out, and everything is clean.

The woes against the keeping:
- It tithes mint and rue and every herb, the least things exactly, and passes over "the calling and the love of God" [11:42 ◊].
- It loves the best seats and the greetings [11:43].
- Grey: it is like unmarked graves, death walked over unknowingly [11:44 g].

A lawyer says the interpreting is insulted too [11:45 g]. The woes against the interpreting:
- It loads the soul with burdens and does not touch them with one finger [11:46]: it lays the received order on the soul as weight and does not carry it itself.
- It builds the tombs of the prophets whom the fathers killed, and so approves the fathers' deeds [11:47–48]: it honours the dead prophetic voice in monuments while continuing what killed it.
- "You have taken away the key of knowledge; you did not enter yourselves, and you hindered those who were entering" [11:52]: it holds the key that opens knowledge, and neither enters nor lets in.
