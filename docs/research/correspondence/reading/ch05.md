# Chapter 5 — Luke 8:1–8, 16–18, 20–25

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey · `[v ◊]` doubtful wording (README §1).

## Inner sense

### The women who provide [8:1 g; 8:2–3]

Truth goes through towns and villages proclaiming the kingdom, with the twelve [8:1 g]. With them go women who have been healed of evil spirits and illnesses [8:2–3]:
- Mary called Magdalene, "from whom seven daimons had gone out";
- Joanna, the wife of Herod's steward;
- Susanna;
- many others.

They provide for truth and its powers out of their own means. Affections of the soul that were freed from foreign occupation now provide for truth from what they have. One of them comes from the household of the partial ruler of the outer degree. The number seven cannot be derived from the text (?).

### The sower [8:4; 8:5–7 g; 8:8]

As a great multitude gathers from town after town, truth speaks in a parable [8:4]. Grey: a sower sows his seed [8:5–7 g]:
- some falls on the path, is trampled, and the birds of the sky eat it;
- some falls on rock and withers for lack of moisture;
- some falls among thorns, which grow up with it and choke it.

Some falls into good soil, and when it grows it produces a hundredfold [8:8]. What is sown in the soul grows according to the ground that receives it:
- a ground hardened by traffic, where it is trampled and carried off;
- a ground without depth or moisture;
- a ground already occupied by other growth;
- a ground that receives.

Only the receiving ground brings it to fullness. "Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear": the whole matter is the soul's hearing. The interpretation of the parable is not in the printed text, and the reading stays with its images.

### The lamp and hearing [8:16–18]

No one lights a lamp and hides it under a jar or a bed; it is put on a lampstand, "so that those who come in may see the light" [8:16]. What truth lights in the soul is there to make the soul's whole state visible to everything that enters it. Nothing hidden will not be made manifest [8:17]: what the soul hides in itself will come to light.

"So take care how you hear" [8:18]. To whoever has, more is given. From whoever does not have, even what it thinks it has is taken. The soul's hearing decides whether what it receives grows or is lost.

### Mother and brothers [8:20; 8:21 ◊]

Truth is told that its mother and brothers are standing outside and want to see it [8:20]. Truth answers: "Who is my mother, and who are my brothers, except those who hear my words and do them?" [8:21 ◊]. The soul's natural origin stands outside and wants to see truth. Truth's kin are the parts of the soul that hear and do.

### The storm on the lake [8:22–25]

Truth gets into a boat with its following powers: "Let us go across to the other side of the lake" [8:22]. The soul's understanding, carrying truth, sets out over its mass of knowing toward the other side. As they sail, truth falls asleep [8:23]. A storm comes down on the water; the boat fills and they are in danger. When truth seems inactive, the soul's thoughts rise and flood the structure that carries it.

The powers wake truth: "Master, Master, we are perishing!" [8:24]. Truth rebukes the wind and the raging water, as it rebuked the foreign occupants, and there is a calm. "Where is your faith?" [8:25]. Afraid and amazed, the powers ask one another: "Who then is this, that he commands even the winds and the water, and they obey him?" They begin to ask what truth is.
