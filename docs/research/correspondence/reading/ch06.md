# Chapter 6 — Luke 8:26–9:17

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey · `[v ◊]` doubtful wording (README §1).

## Inner sense

### Legion [8:26 g; 8:27–28; 8:29 g; 8:30–32; 8:33–39 g]

They sail to the region of the Gerasenes, "which is opposite Galilee" [8:26 g]: across the water, a region foreign to the soul's order. As truth steps on land, a man from the town meets it, occupied by daimons [8:27]. For a long time he has worn no clothes and has not lived in a house, but among the tombs. A part of the soul's settled structure is so occupied that it has no outer form and no state of life of its own. It dwells where life is absent.

Seeing truth, it cries out and falls down: "What have I to do with you, Jesus, son of the Most High God? I beg you, do not torment me" [8:28]. Again the foreign occupant is the first to name the inner form, and it fears that inner form as its torment. Grey: it has broken every chain, and is driven into the wilderness [8:29 g].

Truth asks its name: "Legion", "because many daimons had entered him" [8:30]. The occupation is many and organised like the outer rule's army. The occupants beg not to be sent into the abyss [8:31]. A large herd of pigs is feeding on the hillside, and they beg to enter them; truth gives permission [8:32]. Pigs are what the law the soul keeps counts unclean, and they feed on anything: the soul's unclean appetites (?).

Grey [8:33–39 g]:
- the occupants enter the herd, and it rushes down into the lake and drowns: the occupation goes down with the appetites into the depths;
- the people find the man sitting at truth's feet, clothed and in his right mind, and they are afraid and ask truth to leave;
- the man begs to stay with truth and is sent home to tell "how much God has done for you", and proclaims "how much Jesus had done for him".

### The bleeding woman [8:40–41 g; 8:42–44; 8:45 ◊; 8:46; 8:47 g; 8:48]

Grey: on truth's return, the leader of an assembly, Jairus, falls at its feet [8:40–41 g]. He has an only daughter of about twelve, and she is dying [8:42]. As truth goes, the multitude presses in.

A woman has suffered a flow of blood for twelve years. She has spent all she had on physicians and no one could heal her [8:43]. Life has been flowing out of a bearing part of the soul for a complete period, and all its means went to other healers. She comes up behind truth and touches the fringe of its cloak, the outermost edge of truth's outer form, and the flow stops at once [8:44].

"Who touched me?" [8:45 ◊]. All deny it. Faith says: "Master, the crowds are surrounding you and pressing in on you." Truth answers: "Someone touched me; for I know that power has gone out from me" [8:46]. Of all the parts pressing on truth, only the one that touches in faith receives its power. Pressing is not touching. Grey: the woman comes trembling and declares it before all [8:47 g]. "Daughter, your faith has saved you; go in peace" [8:48]. Truth calls the bearing part "daughter": it is received as offspring.

### Jairus' daughter [8:49–56 g]

Grey. Word comes that the daughter is dead [8:49–56 g]:
- "Do not be afraid; only believe."
- Truth takes faith and its two nearest powers, with the father and the mother, into the house.
- "She is not dead but asleep", and they laugh.
- Truth takes her hand: "Child, get up!" Her spirit returns, and she is given something to eat.
- They are to tell no one.

The young bearing state of the part that leads the assembly dies at the age of completeness. Truth calls its death sleep and wakes it, and it is fed. The text gives the same twelve years to the daughter's life and to the woman's loss (?).

### The twelve sent out [9:1–3; 9:4 g; 9:5–6]

Truth calls the twelve together and gives them power and authority over all daimons, and to cure diseases [9:1]. It sends them to proclaim the kingdom and to heal [9:2]. Truth gives its own power to the soul's chosen powers and sends them through the soul.

They take nothing [9:3]: no staff, no bag, no bread, no money, no second tunic. The powers go out with nothing of their own. Grey: they stay in the house they enter [9:4 g]. Where they are not welcomed, they shake the dust of that town off their feet as a testimony [9:5]: a structure that does not receive keeps its own dust, and the powers carry nothing of it away. They go through the villages bringing the good news and healing [9:6].

### Herod's perplexity [9:7–8; 9:9 g]

Herod the tetrarch, the partial ruler of the outer degree, hears of all that is happening and is perplexed [9:7–8]. It is said that John has been raised from the dead, or that Elijah has appeared, or that one of the ancient prophets has arisen. The outer rule can explain truth only by what it already knows: a return of the preparing state, or of the prophetic voice. Grey: "John I beheaded; but who is this…?", and Herod tries to see truth [9:9 g]. The outer rule had cut off the head of the preparing state.

### The feeding [9:10–11 g; 9:12–14; 9:15 g; 9:16 ◊; 9:17]

Grey: the sent powers return; truth withdraws with them, the multitude follows, and truth heals them [9:10–11 g].

The day begins to decline: a state is closing [9:12]. The twelve ask truth to send the multitude away, to find lodging and food in the villages around, "for we are here in a deserted place". The powers would send the soul's multitude back to feed itself from its own settled structures. Truth says: "You give them something to eat" [9:13]. They have only five loaves and two fish. There are about five thousand, and truth has them sit in groups of about fifty [9:14; 9:15 g].

Truth takes the five loaves and the two fish, looks up to heaven, blesses over them, breaks them, and gives them to the following powers to set before the multitude [9:16 ◊]:
- it takes the little that the powers have;
- it turns it toward the source;
- it blesses it, breaks it, and gives it out through the powers.

All eat and are filled, and twelve baskets of broken pieces are left over [9:17]. The good that sustains the will goes out from the source through truth and through the soul's powers to the whole multitude, with a complete measure left.
