# Chapter 22 — Luke 24:1–11, 13–23, 25–26, 28–31, 36–43, 47

## Inner sense

### The tomb at dawn [24:1; 24:2 g; 24:3–5; 24:6 ◊; 24:7; 24:8 g; 24:9; 24:10 g; 24:11]

On the first day of the week, at early dawn, the affections go to the tomb with the spices they have prepared [24:1]. At the opening of a new state, they bring their love, ready to apply, to the place where life was absent. Grey: they find the stone rolled away [24:2 g]. Going in, they do not find "the body of the Lord Jesus" [24:3]: the outer form is not where it was laid.

While they are at a loss, two men in dazzling clothing stand by them [24:4]. Afraid, the women bow their faces to the ground. The men say: "Why do you seek the Living One among the dead?" [24:5]. The source's messengers give the inner form its name here: "the Living One". Truth is life itself, and it is not in the place of the dead.

"He has been raised. Remember all that he told you while he was still in Galilee" [24:6 ◊]: remember what truth said in the outer degree. "The son of man must be delivered into the hands of sinful men and be crucified and on the third day rise" [24:7]. Grey: they remember [24:8 g].

Returning, they tell all this to the eleven and all the rest [24:9]. The eleven are the complete set of sent powers, less the one that handed truth over. Grey: the women are Mary Magdalene, Joanna, Mary the mother of James, and the others [24:10 g]. To the powers, "these words seemed … an idle tale, and they did not believe them" [24:11]. The soul's understanding treats what its affections bring from the empty tomb as nonsense.

### Emmaus [24:13; 24:14 g; 24:15–16; 24:17 g; 24:18–19; 24:20 g; 24:21 ◊; 24:22–23 g; 24:25 ◊; 24:26; 24:28–29 g; 24:30–31]

That same day two of them are going to a village called Emmaus, about sixty stadia (?) from Jerusalem [24:13]. Two powers walk away from the centre. Grey: they talk with each other about all that has happened [24:14 g].

Truth itself comes near and walks with them [24:15], but their eyes are held from recognising it [24:16]. The understanding of these powers does not see truth, though it walks beside them. Grey: truth asks what they are discussing, and they stand still, looking sad [24:17 g].

Cleopas answers: "Are you the only visitor to Jerusalem who does not know the things that have happened there in these days?" [24:18]. "What things?" They describe truth as they know it [24:19]: "Concerning Jesus of Nazareth, a man who was a prophet mighty in deed and word before God and all the people". They know truth by its outer name and its outer power. Grey: they tell how the chief priests and rulers handed truth over to be condemned to death and crucified [24:20 g].

"But we thought that he was the one to redeem Israel" [24:21 ◊]. What they thought truth was for, the setting free of the soul's people, seems ended, "and it is now the third day". Grey: the women amazed them, having seen a vision of angels who said truth was alive [24:22–23 g].

Truth says: "O foolish ones, and slow of heart to believe all that he spoke to you!" [24:25 ◊]. The slowness is in the will, the heart. The powers were told and did not take it in. "Was it not necessary that the Anointed One should suffer these things and enter into his glory?" [24:26]. Truth names its inner form, and its way through suffering into glory.

Grey: they come near the village. Truth makes as if to go on. They press it: "Stay with us, for it is toward evening". Truth goes in [24:28–29 g]. As a state closes, the powers ask truth to stay.

At table, truth takes the bread, blesses it, breaks it and gives it to them [24:30]. It is the act of the feeding (9:16) and of the supper (22:19). "And their eyes were opened, and they recognized him. And he vanished from their sight" [24:31]. Recognition comes in the act of being given the good, not in being told. Once truth is recognised, the outer form is no longer seen.

### Truth among them [24:36 g; 24:37–39 ◊; 24:40 g; 24:41; 24:42–43 g]

Grey: as they are speaking, truth stands among them and says, "Peace to you!" [24:36 g]. They are startled and afraid, and think they see a spirit [24:37]. "Why are you troubled, and why do doubts arise in your hearts?" [24:38]. The doubt arises in the will.

"See my hands and my feet, that it is I myself; for a spirit does not have bones as you see that I have" [24:39 ◊]. Truth shows its hands, its power to act, and its feet, its contact with the outermost. It is itself, with structure (bones), not an appearance. The outer form of truth is real. Grey: truth shows them its hands and feet [24:40 g].

While they still disbelieve for joy and marvel, truth asks: "Have you anything here to eat?" [24:41]. Truth asks to receive from the soul. Grey: they give it a piece of broiled fish, and it eats before them [24:42–43 g].

### Proclaimed to all the nations [24:47]

"… and that repentance for the forgiveness of sins should be proclaimed in his name to all the nations" [24:47]. The turning of the soul's strayed parts, and their release, is to be announced in truth's name to all the nations: to every people and part of the soul. The printed text ends here.
