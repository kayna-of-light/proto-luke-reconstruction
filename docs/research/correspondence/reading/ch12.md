# Chapter 12 — Luke 14:12–24

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey · `[v ◊]` doubtful wording (README §1).

## Inner sense

### Whom to invite [14:12; 14:13 g; 14:14]

Truth speaks "to the man who had invited him" [14:12]: the part of the soul that received truth at its table. When it gives a meal, it is not to invite its friends, brothers, relatives or rich neighbours, "in case they invite you in return and you are repaid". The soul's feasting with what it is already joined to, or with what is full, is exchange.

Grey: invite the poor, the crippled, the lame, the blind [14:13 g]. "And you will be blessed, because they cannot repay you; for you will be repaid at the resurrection of the righteous" [14:14]. Giving to what is lacking, which cannot return anything, is repaid when the righteous parts of the soul rise.

### The great banquet [14:15 g; 14:16–24]

Grey: one at table says, "Blessed is anyone who will eat bread in the kingdom of God!" [14:15 g]. Truth answers with a parable [14:16]: a man gives a great banquet and invites many. At the hour he sends his servant: "Come, for everything is now ready" [14:17]. The source prepares its feast and calls the soul's parts that are already invited.

They all make excuses [14:18–20]:
- one has bought a field and must see it;
- one has bought five yoke of oxen and must try them;
- one has married a wife and cannot come.

The invited parts are bound to what they have acquired (land, working strength) and to their own union. They decline the source's feast.

The master of the house, angry, sends the servant into the streets and lanes of the town for "the poor and the crippled and the blind and the lame" [14:21]. These are the parts of the soul's settled structure that are lacking and unable. There is still room [14:22]. He sends the servant out again, into the roads and hedgerows, the soul's outermost and unordered parts: "compel people to come in, so that my house may be filled" [14:23]. "None of those men who were invited will taste my banquet" [14:24]. The source fills its house with what lacks. What was invited and stayed with its own does not taste it.

## Names

None of the central figure in this chapter. "The master of the house" in the parable is the source.
