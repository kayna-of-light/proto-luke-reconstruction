# Chapter 14 — Luke 16:1–31

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey · `[v ◊]` doubtful wording (README §1).

## Inner sense

### The steward [16:1 g; 16:2; 16:3 g; 16:4–7; 16:8 g; 16:9; 16:10 g; 16:11; 16:12 ◊; 16:13]

Grey: a rich man has a steward who is accused of squandering his property [16:1 g]. "Give an account of your stewardship, for you can no longer be steward" [16:2]. The power set over the soul's household, which has used what is not its own as if it were, comes to the end of its holding. Grey: it cannot dig, and is ashamed to beg [16:3 g].

It decides what to do, "so that when I am removed from the stewardship, people will receive me into their houses" [16:4]. It calls the master's debtors one by one [16:5–7]:
- the one who owes a hundred measures of oil writes fifty;
- the one who owes a hundred measures of wheat writes eighty.

Before its holding ends, it releases part of what others owe. It gives away what it held so that it will be received. Grey: the master praises it for acting shrewdly [16:8 g].

"Make friends for yourselves by means of the mammon of injustice, so that when it fails they may receive you into the eternal dwellings" [16:9]. What the soul holds as its own is to be used to make friends, so that when the holding fails, the soul is received into lasting states. Grey: faithful in little, faithful in much [16:10 g].

- If the soul has not been faithful in the unjust mammon, who will entrust it with the true riches? [16:11].
- If it has not been faithful in "what belongs to another", who will give it "what is mine"? [16:12 ◊]. All that the soul holds belongs to another, to the source. Faithfulness with that is the condition for receiving what is truth's own.

No servant can serve two masters; "You cannot serve God and mammon" [16:13]. The soul cannot serve the source and its own holding at once.

### Justifying before people [16:14–16; 16:17 ◊; 16:18]

The self-righteous keeping, "lovers of money", hears this and mocks [16:14]. Truth: "You are those who justify yourselves before people, but God knows your hearts; for what is exalted among people is an abomination before God" [16:15]. The source knows the will (hearts). What the soul's outer judgement exalts, the source abhors.

"The law and the prophets were until John; since then the kingdom of God is proclaimed as good news, and everyone forces his way into it" [16:16]. The order the soul received and the prophetic voice ran until the preparing state. Since then the rule from within is announced, and every part presses into it.

"It is easier for heaven and earth to pass away than for one stroke of my words to fall" [16:17 ◊]. Truth's words stand firmer than the inner and outer degrees as they are. Whoever divorces his wife and marries another commits adultery, as does whoever marries a woman divorced from her husband [16:18]. A power that leaves the union it is in and joins another joins with what is not its own (?).

### The rich man and Lazarus [16:19–22; 16:23 ◊; 16:24–31]

A rich man is clothed in purple and fine linen and feasts splendidly every day [16:19]. At his gate lies a poor man named Lazarus, covered with sores [16:20], longing to be fed with what falls from the rich man's table, while the dogs lick his sores [16:21]. Two states of the soul:
- one clothes itself in splendour and takes in fullness every day;
- at its gate lies a part with nothing, wounded, longing for what falls.

The poor one dies and is carried by the angels to Abraham's bosom [16:22]: the source's messengers carry the part that claimed nothing to the origin. The rich one dies and is buried. In Hades, in torment, he lifts his eyes and sees Abraham far off, and Lazarus "in his bosom" [16:23 ◊]. "Father Abraham, have mercy on me, and send Lazarus to dip the tip of his finger in water and cool my tongue, for I am in anguish in this flame" [16:24].

"Child, remember that you received your good things in your lifetime, and Lazarus likewise bad things; but now he is comforted here, and you are in anguish" [16:25]. A great chasm is fixed between them, so that none can cross either way [16:26]. When their state ends, each part is where its love has set it, and the states cannot pass into each other.

The rich one asks that Lazarus be sent to his father's house, to warn his five brothers [16:27–28]. "They have Moses and the prophets; let them listen to them" [16:29]. "No, father Abraham; but if someone goes to them from the dead, they will repent" [16:30]. "If they do not listen to Moses and the prophets, neither will they be convinced if someone rises from the dead" [16:31]. The full state asks for a sign for its kin. The answer: the received order and the prophetic voice are enough. If those are not heard, a return from the dead will not convince either.
