# Chapter 2 — Luke 5:1–38; 6:1–11

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey · `[v ◊]` doubtful wording (README §1).

## Inner sense

### The empty nets and the great catch [5:1 g; 5:2; 5:3–8 g; 5:9–11]

The soul's multitude presses on truth to hear "the word of God". Truth stands by the lake, the soul's mass of knowing in the outer degree (?) [5:1 g]. Two boats lie idle at the water's edge [5:2]. The fishermen have stepped out of them and are washing their nets: the soul's structures for gathering from its knowing lie unused, their work over and empty.

Truth gets into Simon's boat, the structure that belongs to faith, puts out a little from the land, and teaches the multitude from it [5:3 g]. It tells faith to put out into the deep and let down the nets [5:4 g]. Faith answers: "Master, we worked hard all night and caught nothing. But at your word I will let down the nets" [5:5 g]. By its own effort, in a dark state, faith gathered nothing. At truth's word it gathers a fullness its structures cannot hold: the nets begin to break, the partners are called, both boats fill until they begin to sink [5:6–7 g]. Faith sees its own state against what truth has done and falls at truth's knees: "Go away from me, Lord, for I am a sinful man" [5:8 g].

Amazement seizes faith and all the powers with it at the catch [5:9]: James and John too, the partners nearest to faith [5:10]. Truth says to faith: "Do not be afraid; from now on you will be catching people alive." Faith's work changes from gathering knowledge to gathering the soul's parts into life. The powers bring their boats to land, leave everything they held, and follow truth [5:11].

### The leper [5:12–14 ◊; 5:15–16 g]

In one of the towns there is a man full of leprosy: a part of the soul corrupted all through, and therefore separated from the rest [5:12]. Seeing truth, it falls on its face: "Lord, if you are willing, you can make me clean." The corrupted part names truth "Lord" in its humbling. Truth stretches out its hand, its power to act, and touches it: "I am willing; be made clean." The corruption leaves at once [5:13].

Truth tells it to say nothing, but to go to the priest and make the offering "as Moses commanded" [5:14 ◊]. The cleansed part returns to the order the soul has received and makes its offering there. Truth does not set that order aside; the cleansing is to stand as a testimony within it ("for you" ◊). Even so, the report spreads, and the multitude comes to hear and be healed [5:15 g]. Truth withdraws to deserted places and prays [5:16 g]: in states empty of the soul's structures it turns to its source.

### The paralysed man and the first self-naming [5:17–18; 5:19 g; 5:20–21; 5:22–23 g; 5:24 ◊; 5:25 g; 5:26]

While truth teaches, the soul's self-righteous keeping and its interpreting of the law sit there [5:17]. They have come from every village of the outer degree, from the centre and from Jerusalem: from every degree. "The power of the Lord was with him to heal." Men bring a man who is paralysed, a part of the soul that cannot move, carried by the effort of others [5:18]. Grey: they find no way through the multitude, go up on the roof, and let him down in front of truth [5:19 g].

Truth sees "their faith", the acknowledgement of the parts that carried him, and says: "Man, your sins are forgiven you" [5:20]. The cause of the paralysis is released first, inwardly. The interpreting part objects: "Who can forgive sins but God alone?" [5:21]. It knows that only the source releases, but does not recognise the source at work in truth. Grey: truth knows their thoughts and asks which is easier to say [5:22–23 g].

Truth then names its own inner form for the first time: "so that you may know that the son of man has authority on earth to forgive sins" [5:24 ◊]. The authority of the inner form reaches the outer degree, "on earth". Truth tells the paralysed part to get up, take up what it lay on, and go home [5:24]. The part rises, carries what had carried it, and returns to its own state of life [5:25 g]. The whole soul is struck and gives glory to the source: "We have seen strange things today" [5:26].

### Levi and the banquet [5:27; 5:28–29 g; 5:30–31; 5:32 g]

Truth sees a part sitting at its post in the service of the outer rule, collecting for it: Levi, the tax collector. Truth says to it, "Follow me" [5:27]. Grey: it leaves everything and follows, and holds a great banquet in its house, where many parts in the same service recline with truth [5:28–29 g].

The self-righteous keeping and its interpreting grumble to the following powers: why eat and drink with those in the outer rule's service and with those who have gone astray? [5:30]. Truth answers: "Those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick" [5:31]. Truth now takes up the name the inherited formation gave it at 4:23, physician, and gives it the opposite sense. It is not there to prove itself to those who claim it; it is there for the parts that need it. Grey: it came to call the strayed parts to turn, not the righteous [5:32 g].

### The bridegroom, the garment and the wine [5:33–38]

The preparing state (John) and the self-righteous keeping both practise abstention: fasting and prayers. Truth's following powers eat and drink [5:33]. Truth answers that the wedding guests cannot fast while the bridegroom is with them [5:34]. Truth is present as the bridegroom, the one who unites with the soul. Abstention belongs to the state in which the bridegroom is taken away: "then they will fast in those days" [5:35]. Truth here foretells its own removal.

No one tears a patch from a new garment to mend an old one: the new is torn and does not match the old [5:36]. No one puts new wine into old wineskins: the wine bursts the skins and both are lost [5:37]. New wine must go into new wineskins [5:38]. Living truth cannot be fitted into the soul's old forms, the outer form it shows and the forms that hold its truths. It needs new forms.

### The sabbath: grain and the withered hand [6:1–7; 6:8 g; 6:9; 6:10–11 g]

On a sabbath, in the state of rest, the following powers go through the grainfields, pluck the heads, rub them in their hands and eat: they take good directly from the field [6:1]. The self-righteous keeping objects that this is unlawful on the sabbath [6:2]. Truth answers with David [6:3–4]. When he and his companions were hungry, he entered the house of God and ate the bread of the presence, which is reserved for the priests. When it hungered, the soul's past kingship took the holy good and shared it. "The son of man is lord even of the sabbath" [6:5 ◊]: the inner form rules over rest itself.

On another sabbath truth enters the assembly and teaches. A man is there whose right hand is withered: the power to act, in its principal use, dried up [6:6]. The interpreting and the self-righteous keeping watch to see whether truth will heal on the sabbath, to find a charge against it [6:7]. Grey: truth calls the man into the middle [6:8 g]. Truth asks whether on the sabbath it is lawful to do good or to do harm, to save life or to destroy it [6:9]. It sets rest against the question of life. Grey: the hand is stretched out and restored, and the watchers are filled with fury and discuss what to do to truth [6:10–11 g]. This is the first time the soul's rule-keeping parts plan against truth.
