# Chapter 4 — Luke 7:1–28, 36–50

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey · `[v ◊]` doubtful wording (README §1).

## Inner sense

### The centurion's slave [7:1 g; 7:2; 7:3–8 g; 7:9 ◊; 7:10 g]

Truth enters Capernaum again [7:1 g]. A centurion has a slave who is ill and close to death, a slave he values highly [7:2]. Within the parts that serve the outer rule there is a part with authority of its own, and its servant part is dying.

Grey: the centurion sends the elders of the inherited people, who plead that he deserves it because he built their assembly [7:3–5 g]. Then he sends friends to say: "Lord, do not trouble yourself, for I am not worthy to have you come under my roof… But say the word, and let my servant be healed. For I too am a man set under authority" [7:6–8 g]. The part with authority in the outer service knows what authority is. It knows that truth's word alone acts, and that truth need not come in.

Truth is amazed, and turns to the multitude: "such faith I have not found even in Israel" [7:9 ◊]. A part in the service of the outer rule acknowledges more than the soul's inherited people do. Grey: the servant is found well [7:10 g].

### The widow's son at Nain [7:11 g; 7:12; 7:13 g; 7:14–16; 7:17 g]

At the gate of a town, a dead man is being carried out [7:12]. He is his mother's only son, and she is a widow. A state of the soul has lost both what governed it (husband) and the only thing it brought forth (son). What it brought forth is being carried out of its structure, dead.

Grey: "the Lord" has compassion and tells her not to weep [7:13 g]. Truth comes forward and touches the bier, and the bearers stand still [7:14]. "Young man, I say to you, rise." The dead sits up and speaks, and truth gives him back to his mother [7:15]. Life is returned to what the state brought forth, and it is given back to that state.

Fear seizes all, and they give glory to the source [7:16]: "A great prophet has arisen among us", and "God has visited his people". The soul's thoughts recognise truth as a prophet, and recognise the source's visit in it. Grey: the word spreads through all Judea [7:17 g].

### John's question [7:18–20; 7:21 g; 7:22–23]

The preparing state (John) hears of all this through its learning powers. It sends two of them "to the Lord": "Are you the one who is to come, or are we to wait for another?" [7:18–20]. The preparing state asks truth whether this is what it was preparing for.

Grey: in that hour truth heals many and gives sight to many blind [7:21 g]. Truth answers with what it does, not with a name [7:22]:
- the blind see again;
- the lame walk;
- lepers are cleansed;
- the deaf hear;
- the dead are raised;
- the poor are given the good news.

Every closed function of the soul is opened. "Blessed is anyone who does not take offence at me" [7:23].

### Truth on John [7:24; 7:25 g; 7:26–28]

What did the soul go out into the wilderness to see? "A reed shaken by the wind?" [7:24]: something weak, moved by every breath. Grey: a man in soft clothing, such as live in palaces [7:25 g]. "A prophet? Yes, … and more than a prophet" [7:26]. It is the one of whom it is written, "Behold, I send my messenger before your face, who will prepare your way before you" [7:27]. The preparing state is the messenger sent ahead of truth, to prepare its way in the soul.

"Among those born of women no one is greater than John; yet the least in the kingdom of God is greater than he" [7:28]. The greatest the soul can bring forth by natural birth is the preparing state. The least thing in the soul when it is ruled from within is greater.

### The woman in the Pharisee's house [7:36–38; 7:39–43 g; 7:44–48; 7:49 g; 7:50]

The self-righteous keeping invites truth to eat with it, and truth goes in and reclines [7:36]. A woman of the town, a sinner, learns of it and comes with an alabaster jar of ointment [7:37]. She is a part of the soul that has gone astray, and she brings the most precious thing she has: love applied. She stands behind truth at its feet, its outermost point. She weeps, wets its feet with her tears, wipes them with the hair of her head, kisses them and anoints them [7:38].

Grey: the self-righteous keeping thinks that a prophet would know what kind of woman this is [7:39 g]. Truth tells it the parable of two debtors, one forgiven much and one little, and asks which will love more. Simon answers: the one forgiven more [7:40–43 g].

Truth turns to the woman and sets the two receptions side by side [7:44–46]:

| The self-righteous keeping gave | The strayed part gave |
|---|---|
| no water for the feet | tears |
| no kiss | kisses that have not stopped |
| no oil for the head | ointment for the feet |

The self-righteous keeping received truth into its house and gave it nothing. The strayed part gave everything, at the outermost point. "Her sins, which were many, have been forgiven, because she loved much; but the one to whom little is forgiven loves little" [7:47]. "Your sins are forgiven" [7:48]. Grey: those at table ask who this is that forgives sins [7:49 g]. "Your faith has saved you; go in peace" [7:50].
