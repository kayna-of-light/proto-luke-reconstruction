# Chapter 11 — Luke 13:10–21, 23–28

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey · `[v ◊]` doubtful wording (README §1).

## Inner sense

### The bent woman on the sabbath [13:10–13 g; 13:14–16; 13:17 g]

Grey: truth is teaching in an assembly on the sabbath [13:10–13 g]. A woman is there who for eighteen years (?) has had "a spirit of weakness". She is bent over and cannot straighten herself at all: a bearing part of the soul, bound so that it cannot look up. Truth calls her and says, "Woman, you are freed from your weakness". It lays its hands on her, she stands up straight at once, and she gives glory to the source.

The leader of the assembly, indignant that truth has healed on the sabbath, tells the multitude [13:14]: there are six days for work; come and be healed on those, not on the sabbath. The keeper of the soul's learned teaching would confine healing to the soul's working days.

"The Lord" answers [13:15]: "Hypocrites! Does not each of you on the sabbath untie his ox or his donkey from the manger and lead it away to water it?" Then [13:16]: "And this woman, a daughter of Abraham whom Satan bound for eighteen long years, ought she not to be set free from this bond on the sabbath day?" On its day of rest the soul unties even its working beasts and leads them to water. How much more should a daughter of the origin, bound by the foreign rule, be freed then. Freeing is what rest is for. Grey: the opponents are put to shame and the multitude rejoices [13:17 g].

### Mustard seed and leaven [13:18 g; 13:19–21]

Grey: "What is the kingdom of God like?" [13:18 g].

- **The mustard seed.** A man sows it in his garden. It grows, becomes a tree, and the birds of the sky nest in its branches [13:19]. The rule from within begins as the smallest thing sown in the soul's cultivated ground. It becomes a structure in which the soul's thoughts find rest.
- **The leaven.** A woman hides it in three measures of flour "until it was all leavened" [13:20–21]. Leaven does the same work as at 12:1: it spreads hidden through the whole. There it was the pretence of the self-righteous keeping. Here it is the rule from within, working hidden through the whole substance of the soul until all is changed.

The two images are a pair: a man in the garden outside, a woman with the flour inside the house (?).

### The shut door [13:23–24 g; 13:25–27; 13:28 ◊]

Grey: someone asks whether those who are saved will be few [13:23–24 g]. Truth says to strive to enter through the narrow door; many will seek and not be able.

Once the master of the house has risen and shut the door, those outside knock: "Lord, open to us" [13:25]. He answers, "I do not know where you come from". They say: "We ate and drank in your presence, and you taught in our streets" [13:26]. "I do not know where you come from. Depart from me, all you workers of injustice!" [13:27].

There will be weeping and gnashing of teeth "when you see all the righteous in the kingdom of God, and yourselves held outside" [13:28 ◊]. These are parts of the soul that were near truth outwardly: they ate in its presence and heard it in their streets. But their origin is not truth, so when the door is shut they stand outside. The righteous parts are inside the rule from within, and the others are held out of it.
