# Chapter 7 — Luke 9:18–48, 51–62

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey · `[v ◊]` doubtful wording (README §1).

## Inner sense

### Faith names the inner form [9:18–21; 9:22 ◊]

Truth is praying alone, with its following powers near [9:18]. It asks who the multitude says it is. They answer: John the Baptist, Elijah, or one of the ancient prophets [9:19]. These are the soul's familiar categories, as with the outer rule at 9:7–8. "But who do you say that I am?" Faith answers: "The Anointed One of God" [9:20]. For the first time the soul's own faith names the inner form: truth on which the source has poured its love. The foreign parts knew this name (4:41) and were silenced. Faith knows it now, and is also ordered to tell no one [9:21].

As soon as faith has named the inner form, truth says what will be done to it [9:22 ◊]:
- the son of man must suffer many things;
- it will be rejected by the elders, the chief priests and the scribes;
- it will be killed;
- "after three days" it will be raised.

The soul's governing centre and its interpreting will reject the inner form and kill it in its outer form.

### Losing life to keep it [9:23 g; 9:24; 9:25 g; 9:26; 9:27 g]

Grey: whoever would come after truth must deny itself and take up its cross daily [9:23 g]. Whoever wants to save its life will lose it; whoever loses it for truth's sake will save it [9:24]. The soul's life held as its own is lost; given up for truth, it is kept. Grey: what profit is the whole world if the self is lost [9:25 g].

Whoever is ashamed of truth and its words, the son of man will be ashamed of when it comes "in his glory and the glory of the Father and of the holy angels" [9:26]. A part that will not own truth in the outer degree will not be owned by the inner form when it appears in full, with the source's own glory. Grey: some standing here will see the kingdom before they die [9:27 g].

### The mountain: the source names the son [9:28–35 ◊; 9:36 g]

About eight days later (?), truth takes faith and its two nearest powers and goes up the mountain to pray [9:28]. While it prays, the appearance of its face changes and its clothing becomes dazzling white [9:29]. In elevation, truth's outer form (face and clothing) shines with what is within.

Two men talk with it, Moses and Elijah, who appear in glory [9:30–31]. The law and the prophetic voice appear beside truth and speak with it. Faith and the others are heavy with sleep, but fully awake they see its glory and the two men [9:32].

As the two are leaving, faith says: "Master, it is good for us to be here; let us make three tents, one for you, one for Moses and one for Elijah", "not knowing what he was saying" [9:33]. Faith would fix the vision in three equal dwellings, setting truth on the same level as the law and the prophetic voice.

A cloud comes and overshadows them, and they are afraid as they enter it [9:34]. The soul's sight is covered. Out of the cloud a voice says: "This is my beloved son; listen to him" [9:35 ◊]. The source itself names the inner form as its beloved son: what Divine Love brings forth. Grey: when the voice has spoken, truth is found alone [9:36 g]. The law and the prophetic voice withdraw, and truth remains. The powers keep silent.

### The boy the powers could not free [9:37–39 g; 9:40; 9:41 ◊; 9:42–43a g]

Grey: coming down the next day, a great multitude meets truth [9:37–39 g]. A man's only son is seized by a spirit that convulses him. "I begged your disciples to cast it out, but they could not" [9:40]. Truth's own powers could not free this part. "O faithless generation, how long shall I be with you and bear with you? Bring your son here" [9:41 ◊]. Truth names the whole present state of the soul as without faith.

Grey [9:42–43a g]:
- the occupant throws the boy down;
- truth rebukes it, heals him and gives him back to his father;
- all are astounded at the greatness of the source.

### Handed over into human hands [9:43b–44; 9:45 g]

While all marvel, truth tells its powers: "Let these words sink into your ears: the son of man is about to be handed over into human hands" [9:44]. The inner form will be delivered to the soul's natural parts. Grey: they do not understand, it is hidden from them, and they are afraid to ask [9:45 g].

### The child [9:46–48]

The powers argue about which of them is greatest [9:46]. Truth, knowing the thoughts of their hearts, takes a child and sets it beside itself [9:47]. Whoever receives this child in truth's name receives truth, and whoever receives truth receives the one who sent it; "the least among all of you is the greatest" [9:48]. To receive the state that claims nothing is to receive truth, and through truth, the source.

### Toward the centre: the Samaritan village [9:51 g; 9:52; 9:53 g; 9:54–55; 9:56 g]

Grey: as the days draw near for truth to be taken up, it sets its face to go to Jerusalem, the centre [9:51 g]. Truth sends messengers ahead, who enter a village of the Samaritans to make ready for it [9:52]. Samaria is the region between the outer degree and the centre, foreign to the inherited worship. Grey: the village does not receive truth, "because his face was set towards Jerusalem" [9:53 g].

James and John, the powers nearest faith, ask: "Lord, do you want us to command fire to come down from heaven and consume them?" [9:54]. They would destroy what does not receive. Truth turns and rebukes them [9:55]. Grey: they go on to another village [9:56 g].

### Following [9:57–62]

One part says it will follow wherever truth goes [9:57]. "Foxes have holes, and the birds of the sky have nests, but the son of man has nowhere to lay his head" [9:58]. The inner form has no resting place in the soul's settled structures.

To another truth says, "Follow me". It answers, "Lord, first let me go and bury my father" [9:59]. "Let the dead bury their own dead; but you, go and proclaim the kingdom of God" [9:60]. What is dead in the soul's inheritance is left to what is dead.

Another will follow, "but first let me say farewell to those at my home" [9:61]. "No one who puts his hand to the plough and looks back is fit for the kingdom of God" [9:62]. A power that turns back to its natural origin while it works the ground is not fit for the rule from within.
