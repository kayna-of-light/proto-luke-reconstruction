# Chapter 16 — Luke 18:1–23, 35–43

Marks: `[v]` certain · `[v g]` grey · `[v ◊]` doubtful wording (README §1).

## Inner sense

### The widow and the judge [18:1–3; 18:4 g; 18:5; 18:6 g; 18:7; 18:8 g]

A parable about praying always and not losing heart [18:1]. In a town there is a judge who neither fears God nor respects people [18:2]. There is a widow who keeps coming to him: "Give me justice against my adversary" [18:3]. A bereft part of the soul, without what governs it, keeps coming to a judgement in the soul that has no regard for the source or for anyone.

Grey: for a while he refuses [18:4 g]. Then, because she keeps troubling him, he gives her justice [18:5]. Persistence is answered even there. Grey: "the Lord" says to hear what the unjust judge says [18:6 g]. "And will not God give justice to his chosen ones, who cry to him day and night? Will he delay long over them?" [18:7]. How much more does the source answer the parts that cry to it without ceasing. Grey: it will be quickly; "when the son of man comes, will he find faith on earth?" [18:8 g]. When the inner form comes, will it find acknowledgement in the outer degree?

### The Pharisee and the tax collector [18:9 g; 18:10–14]

Grey: a parable for those who trust in their own righteousness [18:9 g]. Two men go up into the temple to pray, a Pharisee and a tax collector [18:10]. Two parts of the soul go up to its centre of worship.

- **The self-righteous keeping** stands by itself and thanks the source for itself: "I am not like other people… or even like this tax collector. I fast twice a week; I give tithes of all that I get" [18:11–12].
- **The part in the outer rule's service** stands far off and will not lift its eyes. It beats its breast, its will, and asks: "God, be merciful to me, a sinner!" [18:13].

"This man went down to his house justified rather than the other; for everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, but the one who humbles himself will be exalted" [18:14]. The part that asks mercy goes back to its own state set right.

### The children [18:15 g; 18:16; 18:17 g]

Grey: infants are brought to truth, and the following powers rebuke those who bring them [18:15 g]. Truth: "Let the children come to me, and do not hinder them; for to such belongs the kingdom of God" [18:16]. The rule from within belongs to the states that receive without claiming. Grey: whoever does not receive it like a child will not enter [18:17 g].

### The rich ruler [18:18; 18:19 ◊; 18:20 ◊; 18:21; 18:22 ◊; 18:23]

A ruling part of the soul asks: "Good teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?" [18:18]. Truth: "Why do you call me good? No one is good except one, God the Father" [18:19 ◊]. Truth in its outer form does not take "good" for itself. Good is the source alone.

"You know the commandments": do not kill, do not commit adultery, do not steal, do not bear false witness, honour father and mother [18:20 ◊]. "All these I have kept from my youth" [18:21]. The ruling part has kept the received order.

"One thing you lack: sell all that you have and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me" [18:22 ◊]. It lacks one thing: to let go of everything it holds as its own, and to follow. Hearing this, it becomes very sad, "for he was very rich" [18:23]. It cannot let go, because it holds much.

### The blind man at Jericho [18:35–43]

As truth draws near to Jericho, the last town on the way to the centre (?), a blind man sits by the roadside begging [18:35]. He is a part of the soul whose understanding is closed, sitting at the edge of the way. Hearing a crowd pass, he asks what it is [18:36]. They tell him: "Jesus of Nazareth is passing by" [18:37]. The multitude names truth by its outer origin.

He cries out: "Jesus, son of David, have mercy on me!" [18:38]. The blind part calls truth by a name from the soul's inheritance, the son of its past kingship. Those in front rebuke him and tell him to be silent, and he cries out all the more [18:39]. This time it is the parts in front that would silence the name, not truth. Truth stops and has him brought [18:40]. "What do you want me to do for you?" "Lord, let me see again" [18:41]. "See again; your faith has saved you" [18:42]. At once he sees, follows truth, and gives glory to the source, and all the people praise the source [18:43].
